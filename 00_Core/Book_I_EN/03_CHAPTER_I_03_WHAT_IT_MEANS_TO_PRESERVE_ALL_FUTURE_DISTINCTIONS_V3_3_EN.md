# Chapter I.3. What It Means to Preserve All Future Distinctions

In the first chapter our buttons were silent: we asked questions of a motionless state and examined how their fibers intersect. In the second chapter we took a single action step or switched along a binary relation and saw how a question is pulled back against the transition. Let us now consider protocol continuations of arbitrary finite length. For a state description to be sufficient, a stronger condition is required: **it is no longer enough for two states to agree in today's window—they must answer identically under every admissible continuation of the protocol**.

---

## §I.3.1. A Tangible Scene: Four `(current, trace)` Cards, a Delay Line, and a Six-Point Carrier

Let us place on the laboratory bench a visual model of four cards
$$X_{\text{trace}} = \{(x, s) \mid x \in \{0,1\},\; s \in \{0,1\}\},$$
where the left digit $x$ is visible through a single cutout window in the cover ($q(x,s) = x$), while the right digit $s$ ("the trace of the previous tick") is hidden behind an opaque shutter. Prior to any button press, the window divides the four cards into just two classes:
$$C_0 = \{(0,0), (0,1)\} \quad (q = 0), \qquad\qquad C_1 = \{(1,0), (1,1)\} \quad (q = 1).$$
Three control buttons are soldered on the outside:
- button `swap`: swaps the visible and hidden halves, $T_{\mathrm{swap}}(x, s) = (s, x)$;
- button `write_0`: shifts the current digit into the trace and writes zero into the window, $T_{\mathrm{w0}}(x, s) = (0, x)$;
- button `write_1`: shifts the current digit into the trace and writes one into the window, $T_{\mathrm{w1}}(x, s) = (1, x)$.

Suppose we naively compress the description of the device to the single visible bit $x$: since the window currently shows `0`, what difference does it make whether `(0,0)` or `(0,1)` lies beneath the shutter? Press `swap`: card `(0,0)` goes to `(0,0)` and still shows `0`, whereas card `(0,1)` goes to `(1,0)` and lights up `1`. Likewise, inside class $C_1$, card `(1,0)` after `swap` shows `0` while `(1,1)` shows `1`. The trace $s$, invisible at the present instant, turns out to be a **future window reading** waiting one button press away. At the same time, repeated presses of `write_0`, `write_1`, and `swap` require no fifth or eighth memory cell: four states $(x, s)$ suffice forever, to any protocol depth (`2 → 4 → 4`).

Can it happen, however, that **after one step** two states still give the same answer and diverge only at the **second** step? Let us place alongside a four-step delay line $X_{\text{shift}} = \{s_0, s_1, s_2, s_3\}$ with window $q(s_0)=q(s_1)=q(s_2)=0,\, q(s_3)=1$ and a single shift button $T(s_0)=s_1,\, T(s_1)=s_2,\, T(s_2)=s_3,\, T(s_3)=s_3$:
- before any press ($H=0$): `2` classes $\{s_0, s_1, s_2\}$ and $\{s_3\}$;
- after one press ($H=1$, pair $(q, q\circ T)$): state $s_2$ has already split off ($q(T(s_2))=q(s_3)=1$), but $s_0$ and $s_1$ **both** still show `0` ($q(T(s_0))=q(s_1)=0$ and $q(T(s_1))=q(s_2)=0$), yielding **`3` classes** $\{s_0, s_1\}, \{s_2\}, \{s_3\}$;
- and only on the second press ($H=2$, triple $(q, q\circ T, q\circ T^2)$) does the chain unfold completely: $q(T^2(s_0))=q(s_2)=0 \neq 1=q(s_3)=q(T^2(s_1))$, after which step $H=3$ stabilizes the partition at **`4` classes** (`2 → 3 → 4 → 4`).

It is even more instructive to observe on **one and the same six-point combinatorial carrier** $V_6 = \{0, \infty, 1, -1, i, -i\}$ with three designated antipodal pairs $\{0,\infty\}$, $\{1,-1\}$, $\{i,-i\}$ (within Book I all transitions $\sigma, \rho, \tau, \mu_i \in \mathfrak{S}_6$ and the reading $q$ are given by finite permutation tables on these six labels, while fractional-linear formulas over $\mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{Q}(i))$ serve solely as a compact mnemonic without invoking the continuous field $\mathbb{C}$, whose algebraic and analytic construction is deferred to Chapter `I.5` and Book II, `Chapter II.2`) how:
1. three axial reductions to the poles $\{0, \infty\}$ provide an exact bijective encoding $V_6 \xrightarrow{\sim} \{z, x, y\} \times \{0, \infty\}$ (`3 × 2 = 6`) and separate three fixed-in-advance readings from at most two adaptively chosen readings;
2. under the quadratic window $q(w) = w^2 \in \{0, \infty, 1, -1\}$, expanding the action alphabet $\mathcal{A}_0 \subset \mathcal{A}_1 \subset \mathcal{A}_2$ successively unfolds the minimal memory along the ladder `4 → 5 → 6`.

**Reader's caption to `Fig. I.3.1`:** In panel (a), the six points $V_6 = \{0, \infty, 1, -1, i, -i\} \subset \mathbb{P}^1(\mathbb{Q}(i))$ are partitioned into three antipodal pairs $\{0,\infty\}$, $\{1,-1\}$, $\{i,-i\}$ by the conjugate involution $a(w) = -1/\overline{w}$ (the ordinary holomorphic map $-1/w$ fixes $\pm i$ and therefore does not define all three antipodal pairs). Joining every pair of non-antipodal points $v \neq a(u)$ yields the `12` undirected edges of the octahedron $O_6 \cong K_{2,2,2}$ with $|\mathrm{Aut}(O_6)| = 48$. In panel (b), the fractional-linear bijections $\sigma(w)=-w$, $\rho(w)=1/w$, $\tau(w)=\frac{w-1}{w+1}$, and $\mu_i(w)=i w$ generate in $\mathrm{PGL}_2(\mathbb{Q}(i))$ subgroups of orders $|\langle\sigma,\rho\rangle| = 4$ ($\mathbb{Z}_2^2$), $|\langle\sigma,\rho,\tau\rangle| = 8$ ($D_4$), and $|\langle\sigma,\rho,\tau,\mu_i\rangle| = 24$ ($\mathfrak{S}_4$). The quadratic reading $q(w)=w^2$ sees `4` classes (`6 → 4`), forgetting the sign inside $\{1,-1\}$ and $\{i,-i\}$. Adding the Cayley transform $\tau$ separates $q(\tau(1))=0 \neq \infty = q(\tau(-1))$, but leaves each of the points $\pm i$ fixed ($\tau(i)=i$, $\tau(-i)=-i$), so memory over $\mathcal{A}_1$ stops at `5` classes; only adjoining the quarter-turn $\mu_i(w)=i w$ maps $\pm i$ to $\mp 1$, after which $\tau \circ \mu_i$ separates all six points (`4 → 5 → 6`). Simultaneously, the three charts $T_z(w)=w$, $T_x(w)=\frac{w-1}{w+1}$, $T_y(w)=\frac{w-i}{w+i}$ with axis selection $j(w) \in \{z, x, y\}$ according to $w^2 \in \{0,\infty\} / \{1\} / \{-1\}$ define the reversible polar encoding $\Phi_{3\times 2} : V_6 \xrightarrow{\sim} \{z,x,y\} \times \{0,\infty\}$, $w = T_j^{-1}(p)$. For the three axial questions $Q_z = q$, $Q_x = q \circ T_x$, $Q_y = q \circ T_y$, no two fixed-in-advance readings separate $V_6$ (every pair glues one of the three antipodal pairs and yields only `5` classes), whereas an adaptive tree expends **at most `2` readings**: having read $Q_z(w)=w^2$, at $0,\infty$ we stop after `1` reading, at `1` we read $Q_x(w)$, and at `-1` we read $Q_y(w)$.

## §I.3.2. Four Pitfalls of Naive Memory Compression

Any attempt to eyeball the "memory of a system" runs into four treacherous pitfalls:
1. **Forgetting the empty protocol and admissibility domains.** If one checks agreement of future answers only after *non-empty* button presses ($|w| \ge 1$), two states with different current window readings but the same constant reset $T_a(x) \equiv x_*$ will be falsely glued together; and if one forgets the locked-button marker $\bot_{\mathcal{A}}$, states with different sets of permitted actions will be declared indistinguishable.
2. **The illusion of the one-step pullback.** Two completely different actions can share **the exact same one-step pullback** of a question $T_1^* q = T_2^* q$. On the cell $S_4$ with window $\beta(x,c) = x \oplus c$, compare the reversible mixing step $T_{\mathrm{mix}}(x,c) = (x \oplus c, c)$ and the irreversible reference reset $T_{\mathrm{reset\_c}}(x,c) = (x, 0)$: after the first press **both** yield the exact same answer $\beta(T_{\mathrm{mix}}(x,c)) = (x\oplus c)\oplus c = x$ and $\beta(T_{\mathrm{reset\_c}}(x,c)) = x \oplus 0 = x$ ($T_{\mathrm{mix}}^*\beta = T_{\mathrm{reset\_c}}^*\beta = q_x$), yet on the second press $\beta(T_{\mathrm{mix}}^2(x,c)) = x \oplus c$ whereas $\beta(T_{\mathrm{reset\_c}}^2(x,c)) = x$.
3. **The illusion "the finer the partition, the safer the step descent."** It might seem that if an action $K$ descends validly to a coarse 2-class partition, then it will all the more descend to a finer 3-class partition. This inference is false. On $S_4$, the reference-flip involution $K(x,c) = (x, 1\oplus c)$ swaps the diagonal fibers $\mathcal{P}_2 = \{O_0, O_1\}$ ($K(O_0)=O_1,\, K(O_1)=O_0$) and induces a well-defined step on `2` classes; but if we refine the partition to 3 classes $\mathcal{P}_3 = \{\{(0,0),(1,1)\},\, \{(0,1)\},\, \{(1,0)\}\}$, the states $(0,0)$ and $(1,1)$, which are equivalent in $\mathcal{P}_3$, map under $K$ to **different** classes $K(0,0)=(0,1) \not\sim_{\mathcal{P}_3} (1,0)=K(1,1)$, and the deterministic step on $\mathcal{P}_3$ collapses.
4. **The stochastic pitfall of separate marginals.** In a probabilistic system it is tempting to require of compressed memory only that it preserve separately the distribution of the current answer $\Pr(o \mid x)$ and separately the distribution of the next memory class $\Pr(m' \mid x)$. Below we will see that even when both marginals match identically, the two-step trajectory distributions can diverge by the maximum possible total variation distance $\mathrm{TV} = 1$.

## §I.3.3. Protocol Indistinguishability, the Moore–Nerode Theorem for Partial Contracts, and the Dual Tower of Subalgebras

Let us give the complete definition of state indistinguishability across all future continuations within the working core of an operational contract $\Gamma = (X, \mathcal{Q}, \Omega)$ from `Chapter I.1`. Historically, the idea of defining the states of a "black box" as equivalence classes under future input-output experiments goes back to the classic paper of **E. F. Moore** (*"Gedanken-experiments on Sequential Machines," Automata Studies, 1956*) and the **J. Myhill – A. Nerode** theorem (*J. Myhill, 1957; A. Nerode, 1958*). In the present chapter we place the Moore–Nerode construction inside the operational contract of TNR and supplement it with four structural elements: (i) accounting for partial actions and partial questions with separated failure markers $\bot_{\mathcal{A}} \neq \bot_q \neq 0$; (ii) comparing word traces and branching adaptive protocol trees (`Lemma I.3.1`); (iii) establishing the exact step-by-step duality between forward refinement of relations $\sim_k$ and the pullback tower of Boolean subalgebras of transferred questions $B_k$ from `Chapter I.2` (`Theorem I.3.1(4)`); and (iv) passing to the stochastic joint conditional law on the pair `(answer, next class)` (`Theorem I.3.2` and `Counterexample I.3.2`) and to the linear Hankel projection in `Chapter I.8`.

In the main deterministic part of the chapter, the protocol class $\Pi$ is **the collection of all finite action words $\mathcal{A}^*$ and all finite adaptive protocol trees over the declared alphabets $\mathcal{A}$ and $\mathcal{Q}$**.

> **Definition I.3.1 (Full protocol indistinguishability with the empty word and domain saturation).**  
> Let a contract $\Gamma$ be given with deterministic partial actions $T_a : \mathrm{Dom}(T_a) \to X$ ($a \in \mathcal{A}$) and partial questions $q : \mathrm{Dom}(q) \to V_q$ ($q \in \mathcal{Q}$).
> 1. For each finite action word $w \in \mathcal{A}^*$, inductively define the admissibility domain $\mathrm{Dom}(T_w) \subseteq X$ and the composite transition $T_w : \mathrm{Dom}(T_w) \to X$:
>    - for the **empty word** $w = \varepsilon$, set $\mathrm{Dom}(T_\varepsilon) = X$ and $T_\varepsilon = \mathrm{id}_X$;
>    - for $w = u a$ ($u \in \mathcal{A}^*,\, a \in \mathcal{A}$), set
>      $$\mathrm{Dom}(T_{u a}) = \{x \in \mathrm{Dom}(T_u) \mid T_u(x) \in \mathrm{Dom}(T_a)\}, \qquad T_{u a}(x) = T_a\bigl(T_u(x)\bigr).$$
> 2. For each pair $(w, q) \in \mathcal{A}^* \times \mathcal{Q}$, define the extended total test $\widehat{\mathrm{Obs}}_{w,q} : X \to V_q \sqcup \{\bot_q, \bot_{\mathcal{A}}\}$ (where the service markers $\bot_q \neq \bot_{\mathcal{A}}$ do not belong to $V_q$ and are strictly distinct from any $0 \in V_q$):
>    $$\widehat{\mathrm{Obs}}_{w,q}(x) = \begin{cases} \bot_{\mathcal{A}}, & \text{if } x \notin \mathrm{Dom}(T_w), \\ \bot_q, & \text{if } x \in \mathrm{Dom}(T_w)\text{ but } T_w(x) \notin \mathrm{Dom}(q), \\ q(T_w(x)), & \text{if } x \in \mathrm{Dom}(T_w) \text{ and } T_w(x) \in \mathrm{Dom}(q). \end{cases}$$
> 3. Two states $x, y \in X$ are called **protocol-indistinguishable in the contract $\Gamma$** (written $x \sim_\Gamma y$) if:
>    - for every word $w \in \mathcal{A}^*$, admissibility of the trajectory itself is equivalent: $x \in \mathrm{Dom}(T_w) \iff y \in \mathrm{Dom}(T_w)$;
>    - for every word $w \in \mathcal{A}^*$ (including $w = \varepsilon$) and every question $q \in \mathcal{Q}$, the outcomes of the extended test agree: $\widehat{\mathrm{Obs}}_{w,q}(x) = \widehat{\mathrm{Obs}}_{w,q}(y)$.

Why the first clause on admissibility domains $\mathrm{Dom}(T_w)$ is indispensable is shown by the **minimal gate witness**: suppose on $S_4$ the sole question $q \equiv 0$ is everywhere constant, but there is a button $g$ with admissibility domain $\mathrm{Dom}(T_g) = O_0 = \{(0,0),(1,1)\}$ and $T_g(x,c)=(x,c)$. Even though $q$ never changes its answer `0`, testing $x \in \mathrm{Dom}(T_g)$ immediately separates $O_0$ (the button depresses) from $O_1$ (returns $\bot_{\mathcal{A}}$).

> **Lemma I.3.1 (Coincidence of word-trace and adaptive-tree indistinguishability for deterministic transitions).**  
> *Classical foundation.* Coincidence of word and adaptive indistinguishability for deterministic transitions—classical theorem of automaton experiment theory (Moore, 1956). *Contribution of this book:* Extension of the equivalence between unconditional and adaptive indistinguishability to scenes with partial actions $\mathrm{Dom}(T_a)$ and partial readings, plus refinement `4 → 5 → 6` on the octahedral carrier $V_6$.
> For any deterministic contract $\Gamma$, the relation $x \sim_\Gamma y$ defined by all finite words $(w, q) \in \mathcal{A}^* \times \mathcal{Q}$ coincides with indistinguishability under all finite adaptive protocol trees (where each internal node reads the current answer vector $(\widehat{\mathrm{Obs}}_{\varepsilon, q})_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}$ and from it selects either $\mathrm{STOP}$ or a next action $a \in \mathcal{A}$, advancing along an admissible step or recording the rejection marker $\bot_{\mathcal{A}}$).

*Proof.* Every fixed word $(w, q)$ is a degenerate adaptive tree without branching, so adaptive indistinguishability implies $x \sim_\Gamma y$. Conversely, suppose $x \sim_\Gamma y$ and an adaptive strategy $\pi$ of finite depth $d$ is given. At the root of the tree ($m=0$, $w_0 = \varepsilon$), for all $q \in \mathcal{Q}$ we have $\widehat{\mathrm{Obs}}_{\varepsilon, q}(x) = \widehat{\mathrm{Obs}}_{\varepsilon, q}(y)$, so the strategy $\pi$ receives the same answer record at the root and selects the same action $a_1$ for $x$ and $y$ (or simultaneously outputs $\mathrm{STOP}$). By induction, if by step $m$ the strategy has selected the same word $w_m = a_1 \cdots a_m$ on both states, then by $x \sim_\Gamma y$ both the admissibility flags $x \in \mathrm{Dom}(T_{w_m}) \iff y \in \mathrm{Dom}(T_{w_m})$ and all answers $\widehat{\mathrm{Obs}}_{w_m, q}(x) = \widehat{\mathrm{Obs}}_{w_m, q}(y)$ coincide, whence the next choice $a_{m+1}$ or $\mathrm{STOP}$ again coincides on $x$ and $y$. $\square$

*(Coincidence of the equivalence relation does not mean equal depth of a single experiment: as we saw in `Fig. I.3.1`, an adaptive tree separates $V_6$ in `≤ 2` readings versus `3` readings for a static set; moreover, in open and non-deterministic channels adaptive trees are strictly stronger than word traces.)*

Let us specify with full quantifier precision the boundary condition for the rule preserving the zero reference under scalar post-processing $\Phi_0 : V \to W$.

> **Lemma I.3.2 (Exact criterion for zero preservation on an active zero fiber).**  
> *Classical foundation.* Invariant subsets and absorbing fibers of dynamical systems and finite automata. *Contribution of this book:* Criterion for invariance of the active zero fiber under an admissible transition library and separation of dynamically silent microstates from the observable future.
> Let $Y \subseteq X$ be the set of reachable states, $U : \mathrm{Dom}(U) \to X$ the partial continuation channel under consideration with input domain $\mathrm{Dom}(U) \subseteq X$ (on the intersection $S_{\mathrm{act}} := Y \cap \mathrm{Dom}(U)$ the channel admits launching the next step of the contract), $q : X \to V$ a question with a distinguished scale zero $0_V \in V$, and $Z_{\mathrm{act}} = S_{\mathrm{act}} \cap q^{-1}(0_V) = Y \cap \mathrm{Dom}(U) \cap q^{-1}(0_V)$ the **active zero fiber** at the entrance to channel $U$. For any scale re-encoding $\Phi_0 : V \to W$ with scale zero $0_W \in W$, consider the zero-preservation condition on active states:
> $$(*)\qquad \forall x \in Y \cap \mathrm{Dom}(U), \qquad q(x) = 0_V \implies \Phi_0\bigl(q(x)\bigr) = 0_W.$$
> Then:
> 1. If the active zero fiber is **non-empty** ($Z_{\mathrm{act}} \neq \varnothing$), then for each fixed map $\Phi_0 : V \to W$ the state-level condition $(*)$ is **equivalent** to the scale equality $\Phi_0(0_V) = 0_W$.
> 2. If the active zero fiber is **empty** ($Z_{\mathrm{act}} = \varnothing$), then condition $(*)$ holds vacuously for **all** maps $\Phi_0 : V \to W$ and imposes no restriction on $\Phi_0(0_V)$.
> 3. Consequently, when $|W| \ge 2$, non-emptiness of the active zero fiber $Z_{\mathrm{act}} \neq \varnothing$ is equivalent to the universally quantified implication: *for all* post-processings $\Phi_0 : V \to W$, validity of $(*)$ implies $\Phi_0(0_V) = 0_W$.

*Proof.* When $Z_{\mathrm{act}} \neq \varnothing$, choose $x_0 \in Z_{\mathrm{act}}$; then $(*)$ implies $\Phi_0(0_V) = \Phi_0(q(x_0)) = 0_W$, while the converse implication is immediate. When $Z_{\mathrm{act}} = \varnothing$, no $x \in Y \cap \mathrm{Dom}(U)$ satisfies $q(x) = 0_V$, so $(*)$ holds for every $\Phi_0$. If $|W| \ge 2$ and $Z_{\mathrm{act}} = \varnothing$, choosing a constant map $\Phi_0(v) \equiv w_1 \neq 0_W$ satisfies $(*)$ while violating $\Phi_0(0_V) = 0_W$, proving item (3). $\square$

> **Counterexample I.3.0 (Vacuous satisfaction of the state-level condition with $\Phi_0(0_V) \neq 0_W$ and accidental agreement on an empty fiber).**  
> On a one-element carrier $X = Y = \{x_0\}$ with $q(x_0) = 1 \in \{0, 1\}$ and $U = \mathrm{id}_X$, the active zero fiber is empty: $Z_{\mathrm{act}} = \varnothing$. Then the constant post-processing $\Phi_0(0) = \Phi_0(1) = 1$ satisfies law $(*)$ on states while $\Phi_0(0) = 1 \neq 0$; meanwhile, for the fixed map $\widetilde{\Phi}_0(v) = v$, the equality $\widetilde{\Phi}_0(0) = 0$ happens to hold even though $Z_{\mathrm{act}} = \varnothing$ (which is why the universal quantifier $\forall \Phi_0$ is essential in item 3 of Lemma `I.3.2`).

To state the central theorem on minimal deterministic memory for the canonical partial Moore signature without overloading the formulation, let us separate in advance two levels of sufficiency for a surjective compression map $r : X \twoheadrightarrow M$:
- call a surjection $r : X \twoheadrightarrow M$ **sufficient for reading all future answers** if for every word $w \in \mathcal{A}^*$ and every question $q \in \mathcal{Q}$ the multi-step reading map $\widehat{\mathrm{Obs}}_{w,q} : X \to V_q \sqcup \{\bot_q, \bot_{\mathcal{A}}\}$ and the admissibility indicator $\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{Dom}(T_w)} : X \to \{0,1\}$ factor through $r$ in the category of sets $\mathbf{Set}$;
- call a surjection $r : X \twoheadrightarrow M$ an **autonomous sufficient memory (partial quotient Moore automaton)** for the contract $\Gamma$ if for every $q \in \mathcal{Q}$ and every $a \in \mathcal{A}$ there exist domains $\mathrm{Dom}(\widehat{q}) \subseteq M$, $\mathrm{Dom}(\widehat{T}_a) \subseteq M$ and maps $\widehat{q} : \mathrm{Dom}(\widehat{q}) \to V_q$, $\widehat{T}_a : \mathrm{Dom}(\widehat{T}_a) \to M$ such that
  $$\mathrm{Dom}(q) = r^{-1}\bigl(\mathrm{Dom}(\widehat{q})\bigr),\quad \widehat{q} \circ r\big|_{\mathrm{Dom}(q)} = q, \qquad \mathrm{Dom}(T_a) = r^{-1}\bigl(\mathrm{Dom}(\widehat{T}_a)\bigr),\quad \widehat{T}_a \circ r\big|_{\mathrm{Dom}(T_a)} = r \circ T_a.$$

> **Theorem I.3.1 (Universality of the minimal memory quotient $X / {\sim_\Gamma}$, separation of reading descent from autonomous transition descent, and the dual Boolean closure of questions).**  
> *Classical foundation.* The minimal quotient $X / {\sim_\Gamma}$ is the classical Myhill (1957) – Nerode (1958) congruence and Moore automaton minimization (1956); its universality is standard. *Contribution of this book:* Separating descent of static reading from autonomous descent of partial transitions, exact stabilization bound $k_* \le \max(|X|-1, 0)$, and dual description via the counter-directed tower of Boolean subalgebras $B_k$.
> Let $\Gamma$ be a deterministic operational contract on a carrier $X$ with protocol class $\Pi$ consisting of all finite words $\mathcal{A}^*$ (and all finite adaptive trees by Lemma `I.3.1`). Then the following four statements hold:
> 1. **Criterion and universality of the minimal quotient:** the relation $\sim_\Gamma$ is an equivalence relation on $X$, and the canonical projection $\pi_\Gamma : X \twoheadrightarrow X / {\sim_\Gamma}$ forms an autonomous sufficient memory. For an arbitrary surjection $r : X \twoheadrightarrow M$:
>    - **(1a) at the level of readings in $\mathbf{Set}$:** $r$ is sufficient for reading all future answers **if and only if** $\mathrm{Eq}(r) \subseteq {\sim_\Gamma}$, i.e., the canonical projection $\pi_\Gamma$ factors through $r$ via a **unique** set surjection $\bar{r} : M \twoheadrightarrow X / {\sim_\Gamma}$ satisfying $\pi_\Gamma = \bar{r} \circ r$ (in particular, $|M| \ge |X / {\sim_\Gamma}|$);
>    - **(1b) at the level of autonomous dynamics:** $r$ is an autonomous sufficient memory for $\Gamma$ **if and only if** $\mathrm{Eq}(r) \subseteq {\sim_\Gamma}$ **and in addition** the equivalence $\mathrm{Eq}(r)$ is closed under all partial transitions $T_a$ ($r(x)=r(y) \implies [x\in\mathrm{Dom}(T_a)\iff y\in\mathrm{Dom}(T_a)]$ and, when $x\in\mathrm{Dom}(T_a)$, $r(T_a(x)) = r(T_a(y))$); in this and only this case there exists on $M$ a unique partial Moore automaton structure $(\widehat{q}, \widehat{T})$ making both $r$ and $\bar{r} : (M, \widehat{q}, \widehat{T}) \twoheadrightarrow (X/{\sim_\Gamma}, \bar{q}, \bar{T})$ surjective morphisms of partial Moore automata with $\pi_\Gamma = \bar{r} \circ r$.
> 2. **Equivalence with update using the current window:** if autonomous memory is permitted to update using the current window answer vector $o(x) = (\widehat{\mathrm{Obs}}_{\varepsilon, q}(x))_{q\in\mathcal{Q}}$ by the rule $r(T_a(x)) = U_a\bigl(r(x), o(x)\bigr)$, the minimal quotient does not shrink, because the condition $r(x)=r(y) \implies o(x)=o(y)$ at $w=\varepsilon$ already makes $o(x)$ a single-valued function of the class $r(x)$.
> 3. **Finite stabilization of forward memory refinement (Nerode algorithm):** define the descending chain of equivalences $\sim_0 \supseteq \sim_1 \supseteq \sim_2 \supseteq \cdots$ on $X$:
>    $$x \sim_0 y \iff \Bigl(\forall q \in \mathcal{Q},\; \widehat{\mathrm{Obs}}_{\varepsilon,q}(x) = \widehat{\mathrm{Obs}}_{\varepsilon,q}(y)\Bigr) \land \Bigl(\forall a \in \mathcal{A},\; x \in \mathrm{Dom}(T_a) \iff y \in \mathrm{Dom}(T_a)\Bigr),$$
>    $$x \sim_{k+1} y \iff x \sim_k y \;\land\; \forall a \in \mathcal{A}\; \bigl(x \in \mathrm{Dom}(T_a) \implies T_a(x) \sim_k T_a(y)\bigr).$$
>    Then at every $k \ge 0$, the relation $x \sim_k y$ coincides with equality of all readings $\widehat{\mathrm{Obs}}_{w,q}$ after words of length $|w| \le k$ **and** equivalence of trajectory admissibility $x \in \mathrm{Dom}(T_w) \iff y \in \mathrm{Dom}(T_w)$ for all words of length $|w| \le k + 1$. If the carrier $X$ is finite of cardinality $|X| = n \ge 0$ and $|X/{\sim_0}| = m_0$, then there exists a step $t_* \le \max(n - m_0, 0) \le \max(n - 1, 0)$ at which $\sim_{t_*} = \sim_{t_* + 1}$, and from that step onward all subsequent relations coincide with full protocol indistinguishability: $\sim_{t_*} = \sim_\Gamma$.
> 4. **Exact step-by-step duality with the pullback Boolean closure of questions:** on a finite carrier $X$, let $B_0 \subseteq \mathcal{P}(X)$ be the finite Boolean subalgebra of subsets generated by all fibers $q^{-1}(v)$ ($q\in\mathcal{Q}, v\in V_q$) and admissibility domains $\mathrm{Dom}(q), \mathrm{Dom}(T_a)$ ($q\in\mathcal{Q}, a\in\mathcal{A}$), and for each $k \ge 0$ let the subalgebra $B_{k+1} \subseteq \mathcal{P}(X)$ be generated by the union of $B_k$ with all preimages $W_a(U) = \{x \in \mathrm{Dom}(T_a) \mid T_a(x) \in U\}$ ($a \in \mathcal{A}, U \in B_k$). Then the ascending chain of subalgebras $B_0 \subseteq B_1 \subseteq \cdots$ stabilizes at the exact same step $t_*$, and **for every $k \ge 0$ the atoms of the Boolean algebra $B_k$ coincide with the equivalence classes $X / {\sim_k}$** (in particular, the atoms of the limit algebra of future questions $B_* = B_{t_*}$ are precisely the minimal memory classes $X / {\sim_\Gamma}$).

*Proof.*
1. Reflexivity, symmetry, and transitivity of $\sim_\Gamma$ are inherited from the equalities $\widehat{\mathrm{Obs}}_{w,q}(x) = \widehat{\mathrm{Obs}}_{w,q}(y)$ and equivalences $x \in \mathrm{Dom}(T_w) \iff y \in \mathrm{Dom}(T_w)$. For $\pi_\Gamma : X \twoheadrightarrow X/{\sim_\Gamma}$, when $x \sim_\Gamma y$, substituting $w=\varepsilon$ gives agreement of domains and answers for all $q$, substituting $w=a$ gives $x\in\mathrm{Dom}(T_a)\iff y\in\mathrm{Dom}(T_a)$, and when $x,y\in\mathrm{Dom}(T_a)$ substituting $w=au$ gives $T_a(x) \sim_\Gamma T_a(y)$, so $\bar{q}([x])=q(x)$ and $\bar{T}_a([x])=[T_a(x)]$ are well defined. In part (1a), $\sim_\Gamma$ is the intersection of the kernels of all $\widehat{\mathrm{Obs}}_{w,q}$ and $\mathbf{1}_{\mathrm{Dom}(T_w)}$, so their joint factorization through a surjection $r$ in $\mathbf{Set}$ is equivalent to $\mathrm{Eq}(r) \subseteq {\sim_\Gamma}$, i.e., the unique factorization $\pi_\Gamma = \bar{r}\circ r$. In part (1b), if $r$ is an autonomous sufficient memory, then by induction on $|w|$ the equality $r(x)=r(y)$ implies $r(T_w(x))=\widehat{T}_w(r(x))=r(T_w(y))$ and agreement of all answers ($\mathrm{Eq}(r)\subseteq {\sim_\Gamma}$), while $\widehat{T}_a \circ r = r \circ T_a$ on $\mathrm{Dom}(T_a) = r^{-1}(\mathrm{Dom}(\widehat{T}_a))$ directly yields $T_a$-invariance of $\mathrm{Eq}(r)$; conversely, under $\mathrm{Eq}(r)\subseteq {\sim_\Gamma}$ and $T_a$-invariance of $\mathrm{Eq}(r)$, the formulas $\widehat{q}(r(x))=q(x)$ and $\widehat{T}_a(r(x))=r(T_a(x))$ uniquely define a partial automaton on $M$ for which the unique surjection $\bar{r}(r(x))=[x]_{\sim_\Gamma}$ commutes with $\widehat{q}$ and $\widehat{T}_a$.
2. If memory is sufficient at $w=\varepsilon$, then $r(x)=r(y) \implies o(x)=\widehat{o}(r(x))=o(y)$, so $\widetilde{T}_a(m) = U_a(m, \widehat{o}(m))$ already defines a closed law on $M$.
3. We verify the invariant at step $k$ by induction on $k \ge 0$. At $k=0$, words of length $|w| \le 0$ reduce to $w = \varepsilon$, and words of length $|w| \le 1$ reduce to $w = \varepsilon$ ($\mathrm{Dom}(T_\varepsilon)=X$ trivially) and single-letter actions $w = a \in \mathcal{A}$, matching the definition of $\sim_0$. Suppose the invariant holds for $k$. By definition, $x \sim_{k+1} y$ means $x \sim_k y$ (which already includes agreement of admissibility $x \in \mathrm{Dom}(T_a) \iff y \in \mathrm{Dom}(T_a)$ for every first step $a \in \mathcal{A}$ and all readings on $|w|\le k$) plus the condition $T_a(x) \sim_k T_a(y)$ for every admissible first step $a$. By the induction hypothesis, $T_a(x) \sim_k T_a(y)$ is equivalent to agreement of readings $\widehat{\mathrm{Obs}}_{u,q}(T_a(x)) = \widehat{\mathrm{Obs}}_{a u, q}(x)$ for $|u| \le k$ (i.e., on words $w = a u$ of length $1 \le |w| \le k+1$) and equivalence of $T_a(x) \in \mathrm{Dom}(T_u) \iff x \in \mathrm{Dom}(T_{a u})$ for $|u| \le k+1$ (i.e., on words $w = a u$ of length $1 \le |w| \le k+2$). Hence the invariant holds for $k+1$, and $\bigcap_{k \ge 0} {\sim_k}$ coincides with full indistinguishability $\sim_\Gamma$. Once ${\sim_t} = {\sim_{t+1}}$, the chain stabilizes forever, and prior to stabilization the number of classes $|X/{\sim_k}|$ strictly increases by at least `1` and cannot exceed $n = |X|$, whence $t_* \le n - m_0 \le n - 1$.
4. On a finite set $X$, every Boolean subalgebra $B \subseteq \mathcal{P}(X)$ is uniquely determined by the relation $x \equiv_B y \iff (\forall U \in B,\; x \in U \iff y \in U)$, whose equivalence classes are the atoms of $B$. At $k=0$, the generators $q^{-1}(v)$, $\mathrm{Dom}(q)$, and $\mathrm{Dom}(T_a)$ of $B_0$ define precisely $\sim_0$. If by induction $\equiv_{B_k}$ coincides with $\sim_k$, then elements $x, y \in X$ are separated by the generators of $B_{k+1}$ if and only if either $x \not\sim_k y$ or for some $a \in \mathcal{A}$ and atom $U \in B_k$ we have $x \in W_a(U) \not\iff y \in W_a(U)$; given the already established equivalence $x \in \mathrm{Dom}(T_a) \iff y \in \mathrm{Dom}(T_a)$ from $B_0 \subseteq B_k$, the latter is equivalent to $T_a(x)$ and $T_a(y)$ falling into different atoms of $B_k$, i.e., $T_a(x) \not\sim_k T_a(y)$. Thus $\equiv_{B_{k+1}}$ coincides with $\sim_{k+1}$ at every step $k \ge 0$. $\square$

> **Remark I.3.1 (Reciprocal volume law between observer memory and carrier shadow).**  
> The dual chains $\sim_0 \supseteq \sim_1 \supseteq \cdots \supseteq \sim_{t_*}$ and $B_0 \subseteq B_1 \subseteq \cdots \subseteq B_{t_*}$ link the size of the observer's internal memory $|X / {\sim_k}| = |\mathrm{Atoms}(B_k)|$ to the size of the unfolded shadow in two exact regimes:
> 1. **Homogeneous (group or linear) regime:** when all equivalence classes of $\pi_k : X \twoheadrightarrow X / {\sim_k}$ have equal size (for instance, under affine readings of rank $r$ on $X = \mathbb{F}_2^n$, or for the mismatch projection $\mu : (\{0,1\}^2)^3 \twoheadrightarrow \{0,1\}^3$ in Lemma `I.3.5`), the exact multiplicative reciprocal-volume identity holds:
>    $$|X / {\sim_k}| \times |\mathrm{Shadow}_k| \;=\; |X| \qquad \bigl(2^r \cdot 2^{n-r} = 2^n;\; 8 \times 8 = 64 \text{ on } Z_2 \text{ in Lemma I.3.5}\bigr),$$
>    so that every factor by which the observer's distinguishable memory classes grow shrinks each shadow fiber of the carrier by the exact same factor.
> 2. **Inhomogeneous regime:** when the fibers $\pi_k^{-1}(m)$ have unequal sizes (for instance, on the delay line `3 + 1 → 2 + 1 + 1 → 1 + 1 + 1 + 1` or on the scalar counter $Z_0 = b$ with binomial fibers `8, 24, 24, 8` in Lemma `I.3.5(1)`), the additive partition $\sum_{m \in X/{\sim_k}} |\pi_k^{-1}(m)| = |X|$ holds (along with Shannon's conditional entropy identity under the uniform measure on $X$: $H(\pi_k) + H(X \mid \pi_k) = \log_2 |X|$).

> **Counterexample I.3.1 (Redundant refinement in $\mathbf{Set}$ without autonomous descent, and breakdown of duality under asymmetric addition of edge outcomes).**
> 1. **Preserving all future readings without an autonomous automaton on $M$:** on $X=\{0,1,2\}$ with constant question $q \equiv 0$ and everywhere-defined step $T=(0, 2, 2)$, the minimal quotient is a singleton ($X/{\sim_\Gamma}=\{X\}$), so the surjection $r : \{0,1\}\mid\{2\}$ onto two classes satisfies $\mathrm{Eq}(r) \subseteq {\sim_\Gamma}$ (condition 1a: $\pi_\Gamma$ factors through $r$), but **does not admit an autonomous step** $\widehat{T}$ on $M$ (condition 1b fails), because $r(0)=r(1)$ whereas $r(T(0))=r(0) \neq r(2)=r(T(1))$ (similarly, on $S_4$ the three-class refinement $\mathcal{P}_3 = \{O_0,\{(0,1)\},\{(1,0)\}\}$ under the involution $K(x,c)=(x,1\oplus c)$ preserves all future readings of $\beta$ but loses the autonomous step $\widehat{K}$).
> 2. **Breakdown of duality under one-sided addition of an action outcome:** if an action $a \in \mathcal{A}$, beyond changing the state, emits its own instantaneous outcome $o_a : \mathrm{Dom}(T_a) \to O_a$, but the fibers $o_a^{-1}(u)$ are added only to the initial subalgebra $B_0$ while retaining the old $\sim_0$ without edge outcomes, then already on $X=\{0,1\}$ with $q \equiv 0$, $T_a = \mathrm{id}_X$, and $o_a(x) = x$, the algebra $B_0$ has `2` atoms $\{0\}, \{1\}$ whereas the old $\sim_0$ has `1` class $\{0, 1\}$.

Let us state separately the synchronous correspondence between the chain of equivalences and the Boolean subalgebras for automata with instantaneous action outcomes on edges (Mealy–Moore signature).

> **Lemma I.3.3 (Synchronous correspondence of subalgebras and indistinguishability for a partial automaton with instantaneous Mealy outcomes).**  
> *Classical foundation.* Duality between finite partitions and atomic Boolean subalgebras, and reduction of Mealy automata to Moore automata—classical automata theory. *Contribution of this book:* Synchronous anti-isomorphism $\mathrm{Atoms}(B_k) = X/{\sim_k}$ for contracts with partial steps, partial readings, and instantaneous Mealy outputs (`256 + 625 + 6561` verified machines).
> Suppose on a finite carrier $X$, in addition to partial state readings $q : \mathrm{Dom}(q) \to V_q$ ($q \in \mathcal{Q}$), for each action $a \in \mathcal{A}$ there are given a partial step $T_a : D_a \to X$ ($D_a = \mathrm{Dom}(T_a) \subseteq X$) and an instantaneous action outcome $o_a : D_a \to O_a$. Totalize each reading $\widehat{q} : X \to V_q \sqcup \{\bot_q\}$ ($\bot_q \notin V_q$) and each edge outcome $\widehat{o}_a : X \to O_a \sqcup \{\bot_{o,a}\}$ ($\bot_{o,a} \notin O_a$, setting $\widehat{o}_a(x) = \bot_{o,a}$ for $x \notin D_a$). Define at the zeroth stage:
> - the equivalence $x \sim^{\mathrm{M}}_0 y \iff \bigl(\forall q \in \mathcal{Q},\; \widehat{q}(x) = \widehat{q}(y)\bigr) \land \bigl(\forall a \in \mathcal{A},\; \widehat{o}_a(x) = \widehat{o}_a(y)\bigr)$ (equality $\widehat{o}_a(x) = \widehat{o}_a(y)$ already entails equivalence of admissibility $x \in D_a \iff y \in D_a$);
> - the Boolean subalgebra $B^{\mathrm{M}}_0 \subseteq \mathcal{P}(X)$ generated by all fibers of totalized readings $\widehat{q}^{-1}(v)$ and of all totalized edge outcomes $\widehat{o}_a^{-1}(u)$ (including $D_a = X \setminus \widehat{o}_a^{-1}(\bot_{o,a})$).  
> For each $k \ge 0$, set
> $$x \sim^{\mathrm{M}}_{k+1} y \iff x \sim^{\mathrm{M}}_k y \;\land\; \forall a \in \mathcal{A}\; \bigl(x \in D_a \implies T_a(x) \sim^{\mathrm{M}}_k T_a(y)\bigr)$$
> and generate $B^{\mathrm{M}}_{k+1} \subseteq \mathcal{P}(X)$ by the union of $B^{\mathrm{M}}_k$ with all preimages $W_a(U) = T_a^{-1}(U) = \{x \in D_a \mid T_a(x) \in U\}$ ($a \in \mathcal{A},\; U \in B^{\mathrm{M}}_k$). Then at every $k \ge 0$ the equivalence classes $X / {\sim^{\mathrm{M}}_k}$ coincide with the atoms $\mathrm{Atoms}(B^{\mathrm{M}}_k)$, where state readings $\widehat{q}$ are tested after all admissible words of length $|w| \le k$, while edge outcomes $\widehat{o}_a$ and action admissibility are tested on all continuations of length $\le k + 1$. *(In particular, on the 4-state delay line $X=\{1,2,3,4\}$ with $T=(2,3,4,4)$, edge outcome $o=(0,0,1,0)$, and $q \equiv 0$, already the zeroth stage $k=0$ yields `2` classes $\{1,2,4\}$ and $\{3\}$, and the step-by-step cardinality history is **`2 → 3 → 4 → 4`**.)*

*Proof.* At $k = 0$, the generating partitions of $B^{\mathrm{M}}_0$ are precisely the fibers of the maps $\widehat{q}$ ($q \in \mathcal{Q}$) and $\widehat{o}_a$ ($a \in \mathcal{A}$), the intersection of whose kernels equals $\sim^{\mathrm{M}}_0$ by definition; here $\widehat{q}$ are tested on the empty word of length $0$, while $\widehat{o}_a$ (together with the indicator of $D_a$) are tested on one-letter continuations of length $1$. If the claim holds for $k$, then whenever $x \sim^{\mathrm{M}}_k y$ we already have $x \in D_a \iff y \in D_a$ for all $a \in \mathcal{A}$ (since $B^{\mathrm{M}}_0 \subseteq B^{\mathrm{M}}_k$). Therefore the condition that $x$ and $y$ are not separated by any set $W_a(U)$ for an atom $U \in \mathrm{Atoms}(B^{\mathrm{M}}_k)$ is equivalent to the condition that for every $a \in \mathcal{A}$ with $x \in D_a$ the next states $T_a(x)$ and $T_a(y)$ lie in the same atom of $B^{\mathrm{M}}_k$, i.e., $T_a(x) \sim^{\mathrm{M}}_k T_a(y)$, which by the induction hypothesis means agreement of state readings on words $a u$ of length $\le k + 1$ and agreement of edge outcomes and admissibility on continuations of length $\le k + 2$. $\square$

Let us also formulate a general criterion for reconstructing an ensemble from a family of projections, which is important to distinguish from dynamical memory refinement.

> **Lemma I.3.4 (Cylindrical closure $\mathcal{C}(R)$ and criterion for lossless ensemble reconstruction from shadows).**  
> *Classical foundation.* Cylindrical closure and natural join of projections in relational algebra (Codd, 1970). *Contribution of this book:* Exact criterion for lossless set reconstruction from a family of windows and quantitative counterexamples of cylindrical inflation (`16 vs 32` on $Q_3^2$ and `64 vs 65` on $Q_4^2$).
> Let a family of projections $\{p_i : X \to Y_i\}_{i \in I}$ be given on a carrier $X$. Assign to each subset (relation) $R \subseteq X$ its **cylindrical hull** from its family of shadows $(p_i(R))_{i \in I}$:
> $$\mathcal{C}(R) = \bigcap_{i \in I} p_i^{-1}\bigl(p_i(R)\bigr) \subseteq X$$
> (when $X = \prod_{s \in S} V_s$ and $p_i = \pi_{S_i}$ are coordinate projections onto index subsets with $\bigcup_{i \in I} S_i = S$, the set $\mathcal{C}(R)$ coincides with the relational natural join $\bowtie_{i \in I} \pi_{S_i}(R)$). Then:
> 1. The operator $\mathcal{C} : \mathcal{P}(X) \to \mathcal{P}(X)$ is extensive ($R \subseteq \mathcal{C}(R)$), monotone ($R_1 \subseteq R_2 \implies \mathcal{C}(R_1) \subseteq \mathcal{C}(R_2)$), shadow-preserving ($p_i(\mathcal{C}(R)) = p_i(R)$ for all $i \in I$), idempotent ($\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{C}(R)) = \mathcal{C}(R)$), and is the **largest** subset in $X$ having the same family of projections $(p_i(R))_{i \in I}$.
> 2. Canonical shadow assembly $\mathrm{Rec}\bigl((p_i(R))_{i \in I}\bigr) = \bigcap_{i \in I} p_i^{-1}(p_i(R))$ **reconstructs the ensemble $R$ itself without spurious added tuples** ($\mathrm{Rec}((p_i(R))_{i \in I}) = R$) **if and only if** $R$ is cylindrically closed:
> $$R = \mathcal{C}(R).$$
> *(Even when $R = \mathcal{C}(R)$, strictly smaller non-closed subsets $S \subsetneq R$ may share the exact same shadows—for example, the diagonal $O_0 \subsetneq S_4$ has the same one-dimensional projections $\{0,1\}$ as the entire square $S_4 = \mathcal{C}(S_4)$; moreover, set-theoretic projections do not record multiset multiplicities.)*

*Proof.* For any $x \in R$ and each $i \in I$ we have $p_i(x) \in p_i(R)$, whence $x \in \bigcap_i p_i^{-1}(p_i(R)) = \mathcal{C}(R)$. Consequently, for each $j \in I$ we have $p_j(R) \subseteq p_j(\mathcal{C}(R)) \subseteq p_j\bigl(p_j^{-1}(p_j(R))\bigr) \subseteq p_j(R)$, i.e., $p_j(\mathcal{C}(R)) = p_j(R)$, whence $\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{C}(R)) = \bigcap_i p_i^{-1}(p_i(\mathcal{C}(R))) = \mathcal{C}(R)$. If $S \subseteq X$ has the same projections $p_i(S) = p_i(R)$ for all $i \in I$, then $S \subseteq \mathcal{C}(S) = \mathcal{C}(R)$, so $\mathcal{C}(R)$ is the largest relation with the given shadows, and the equality $\mathrm{Rec}((p_i(R))_{i \in I}) = R$ coincides by definition with $R = \mathcal{C}(R)$ (when $R \subsetneq \mathcal{C}(R)$, assembly returns the strictly larger set $\mathcal{C}(R) \supsetneq R$ having the same projections). $\square$

*(On the cube $\{0,1\}^3$, the two disjoint 4-element parity ensembles $R_0 = \{000,011,101,110\}$ and $R_1 = \{001,010,100,111\}$ have identical two-dimensional shadows $\pi_{12}(R_b)=\pi_{13}(R_b)=\pi_{23}(R_b)=\{0,1\}^2$, so $\mathcal{C}(R_0) = \mathcal{C}(R_1) = \{0,1\}^3$ of size `8` completely erases the triple-parity bit $b$.)*

Let us record two essential boundaries around Theorem `I.3.1` that lead to the following chapters:
1. **Set-theoretic existence versus executability in a restricted library $\Omega$ (bridge to Chapter `I.4`).** Existence of a map $\widehat{T}_a : M \to M$ in $\mathbf{FinSet}$ does not mean that it is computable within a restricted library of elementary operations $\Omega$: on $X=\{0,1\}$ the negation swap $\neg(x)=1\oplus x$ does not belong to the composition closure of the trivial library $\Omega = \{\mathrm{id}_{\{0,1\}}\}$ ($\langle\Omega\rangle = \{\mathrm{id}_{\{0,1\}}\}$), and slipping in a precomputed lookup table without paying for the program that generates it violates the full-cost contract. Likewise, on $Q_3 \times Q_3$ (`64` states) the projection $\pi(x, y) = (x,\, y_1 \oplus y_2 \oplus y_3)$ yields `16` classes only when all transitions descend to $\pi$, whereas the reversible step $T(x, y) = (x \oplus (y_1, 0, 0),\, y)$ under reading $q = \pi$ preserves the parity of $y$ yet in a single tick exposes the low bit $y_1$ and generates **`32` future classes**; and on $Q_4 \times Q_4$ (`256` states) a step of order `4` yields exactly `64` four-cycles if and only if $\mathrm{Fix}(T^2) = \varnothing$ (otherwise a permutation of order `4` without fixed points consisting of `63` four-cycles and `2` two-cycles yields `65` orbits).
2. **The total monoid $n^n$ versus the partial transformation monoid $(n+1)^n$ and multi-valued specifications.** The relation $\sim_\Gamma$ glues *states* of a carrier of size $|X| = n$ and yields at most $n$ classes, whereas the two-sided syntactic congruence on words $\mathcal{A}^*$ carves out a finite transformation monoid: for **everywhere-defined** maps $X \to X$ its cardinality is bounded by **$n^n$** ($4^4 = 256$ at $n=4$), while for **partial** transformations $X \rightharpoonup X$ (total maps on $X \sqcup \{\bot_{\mathcal{A}}\}$ fixing $\bot_{\mathcal{A}} \mapsto \bot_{\mathcal{A}}$) the upper bound is **$(n + 1)^n$** (`9` partial maps versus `4` total maps at $n = 2$, and `625` versus `256` at $n=4$; see `Corollary I.9.2`). And if a specification prescribes a *set of admissible answers* $A(x) \subseteq \{0,1\}$ instead of a single answer, a unique minimal quotient ceases to exist: for $X=\{a,b,c\}$ with $A(a)=\{0\},\, A(b)=\{0,1\},\, A(c)=\{1\}$, both 2-partitions $\{\{a,b\},\{c\}\}$ and $\{\{a\},\{b,c\}\}$ are admissible and minimal, while merging $\{a,b,c\}$ is forbidden ($A(a)\cap A(b)\cap A(c)=\varnothing$).

## §I.3.4. Stochastic Memory, the Six-Bit Testbed of Named Pairs, and Separating Points Versus Measures

Let us now transfer the question of sufficient memory to **stochastic (Markov) scenes with rational transition probabilities** (within Book I all probabilities belong to the exact field $\mathbb{Q}$, with no floating point and no continuous measures). Suppose that upon choosing action $a \in \mathcal{A}$ from state $x \in X$, the system with probability $P_a(o, x' \mid x) \in \mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}$ ($\sum_{o \in O,\, x' \in X} P_a(o, x' \mid x) = 1$) emits observation $o \in O$ and transitions to $x' \in X$, generating the multi-step law $\mathbb{P}^{(H)}_{x_0}(o_1, \dots, o_H \mid a_1, \dots, a_H)$.

> **Theorem I.3.2 (Criterion for an induced Markov kernel on a quotient and finite-horizon error accumulation bound).**  
> *Classical foundation.* Criterion for existence of an induced Markov kernel on a quotient is the classical Kemeny–Snell lumpability condition (1960); telescoping total-variation bound is standard. *Contribution of this book:* Reading strict lumpability as a requirement on the observer's quotient memory, explicit 7-state counterexample of trace equivalence without lumpability, and witness $\mathrm{TV} = 1$ under coinciding one-dimensional marginals.
> Let $\pi : X \twoheadrightarrow M$ be a surjective compression of a finite carrier $X$ onto a quotient memory $M$.
> 1. **Necessity and sufficiency for an induced kernel commuting with $\pi$, and sufficiency for equality of trajectory laws:** on the quotient set $M$ there exists a Markov kernel $\widehat{P}_a(o, m' \mid m)$ commuting with the projection $\pi$ by the rule $\sum_{x' \in \pi^{-1}(m')} P_a(o, x' \mid x) = \widehat{P}_a(o, m' \mid \pi(x))$ for all $x \in X, a \in \mathcal{A}, o \in O, m' \in M$ **if and only if** for all $x, y \in X$ with $\pi(x) = \pi(y)$, every $a \in \mathcal{A}$, every outcome $o \in O$, and every target class $m' \in M$, the **joint conditional law of the pair `(outcome, next class)`** agrees:
> $$\sum_{x' \in \pi^{-1}(m')} P_a(o, x' \mid x) \;=\; \sum_{x' \in \pi^{-1}(m')} P_a(o, x' \mid y).$$
> Satisfaction of this condition is **sufficient** for exact equality of the observable trajectory distributions $\mathbb{P}^{(H)}_x = \widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{(H)}_{\pi(x)}$ across all finite horizons $H \ge 1$ and all action words $(a_1,\dots,a_H)$ (whereas equality of observable trajectory distributions from $x$ and $y$ by itself, without commutation of the kernel with $\pi$, does not yield the converse implication; see `Counterexample I.3.3`).
> 2. **Finite-horizon error accumulation bound:** if **for every $x \in X$ and every $a \in \mathcal{A}$** the one-step discrepancy of the joint conditional law between the exact kernel $\mu_{x,a}(o, m') := \sum_{x' \in \pi^{-1}(m')} P_a(o, x' \mid x)$ and the quotient kernel $\nu_{\pi(x),a}(o, m') := \widehat{P}_a(o, m' \mid \pi(x))$ on $O \times M$ is at most $\delta \in \mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}$ in total variation distance $\mathrm{TV}(\mu, \nu) = \frac{1}{2}\|\mu - \nu\|_1$, then for every $x \in X$, every finite horizon $H \ge 1$, and every action word $(a_1,\dots,a_H)$ the exact linear inequality holds:
> $$\mathrm{TV}\!\left(\mathbb{P}^{(H)}_x(\,\cdot \mid a_1,\dots,a_H),\; \widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{(H)}_{\pi(x)}(\,\cdot \mid a_1,\dots,a_H)\right) \;\le\; H \delta.$$

*Proof.* In item (1), necessity of the condition for the existence of a kernel $\widehat{P}_a$ satisfying $\widehat{P}_a(o, m' \mid \pi(x)) = \sum_{x'\in\pi^{-1}(m')} P_a(o, x' \mid x)$ follows immediately from constancy of the left-hand side on fibers $\pi(x) = \pi(y)$; conversely, constancy of the sum on fibers uniquely defines $\widehat{P}_a$. Sufficiency for trajectories is proved by induction on $H$: at $H=1$, summing over $m' \in M$ gives $\mathbb{P}^{(1)}_x(o_1 \mid a_1) = \widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{(1)}_{\pi(x)}(o_1 \mid a_1)$, and the step from $H-1$ to $H$ substitutes the induction hypothesis $\mathbb{P}^{(H-1)}_{x_1} = \widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{(H-1)}_{m_1}$, which is constant on the fiber $x_1 \in \pi^{-1}(m_1)$:
$$\mathbb{P}^{(H)}_{x_0}(o_1,\dots,o_H) = \sum_{m_1 \in M} \left(\sum_{x_1 \in \pi^{-1}(m_1)} P_{a_1}(o_1, x_1 \mid x_0)\right) \widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{(H-1)}_{m_1}(o_2,\dots,o_H) = \widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{(H)}_{\pi(x_0)}(o_1,\dots,o_H).$$
In item (2), set $\Delta_H := \sup_{x \in X,\, w \in \mathcal{A}^H} \mathrm{TV}(\mathbb{P}^{(H)}_x(\cdot \mid w), \widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{(H)}_{\pi(x)}(\cdot \mid w))$. For any event $B \subseteq O^H$ with slices $B_{o_1} \subseteq O^{H-1}$, introduce the intermediate sum $I(B) := \sum_{o_1, x_1} P_{a_1}(o_1, x_1 \mid x_0)\,\widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{(H-1)}_{\pi(x_1)}(B_{o_1})$. By the triangle inequality, $|\mathbb{P}^{(H)}_{x_0}(B) - I(B)| \le \sum_{o_1, x_1} P_{a_1}(o_1, x_1 \mid x_0)\,|\mathbb{P}^{(H-1)}_{x_1}(B_{o_1}) - \widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{(H-1)}_{\pi(x_1)}(B_{o_1})| \le \Delta_{H-1}$. In the second term, $I(B) - \widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{(H)}_{\pi(x_0)}(B) = \sum_{o_1, m_1} (\mu_{x_0,a_1} - \nu_{\pi(x_0),a_1})(o_1, m_1)\,g(o_1, m_1)$, where $g(o_1, m_1) := \widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{(H-1)}_{m_1}(B_{o_1}) \in [0, 1]$; since $\sum_{o_1, m_1} (\mu_{x_0,a_1} - \nu_{\pi(x_0),a_1}) = 0$ and $|g - \frac{1}{2}| \le \frac{1}{2}$, centering gives $|I(B) - \widehat{\mathbb{P}}^{(H)}_{\pi(x_0)}(B)| = |\sum (\mu_{x_0,a_1} - \nu_{\pi(x_0),a_1})(g - \frac{1}{2})| \le \frac{1}{2}\|\mu_{x_0,a_1} - \nu_{\pi(x_0),a_1}\|_1 = \mathrm{TV}(\mu_{x_0,a_1}, \nu_{\pi(x_0),a_1}) \le \delta$, whence $\Delta_H \le \delta + \Delta_{H-1} \le H\delta$. $\square$

> **Counterexample I.3.2 (Catastrophe $\mathrm{TV} = 1$ despite agreement of both separate marginals).**  
> Suppose at the first step from two states $x, y$ of the same candidate class $\pi(x)=\pi(y)=m_*$, the joint law of the pair `(answer o_1 ∈ {0,1}, next class m' ∈ {m_0, m_1})` is given by the rational matrices
> $$J_x = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{1}{2} & 0 \\[4pt] 0 & \frac{1}{2} \end{pmatrix}, \qquad\qquad J_y = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & \frac{1}{2} \\[4pt] \frac{1}{2} & 0 \end{pmatrix},$$
> while the second step deterministically emits the answer equal to the stored class index ($o_2 = 0$ on $m_0$ and $o_2 = 1$ on $m_1$). Then the row marginals of the answer $o_1$ are equal ($\frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{2}$), and the column marginals of the next class $m'$ are also equal ($\frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{2}$), yet the two-step laws of pairs $(o_1, o_2)$ are supported on disjoint diagonals ($\mathbb{P}^{(2)}_x(0,0)=\mathbb{P}^{(2)}_x(1,1)=\frac{1}{2}$ versus $\mathbb{P}^{(2)}_y(0,1)=\mathbb{P}^{(2)}_y(1,0)=\frac{1}{2}$) with total variation distance $\mathrm{TV}(\mathbb{P}^{(2)}_x, \mathbb{P}^{(2)}_y) = \mathbf{1}$.

> **Counterexample I.3.3 (Trace equivalence of infinite trajectories is strictly weaker than autonomous Markov lumpability).**  
> On the 7-element rational Markov chain $X = \{s, t, u, v, w, A, B\}$ with reading $q(s)=q(t)=q(u)=q(v)=q(w)=0,\, q(A)=1,\, q(B)=2$ and transitions $s \to \frac{1}{2}u + \frac{1}{2}v$, $t \to 1\cdot w$, $u \to 1\cdot A$, $v \to 1\cdot B$, $w \to \frac{1}{2}A + \frac{1}{2}B$, $A \to 1\cdot A$, $B \to 1\cdot B$, the states $s$ and $t$ generate **the exact same distribution of observable trajectories of any length $H \ge 1$** ($\frac{1}{2}$ on $(0,0,1,1,\dots)$ and $\frac{1}{2}$ on $(0,0,2,2,\dots)$), while $\{u\}$, $\{v\}$, and $\{w\}$ are pairwise trace-distinguishable. Yet on the trace partition $\{\{s,t\},\{u\},\{v\},\{w\},\{A\},\{B\}\}$, Markov lumpability fails already at the first step: $P(\{u\} \mid s) = \frac{1}{2} \neq 0 = P(\{u\} \mid t)$ and $P(\{w\} \mid s) = 0 \neq 1 = P(\{w\} \mid t)$.

Let us now examine two substantive finite results showing how minimal memory responds to adjoining named pair-flips (`4, 6, 8, 8`) and how the criterion for separating pure states differs from the criterion for identifying probability measures from expectations.

> **Lemma I.3.5 (Six-bit testbed of three pairs: canonical encoding $\mathrm{enc}$, the sufficient-memory ladder `4, 6, 8, 8`, the graph $K_2 \square P_3 \neq O_6$, and collapse of the counter under unequal rates).**  
> *Classical foundation.* Cartesian products of graphs $K_2 \square P_3$, step-by-step partition refinement, and Markov lumpability verification—standard finite constructions. *Contribution of this book:* Explicit 64-state six-bit testbed $X_{64} = (\mathbb{F}_2^2)^3$ exhibiting the Nerode refinement staircase `4 → 6 → 8 → 8`, active corridor $K_2 \square P_3$, and breakdown of quotient descent under asymmetric perturbation.
> On the six-bit carrier of three pairs of slots $X = (\{0,1\}^2)^3$ ($|X| = 64$, state $x = (x_{1,0}, x_{1,1}, x_{2,0}, x_{2,1}, x_{3,0}, x_{3,1})$), fix the explicit binary state numbering
> $$\mathrm{enc} : (\{0,1\}^2)^3 \xrightarrow{\;\sim\;} \{1,\dots,64\}, \qquad \mathrm{enc}(x) \;=\; 1 + \sum_{j=1}^{3} \bigl(x_{j,0}\, 2^{2j-2} + x_{j,1}\, 2^{2j-1}\bigr),$$
> and denote the projection onto pairwise mismatches by $\mu : X \twoheadrightarrow \{0,1\}^3$, $\mu(x) = \bigl(m_1(x), m_2(x), m_3(x)\bigr)$, where $m_j(x) = x_{j,0} \oplus x_{j,1} \in \{0,1\}$ ($j = 1, 2, 3$; each fiber $\mu^{-1}(m_1,m_2,m_3)$ consists of $2^3 = 8$ states), and the total mismatch counter by $b(x) = m_1(x) + m_2(x) + m_3(x) \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$. Let the stochastic step $K_{\mathrm{sym}}$ flip one of the six bits uniformly at random (with weight $\frac{1}{6}$, so that at the level of mismatch triples $(m_1, m_2, m_3) \in \{0,1\}^3$ it flips each of the three bits $m_j \mapsto 1 \oplus m_j$ with weight $\frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3}$), and let the deterministic named flip $F_j : X \to X$ invert the first bit of the $j$-th pair ($x_{j,0} \mapsto 1 \oplus x_{j,0}$, inducing on $\{0,1\}^3$ the involution $m_j(F_j(x)) = 1 - m_j(x)$ and $m_r(F_j(x)) = m_r(x)$ for $r \neq j$). Then:
> 1. **Autonomy of the counter at $k = 0$:** for the contract with reading $b$ and the single stochastic step $K_{\mathrm{sym}}$ without named $F_j$, the counter $b \in \{0,1,2,3\}$ forms a closed Markov memory of cardinality **`4`** with exact transition probabilities
> $$\Pr\bigl(b \to b + 1\bigr) = \frac{3 - b}{3}, \qquad\qquad \Pr\bigl(b \to b - 1\bigr) = \frac{b}{3}$$
> (the absolute polarities of the bits and the pair labels are redundant).
> 2. **The ladder `4, 6, 8, 8` when named flips of the first $k$ pairs are permitted ($k \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$):** if the named flips $F_1, \dots, F_k$ are adjoined to the reading $b$ and the step $K_{\mathrm{sym}}$, then the minimal sufficient memory is given by the map
> $$Z_k(x) = \Bigl(m_1(x), \dots, m_k(x),\; \sum_{j = k + 1}^{3} m_j(x)\Bigr)$$
> and takes exactly $2^k (4 - k)$ values, namely **`4, 6, 8, 8`** for $k = 0, 1, 2, 3$.
> 3. **Non-isomorphism of the 6-vertex transition graph at $k = 1$ to the octahedron $O_6$:** at $k = 1$, the memory carrier $Z_1 \in \{0,1\} \times \{0,1,2\}$ has `6` vertices, yet the undirected graph of non-zero transitions of $K_{\mathrm{sym}}$ on $Z_1$ is the Cartesian product $K_2 \square P_3$ with **`7` edges** (vertex degrees `(2,2,2,2,3,3)`), which is **not isomorphic** to the 12-edge octahedron $O_6$.
> 4. **Two mechanisms that destroy the scalar counter:** (a) if two pairs switch their mismatches with unequal probabilities $\frac{1}{3}$ and $\frac{2}{3}$ (while the third pair rests), then states with $(m_1, m_2, m_3) = (1, 0, 0)$ and $(0, 1, 0)$ have the same current counter $b = 1$ but different next-step expectations $\mathbb{E}[b' \mid (1,0,0)] = \frac{1}{3}\cdot 0 + \frac{2}{3}\cdot 2 = \mathbf{\frac{4}{3}}$ and $\mathbb{E}[b' \mid (0,1,0)] = \frac{1}{3}\cdot 2 + \frac{2}{3}\cdot 0 = \mathbf{\frac{2}{3}}$, so $b$ ceases to be a sufficient memory; (b) if the admissibility domain of an action depends on the absolute value of a specific bit (for instance, $\mathrm{Dom}(G) = \{x \mid x_{1,0} = 1\}$), memory must retain the absolute polarity $x_{1,0}$ itself and no longer reduces even to $(m_1, m_2, m_3)$. *(The algebraic Vandermonde determinant separating an arbitrary number of pairwise distinct modes is proved in the present volume in `Chapter I.8, §I.8.5`, whereas continuous relaxation generators and smooth semigroups belong to **Book II**, `Chapter II.2`.)*

*Proof.* When $b$ pairs are mismatched, exactly $2b$ out of the $6$ single-bit flips hit mismatched pairs and decrease $b$ by `1` (probability $\frac{2b}{6} = \frac{b}{3}$), while the remaining $2(3-b)$ flips hit matched pairs and increase $b$ by `1` (probability $\frac{3-b}{3}$), proving (1). In item (2), sufficiency of $Z_k$ follows because $F_i$ ($i \le k$) flips the retained bit $m_i \mapsto 1 - m_i$, while $K_{\mathrm{sym}}$ flips $m_i$ with probability $\frac{1}{3}$ for each $i \le k$ and sends the residual sum $s_{\mathrm{rem}} = \sum_{j>k} m_j$ to $s_{\mathrm{rem}} + 1$ (respectively $s_{\mathrm{rem}} - 1$) with probability $\frac{(3-k) - s_{\mathrm{rem}}}{3}$ (respectively $\frac{s_{\mathrm{rem}}}{3}$). Minimality of $Z_k$ follows because for each $i \le k$ the pair of permitted deterministic readings $b(x)$ and $b(F_i(x)) = b(x) + 1 - 2 m_i(x)$ uniquely recovers $m_i(x) = \frac{1 + b(x) - b(F_i(x))}{2}$, after which subtraction recovers the residual sum $s_{\mathrm{rem}}(x) = b(x) - \sum_{i=1}^k m_i(x)$; since $m_1,\dots,m_k \in \{0,1\}$ and $s_{\mathrm{rem}} \in \{0,\dots,3-k\}$ are independent, the number of values of $Z_k$ is $2^k (4 - k)$. At $k=1$, the transitions of $K_{\mathrm{sym}}$ change either $m_1 \in \{0,1\}$ at fixed $s_{\mathrm{rem}} \in \{0,1,2\}$ (`3` edges) or $s_{\mathrm{rem}}$ by $\pm 1$ at fixed $m_1 \in \{0,1\}$ (`2 × 2 = 4` edges), totaling $3 + 4 = 7$ edges of the graph $K_2 \square P_3 \neq O_6$. Item (4) is verified by direct substitution. $\square$

> **Lemma I.3.6 (Criterion for partial descent under point separation, and rank criterion for separating rational measures).**
> *Classical foundation.* Point separation by a family of functions vs. completeness of linear observables on the space of signed measures (finite-dimensional linear algebra). *Contribution of this book:* Minimal four-point counterexample $S_4$: the pair of coordinate windows $(q_x, q_c)$ uniquely separates all $4$ points yet has rank $3 < 4$ on distributions (`24` pairs of indistinguishable laws with an invisible parity kernel $\beta$).
> 1. **Partial descent under separation of reachable images:** let $r : X \twoheadrightarrow M$ be a surjection, $T : D \to X'$ ($D \subseteq X$) a partial transition, and $r' : X' \to M'$ a recording of the target state. If a family of questions $\mathcal{H} = \{h_\alpha : M' \to V_\alpha\}$ separates the points of the set of reachable images $r'(T(D)) \subseteq M'$, then an induced partial transition $\widehat{T} : \widehat{D} \to M'$ with $D = r^{-1}(\widehat{D})$ and $\widehat{T} \circ r|_D = r' \circ T$ exists **if and only if** the domain $D$ is saturated with respect to $r$ ($D = r^{-1}(r(D))$) and every composite reading $h_\alpha \circ r' \circ T : D \to V_\alpha$ is constant on all admissible fibers of $r$ inside $D$. Without point separation of $\mathcal{H}$ on $r'(T(D))$, the criterion fails: for $X = M' = \{a, b, c\}$, $r(a)=r(b)=0,\, r(c)=1$, $T(a)=a,\, T(b)=T(c)=c$, $r' = \mathrm{id}$, and a constant question $h \equiv 0$, the domain $D=X$ is saturated and $h \circ T \equiv 0$ is constant on fibers of $r$, yet $r'(T(a)) = a \neq c = r'(T(b))$.
> 2. **Rank criterion for uniqueness of a rational measure from expectations:** let $M'$ be finite of cardinality $|M'| = n \ge 1$ and let a finite family of rational observables $h_\alpha : M' \to \mathbb{Q}$ be given. The expectation vector $(\mathbb{E}_\mu[h_\alpha])_\alpha$ uniquely determines **every** rational probability measure $\mu \in \mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{Q}}(M')$ **if and only if** the linear span together with the constant function has full rank:
> $$\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}\bigl\{\mathbf{1}_{M'},\; h_\alpha\bigr\} \;=\; \mathbb{Q}^{M'}.$$
> 3. **Contrast between $\lceil \log_2 n \rceil$ and $n - 1$:** when binary questions may be chosen freely, separating $n$ deterministic points of $M'$ requires and suffices with $\lceil \log_2 n \rceil$ questions, whereas uniquely reconstructing an arbitrary rational measure from scalar expectations requires linear rank $n$ together with $\mathbf{1}_{M'}$, i.e., **at least $n - 1$ scalar expectations** (and both quantities are informational characteristics of the observation problem rather than circuit-cost bounds for computing the answer). On the polar cell $S_4$ ($n = 4$), out of $16^2 = 256$ ordered pairs of binary questions, exactly **`24` pairs** completely separate all `4` points ($\lceil \log_2 4 \rceil = 2$), yet for every such pair $\dim_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}\{\mathbf{1}, q_1, q_2\} = 3 < 4$: for instance, $(q_x, q_c)$ yields identical expectations $(\frac{1}{2}, \frac{1}{2})$ on the mixtures $\mu_0 = \frac{1}{2}(\delta_{00}+\delta_{11})$ and $\mu_1 = \frac{1}{2}(\delta_{01}+\delta_{10})$, which are separated only by adjoining the third question $\beta = q_x \oplus q_c$ (or the product $q_x q_c$), bringing the rank to `4`. *(Meanwhile, the self-updating property of memory does not by itself make transitions automatically affine on mixtures without separate convexity axioms—that step belongs to **Book II**, `Chapters II.6–II.7`.)*

*Proof.* In item (1), necessity of saturation $D = r^{-1}(\widehat{D})$ and constancy of $h_\alpha \circ r' \circ T = h_\alpha \circ \widehat{T} \circ r|_D$ on fibers of $r$ is immediate; conversely, for any $x, y \in D$ with $r(x)=r(y)$, agreement $h_\alpha(r'(T(x))) = h_\alpha(r'(T(y)))$ for all $\alpha$ implies, by point separation of $\mathcal{H}$ on $r'(T(D))$, that $r'(T(x)) = r'(T(y))$, uniquely defining $\widehat{T}(m)$ on $\widehat{D} = r(D)$ (an empty $D = \varnothing$ yields the empty map $\widehat{D}=\varnothing$ without removing the requirement to distinguish $D$ from $X\setminus D$). In item (2), if $\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}\{\mathbf{1}_{M'}, h_\alpha\} = \mathbb{Q}^{M'}$, then every point indicator $\mathbf{1}_{\{m'\}}$ is a rational linear combination of $\mathbf{1}_{M'}$ and $h_\alpha$, whence $\mu(m') = \mathbb{E}_\mu[\mathbf{1}_{\{m'\}}]$ is uniquely recovered from the expectations. Conversely, if the rank is strictly less than $n$, then in the orthogonal complement to $\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}\{\mathbf{1}_{M'}, h_\alpha\}$ with respect to the standard inner product on $\mathbb{Q}^{M'}$ there exists a non-zero rational vector $z \in \mathbb{Q}^{M'} \setminus \{0\}$ satisfying $\sum_{m'\in M'} z_{m'} = 0$ and $\sum_{m'\in M'} z_{m'} h_\alpha(m') = 0$ for all $\alpha$. Choose an integer $K > n \max_{m'\in M'} |z_{m'}|$ (the parameter $\frac{1}{K} \in \mathbb{Q}_{>0}$ is an ordinary rational number). Then around the uniform measure $u_{m'} = \frac{1}{n}$, the vectors $\mu_\pm = u \pm \frac{1}{K} z$ are distinct ($\mu_+ \neq \mu_-$), non-negative, sum to `1`, and yield identically equal expectations $\mathbb{E}_{\mu_+}[h_\alpha] = \mathbb{E}_{\mu_-}[h_\alpha]$ for all $\alpha$. Item (3) follows by comparing cardinalities $2^k \ge n$ for binary point coding with the dimension bound $1 + m \ge n \iff m \ge n - 1$ for $\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}\{\mathbf{1}_{M'}, h_1,\dots,h_m\} = \mathbb{Q}^{M'}$. $\square$

*(The linear-algebraic reflection of the same principle over a finite or rational field is this: for a linear system $x_{t+1} = A x_t,\, y_t = C x_t$, a linear summary $z_t = R x_t$ is closed under the dynamics if and only if its row space is right-invariant, $\mathrm{row}(R)\,A \subseteq \mathrm{row}(R)$, and the minimal sufficient linear subspace of all future readings is the Kalman observability subspace $\mathcal{O}_{C,A} = \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \mathrm{row}(C A^k)$.)*

## §I.3.5. Complete Classification `256 = 64 + 96 + 96` of All Transformations of the Polar Cell $S_4$ Under the Window $\beta = x \oplus c$

Let us combine the constructions of Chapters `I.1` and `I.3` into a single exhaustive finite panorama. On the four-point polar cell $S_4 = \mathbb{F}_2^2 = \{(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)\}$ there are $4^4 = \mathbf{256}$ everywhere-defined transition maps $f : S_4 \to S_4$. Fix as the sole open window the derived comparison question $\beta(x,c) = x \oplus c$ with fibers $O_0 = \{(0,0),(1,1)\}$ and $O_1 = \{(0,1),(1,0)\}$, and for each of the `256` maps $f$ find the cardinality of the minimal memory quotient $|\mathcal{P}_{\beta, f}| = |S_4 / {\sim_{\{\beta\},\{f\}}}|$.

Since the empty word $w = \varepsilon$ already reads the non-trivial question $\beta$, the number of memory classes cannot equal `1`, so $|\mathcal{P}_{\beta, f}| \in \{2, 3, 4\}$, and every 3-class refinement of $\{O_0, O_1\}$ coincides with one of the two canonical 3-partitions:
$$\Pi_{0\text{-split}} = \bigl\{\{(0,0)\},\; \{(1,1)\},\; O_1\bigr\}, \qquad\qquad \Pi_{1\text{-split}} = \bigl\{O_0,\; \{(0,1)\},\; \{(1,0)\}\bigr\}.$$
To eliminate any ambiguity between the sets of maps that *invariantly preserve* a given partition and the sets of maps for which that partition forms the *minimal quotient*, introduce explicit names for the three closure families:
$$D_2 = \mathcal{D}\bigl(\{O_0, O_1\}\bigr), \qquad D_a = \mathcal{D}(\Pi_{0\text{-split}}), \qquad D_b = \mathcal{D}(\Pi_{1\text{-split}}).$$

> **Theorem I.3.3 (Exact partition $256 = 64 + 96 + 96$ of the transformation monoid $S_4^{S_4}$ by future memory size under the window $\beta$, and the diagram of families $D_2, D_a, D_b$).**  
> *Classical foundation.* Full transformation monoid $\mathcal{T}_4$ of cardinality $4^4 = 256$ and structural classes of Green's relations (Green, 1951). *Contribution of this book:* Exhaustive decomposition `256 = 64 + 96 + 96` (`96 = 48 + 36 + 12`) of all endomorphisms of the cell $S_4$ relative to the window $q_x$ into memory-preserving (`64`), memory-doubling (`96`), and autonomy-breaking maps.

> 1. **Exactly `64` maps have minimal memory of `2` classes** ($f \in D_2 \iff \mathcal{P}_{\beta, f} = \{O_0, O_1\}$, i.e., $f$ descends to the quotient by $\beta$): these are all maps sending $O_0$ wholly into one of the two $\beta$-fibers ($2 \cdot 2^2 = 8$ ways) and $O_1$ wholly into one of the two $\beta$-fibers ($8$ ways; $|D_2| = 8 \times 8 = \mathbf{64}$). >    - exactly **`16` maps** commute with the free involution $\sigma(x,c)=(1\oplus x, 1\oplus c)$ ($f \circ \sigma = \sigma \circ f$, `4` choices each for $f(0,0)$ and $f(0,1)$);
>    - the remaining **`48` maps** preserve the partition $\{O_0, O_1\}$ **without** $\sigma$-equivariance (including all `4` constant maps $f \equiv s_*$).
> 2. **Exact algebra of intersections and differences of the families $D_2, D_a, D_b$ and exactly `96` maps with strictly 3-class minimal memory:**
>    - each of the three-class closure families has cardinality $|D_a| = |D_b| = 4 \cdot 4 \cdot (1 + 1 + 2^2) = \mathbf{96}$;
>    - the pairwise intersections with the two-class family have cardinality $|D_2 \cap D_a| = |D_2 \cap D_b| = 8 \cdot 6 = \mathbf{48}$, and the **set difference** has cardinality $|D_2 \setminus D_a| = |D_2 \setminus D_b| = 64 - 48 = \mathbf{16}$ (these are precisely the `16` two-class maps that map $O_1$ bijectively onto $O_0$ and therefore descend to `2` classes **without descending** to $\Pi_{0\text{-split}}$, and symmetrically for $D_b$);
>    - the mutual intersection of the three-class families has cardinality $|D_a \cap D_b| = 6 \times 6 = \mathbf{36}$ and is wholly contained in $D_2$, giving for $D_a$ the exact three-part decomposition $|D_a| = \mathbf{96 = 48 + 36 + 12}$ into $|D_a \setminus D_2| = 48$, $|D_a \cap D_b| = 36$, and $|(D_a \cap D_2) \setminus D_b| = 12$ (while $|D_2 \cap (D_a \cup D_b)| = 48 + 48 - 36 = \mathbf{60}$, and the remaining $64 - 60 = \mathbf{4}$ two-class maps bijectively swap $O_0 \leftrightarrow O_1$ and preserve neither 3-partition);

*Proof.* For $\mathcal{P}_{\beta, f}$ to have cardinality `3`, it must coincide with its Nerode fixed point and be closed under $f$: this means $f \in (D_a \cup D_b) \setminus D_2$. The remaining $256 - 64 - 96 = 96$ maps have 4 classes: `64` ($8 \times 8$) split both fibers at $H=1$, and `32` ($16 + 16$) complete the unfolding at $H=2$. $\square$

## §I.3.6. Categorical Consolidation of the Chapter

### Block K-I.3.1. Partial Moore Coalgebras and the Direction of the Universality Arrow of the Minimal Quotient

- **Category:** $\mathbf{Coalg}(F_{\mathrm{Moore}})$ over the base category $\mathbf{Set}$ for the polynomial endofunctor $F_{\mathrm{Moore}}(X) = O_\bot \times (1 + X)^{\mathcal{A}}$, where $O_\bot = \prod_{q\in\mathcal{Q}} (V_q \sqcup \{\bot_q\})$ and $1 + X = \{\bot_{\mathcal{A}}\} \sqcup X$ (in the symmetric variant with instantaneous action outcomes, the functor expands to $O_\bot \times (1 + O_{\mathrm{act}} \times X)^{\mathcal{A}}$).
- **Objects:** pairs $(X, \gamma_X : X \to O_\bot \times (1 + X)^{\mathcal{A}})$ encoding the instantaneous answer signature $\gamma_{X,0}(x) \in O_\bot$ and the partial transitions $\gamma_{X,a}(x) \in 1 + X$.
- **Morphisms:** coalgebra homomorphisms $r : (X, \gamma_X) \to (M, \gamma_M)$ satisfying $\gamma_M \circ r = (\mathrm{id}_{O_\bot} \times (1 + r)^{\mathcal{A}}) \circ \gamma_X$ (precisely the condition for **autonomous** sufficient memory in Theorem `I.3.1(1b)`).
- **Construction / universal property:** the final $F_{\mathrm{Moore}}$-coalgebra of behaviors is the space of admissible colored trees $\mathfrak{B} \subseteq (O_\bot \cup \{\bot_{\mathcal{A}}\})^{\mathcal{A}^*}$, the canonical unfolding morphism $\mathrm{beh}_X : X \to \mathfrak{B}$ has kernel $\mathrm{Eq}(\mathrm{beh}_X) = {\sim_\Gamma}$, and its `Epi–Mono` coimage in $\mathbf{Coalg}(F_{\mathrm{Moore}})$ is the minimal autonomous quotient $\pi_\Gamma : (X, \gamma_X) \twoheadrightarrow (X / {\sim_\Gamma}, \bar{\gamma})$.
- **What is preserved:** every surjective recording $r : X \twoheadrightarrow M$ preserving all future readings in $\mathbf{Set}$ ($\mathrm{Eq}(r) \subseteq {\sim_\Gamma}$) admits a **unique surjection $\bar{r} : M \twoheadrightarrow X / {\sim_\Gamma}$ through which the canonical quotient factors: $\pi_\Gamma = \bar{r} \circ r$** (not the other way around: when $q \equiv 0$ and $r = \mathrm{id}_X$, the minimal quotient is a singleton, and the identity map $\mathrm{id}_X$ cannot factor through a singleton). Autonomous transitions on $M$ are induced if and only if $\mathrm{Eq}(r)$ is additionally $T_a$-invariant; in that case $\bar{r} : (M, \gamma_M) \twoheadrightarrow (X/{\sim_\Gamma}, \bar{\gamma})$ is the unique surjective morphism of Moore coalgebras closing $\pi_\Gamma = \bar{r} \circ r$.
- **Where it breaks / what the construction does NOT claim:** (a) in $\mathbf{Set}$, the inclusion $\mathrm{Eq}(r) \subseteq {\sim_\Gamma}$ alone does not endow $M$ with an $F_{\mathrm{Moore}}$-coalgebra structure (`Counterexample I.3.1(1)`); (b) replacing $F_{\mathrm{Moore}}$ by a pure Mealy signature $(1 + O_\bot \times X)^{\mathcal{A}}$ loses the reading on the empty word $w = \varepsilon$ for dead-end states $\mathrm{Dom}(T_a) = \varnothing$.

### Block K-I.3.2. Rational Stochastic Coalgebras and Non-Decomposability of the Joint Functor $\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{Q}}(O \times -)$

- **Category:** $\mathbf{Coalg}(G_{\mathrm{stoch}})$ for the functor $G_{\mathrm{stoch}}(X) = \mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{Q}}(O \times X)^{\mathcal{A}}$, where $\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{Q}}(Z)$ is the simplex of finitely supported distributions with rational weights on $Z$.
- **Objects:** rational Markov transducers with observation $P : X \to \mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{Q}}(O \times X)^{\mathcal{A}}$.
- **Morphisms:** arbitrary maps $f : X \to M$ satisfying the coalgebra commutativity condition $\widehat{P} \circ f = \mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathrm{id}_O \times f)^{\mathcal{A}} \circ P$; Theorem `I.3.2` on induced quotient memory considers the subclass of surjective quotient morphisms $f = \pi : X \twoheadrightarrow M$.
- **Construction / universal property:** the action of the distribution functor $\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathrm{id}_O \times f)$ sums probabilities inside each fiber $\{o\} \times f^{-1}(m') \subseteq O \times X$, preserving under a surjection $f = \pi$ the joint conditional law on $O \times M$.
- **What is preserved:** commutation with the induced kernel $\widehat{P}$ on $M$ and, as a consequence, the exact probability law of all finite trajectories $(o_1,\dots,o_H)$ under any action word $(a_1,\dots,a_H)$.
- **Where it breaks / what the construction does NOT claim:** (a) the marginalization arrow $\mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{Q}}(O \times M) \to \mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{Q}}(O) \times \mathcal{D}_{\mathbb{Q}}(M)$ is not a monomorphism (`Counterexample I.3.2`, $\mathrm{TV} = 1$); (b) trace equivalence of trajectories does not imply the existence of a coalgebra morphism onto the trace quotient (`Counterexample I.3.3`).

### Historical-Philosophical Note: The "Present Time" of the Gates (`SK 33–35`), the Latent Trace (*saṃskāra*, `YS 1.18, 4.9`), and Preservation of the Future

1. **Motif from the classical sources.** In kārikās `SK 33–35`, classical Sāṃkhya draws a functional distinction between the external channels of perception and action (*bāhya-indriya*), which operate **only in the present time** (*vartamāna-kālaṃ bāhyam*, like gates *dvārāṇi* passing an instantaneous signal), and the internal instrument (*antaḥkaraṇa*), which operates **across the three times** (*trikālaṃ ābhyantaram*, like a gatekeeper *dvārin* collating past, present, and anticipated continuation). In the *Yoga-sūtras* (`YS 1.18, 4.9`), a closely related question is sharpened by distinguishing an active fluctuation (*vṛtti*) from a residual latent trace (*saṃskāra*), which may remain silent in the current tick yet manifest upon the next shift of conditions.
2. **Strict realization in TNR.** Let $X = \{0,1\}^2$, let the window read the visible bit $q(v, h) = v$, and let the step move the hidden trace into the window: $T(v, h) = (h, h)$. The states $(0,0)$ and $(0,1)$ currently give the same answer $q = 0$, but already after a single action they diverge ($0 \neq 1$), so projection onto the visible bit fails to preserve future distinctions. For an exact autonomous reduction $r : X \twoheadrightarrow M$, neither the current reading nor idempotence of a projection $P^2 = P$ alone suffices (a constant projection is idempotent yet erases the future answer): what is required is constancy of questions on fibers of $r$, saturation of admissibility domains, and update congruence $r(x) = r(y) \implies r(T_a x) = r(T_a y)$, allowing all admissible continuations to be preserved by induction.
3. **Boundary of the comparison.** Neither the Nerode refinement algorithm, nor the classification `256 = 64 + 96 + 96`, nor the theorem on the joint conditional law is derived from `SK 33–35` or `YS 4.9`; there is no "universal minimum of `4`, `16`, or `64` states" in the theory outside a specific contract $\Gamma$, and the three functions of *antaḥkaraṇa* are not identified with three bits or $2^3 = 8$ states.
