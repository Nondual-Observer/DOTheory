# Chapter I.4. Executor, Feedback, and Full Cost

In the preceding chapter we determined which states can be glued into a single class of minimal memory $X / {\sim_\Gamma}$ without losing any future answer. Yet knowing that an ideal quotient map $\widehat{T}_a : M \to M$ *exists in the universe of all set-theoretic lookup tables* is not enough: one must **execute** it with available means and account for the cost of implementation. In this chapter we bring the minimal-memory criterion into the plane of actual execution within a restricted library $\Omega$, closed-loop feedback, error correction, and the complete five-dimensional cost passport.

---

## §I.4.1. A Tangible Scene: A Console with a Restricted Library $\Omega$, Feedback Versus a Blind Tape, and a Reversible Branch Register

Let us place three simple instruments on the bench, each demolishing one of the classic illusions of "free execution":
1. **A lock with an empty keyring.** Suppose the carrier consists of just two states $X = \{0, 1\}$, on which there theoretically exist $2^2 = 4$ total and $(2+1)^2 = 9$ partial transformations (taking into account the typing of named actions $a = (\mathrm{name}_a, T_a)$ from Chapter `I.2`). If the permitted library of the actual console contains only the idle step $\Omega = \{\mathrm{id}_{\{0,1\}}\}$, then its semigroup closure is $\langle\Omega\rangle = \{\mathrm{id}_{\{0,1\}}\}$: the flip operation $\neg(x) = 1 - x$ is **unexecutable** in any number of steps, and slipping in a precomputed answer table without a mechanism to generate it violates the ban on unpaid oracles.
2. **A blind punched tape versus a feedback controller.** Suppose the unknown initial state of an instrument is $x \in \{0, 1\}$, the window displays the current bit $q(x) = x$, and the task at the first step is to issue a command $a \in \{0, 1\}$ matching the actual state: $a = x$. Every **open-loop program without reading the window** (a fixed first command $a_0 \in \{0, 1\}$) fails on the state $1 - a_0$, whereas the simplest **closed-loop feedback policy** $\lambda(q(x)) = q(x)$ solves the task error-free on both inputs (this one-step example pertains specifically to the problem with an unknown initial $x$ and in no way forbids replaying a previously recorded valid tape along an already known fixed trajectory). In exactly the same way we saw in Chapter `I.3` on the six-point carrier $V_6$ that choosing the axis according to the first read answer takes `≤ 2` readings versus `3` unavoidable readings for any static program.
3. **Memory compression versus the reversible Bennett–Toffoli branch log.** Nerode compression discards the past to save memory; if, however, the contract requires **reversibility** of the executed step, one cannot erase the traversed branch without recording it in a separate register. On the 3-bit register $S_8 = \mathbb{F}_2^3$ of triples $(x, c, z)$ (where $z \in \mathbb{F}_2$ is an ancilla bit recording the branch), compare two rules:
   - the irreversible overwrite $T_{\mathrm{ret}}(x, c, z) = (x \oplus c,\; c,\; x \oplus c)$, which compresses `8` states into **`4` images** (the original bit $z$ is irreversibly erased);
   - the reversible trace-recording gate $T_{\mathrm{rev}}(x, c, z) = (x \oplus c,\; c,\; z \oplus x \oplus c)$ with explicit inverse $T_{\mathrm{rev}}^{-1}(x', c', z') = (x' \oplus c',\; c',\; z' \oplus x')$ on all **`8` states**. Yet applying it twice is not enough to invert it: $T_{\mathrm{rev}}^2(x, c, z) = (x,\; c,\; z \oplus c) \neq \mathrm{id}_{S_8}$ when $c = 1$, and the full cycle closes in **`4` steps** ($T_{\mathrm{rev}}^4 = \mathrm{id}_{S_8}$, `010→111→011→110→010`): **reversibility and involutivity are two distinct requirements**. *(And if an external reference shift $c_0 \mapsto c_1$ occurs between two applications of $T_{\mathrm{rev}}$, that shift constitutes a separate external action rather than part of $T_{\mathrm{rev}}$ itself.)*

```text
   (a) Closed execution loop (M, λ, δ)                     (b) Branch recording on the cube S_8 = {(x, c, z) ∈ F_2^3}:
       versus a blind tape (a_1, a_2, ...):                    irreversible overwrite T_ret versus bijection T_rev
```

**Reader's caption to `Fig. I.4.1`:** Panel (a) shows the closed interaction tick between the finite executor memory $M$ and the environment $X$: the read outcome $o_t$ updates memory by the rule $m_{t+1} = \delta(m_t, o_t)$, and the policy $\lambda(m_t)$ issues an admissible command (in the minimal input-matching task, $X=\{0,1\}$ and $M=\{m_{\mathrm{init}}, m_0, m_1\}$ suffice). In panel (b), on the eight vertices of the cube $S_8 = \mathbb{F}_2^3$, the projection onto the first two coordinates $\pi_{12}(x,c,z) = (x,c)$ intertwines both operators $T_{\mathrm{ret}}$ and $T_{\mathrm{rev}}$ with one and the same base step $T_{\mathrm{mix}}(x,c) = (x\oplus c, c)$ on $S_4$. However, the irreversible overwrite $T_{\mathrm{ret}}$ collapses all `8` inputs into the `4` vertices of the plane $\{z = x\}$, gluing, for example, $T_{\mathrm{ret}}(1,0,0) = T_{\mathrm{ret}}(1,0,1) = (1,0,1)$, whereas the reversible gate $T_{\mathrm{rev}}$ defines a bijection of order `4` with cycle type `1 + 1 + 2 + 4` (`2` self-loops `000↺, 001↺`, `1` two-cycle `100↔101`, `1` four-cycle `010→111→011→110→010`; its centralizer in $\mathfrak{S}_8$ has order $2! \cdot 2 \cdot 4 = 16$) and sends that same pair to distinct images $T_{\mathrm{rev}}(1,0,0) = (1,0,1) \neq (1,0,0) = T_{\mathrm{rev}}(1,0,1)$.

Let us contrast the three memory regimes that have thereby been separated without conflation:
- **the minimal future quotient $X / {\sim_\Gamma}$** (Chapter `I.3`) retains just enough classes for error-free continuation of visible answers, deliberately discarding forgotten history;
- **the reversible branch-log extension $S_8 = S_4 \times \mathbb{F}_2$** pays for an explicit ancilla register $z$ whenever the contract forbids irreversible `2 → 1` merging;
- **the closed-loop executor $(M, \lambda, \delta)$ over a library $\Omega$** answers whether the control task itself is solvable with the permitted operations and how many memory states are forced by the causal feedback loop. In terms of solution counts, all three regimes of the trilogy appear here in succession: emptiness $|\mathcal{R}| = 0$ under an impoverished library $\neg \notin \langle\Omega\rangle$ or insufficient memory codes ($C \le 10$ in Theorem `I.4.2` below), uniqueness $|\mathcal{R}| = 1$ of the forced quarter-turn first command $a_1 = -x_0$ and radius-`1` syndrome decoding in the `[6, 3, 3]` code, and gauge multiplicity $|\mathcal{R}| > 1$ under a corridor of admissible commands $\mathrm{Adm}(x)$, an approximate $\varepsilon$-partition, or a vertex-potential shift $\Phi$ of edge costs.

Let us formulate the exact criterion for descending a closed-loop executor onto a compressed memory $r : X \twoheadrightarrow M$, uniting the separation between reading descent and autonomous dynamical descent from Chapter `I.3` with the choice of an admissible action and the quantifier order of causal updating.

> **Theorem I.4.1 (Descent of a closed-loop feedback executor and quantifier order of causal update).**  
> *Classical foundation.* Distinction between open-loop and closed-loop control in automata theory and reversible computation with history retention (Bennett, 1973; Toffoli, 1980). *Contribution of this book:* Strict separation of feedback from a program tape already on `Fin 2` and contrast between the order-`4` reversible trace gate $T_{\mathrm{rev}}$ (`8 → 8`, cycles `1+1+2+4`) and erasing overwrite $T_{\mathrm{ret}}$ (`8 → 4`) on $S_8$.
> Let a contract $\Gamma$ be given on a carrier $X$ with typed partial actions $T_a : D_a \to X$ ($a \in \mathcal{A}$), current observation window $o : X \to O_\bot$, surjective memory recording $r : X \twoheadrightarrow M$, and an explicitly fixed set of considered admissible states $R \subseteq X$ (set $R = X$ if the guarantee is required across all fiber representatives, or $R = X_{\mathrm{reach}}$ when closure of the reachable set has been proved separately).
> 1. **Existence and correctness of an already specified update on $R$:** for a policy $\lambda : M \to \mathcal{A} \sqcup \{\mathsf{stop}\}$, execution from every $x \in R$ is admissible if and only if whenever $\lambda(m) = a \in \mathcal{A}$ we have **(a)** $R \cap r^{-1}(m) \subseteq D_a$ *(on $X=\{0,1\}, r(0)=r(1), R=\{0\}, T(0)=0, 1 \notin D_T$, execution on $R$ is valid without $r^{-1}(m)\subseteq D_T$)*. Under condition (a):
>    - **(b) existence of a valid update table $\delta$** on realizable pairs $(m, u)$ with $u \in o(T_a(R \cap r^{-1}(m)))$ is equivalent to representative-independence with the causal quantifier order:
>      $$\forall m \in M\;(\lambda(m)=a \in \mathcal{A})\;\forall u \in O_\bot\;\;\exists m' \in M\;\;\forall x \in R \cap r^{-1}(m):\quad o(T_a(x)) = u \;\Longrightarrow\; r\bigl(T_a(x)\bigr) = m'$$
>      (equivalently: $\forall x, y \in R \cap r^{-1}(m),\; o(T_a x) = o(T_a y) \implies r(T_a x) = r(T_a y)$); and for an **already given** partial function $\delta : M \times O_\bot \rightharpoonup M$, it is required in addition that for every $x \in R \cap r^{-1}(m)$ the pair $(m, o(T_a x))$ lie in $\mathrm{Dom}(\delta)$ and satisfy the equality **$\delta(m, o(T_a x)) = r(T_a x)$** *(even when $|X|=1$, existence of a valid table does not validate a different pre-assigned incorrect $\delta$)*.
>    *(Identical future visible answers do not by themselves pay for an autonomous $\delta$: on $X=\{0,1,2\}$, $r(0)=r(1)=A, r(2)=B$, constant output $o \equiv 0$, and $T(0)=0, T(1)=T(2)=2$, all future visible answers agree, yet the transition from $A$ is ambiguous: $r(T(0))=A \neq B=r(T(1))$.)*
> 2. **Loss of uniqueness of the minimal executor under a corridor of admissible commands:** if in each state $x \in X$ the task permits a *non-empty set* of admissible commands $\mathrm{Adm}(x) \subseteq \mathcal{A}$, then the condition for policy admissibility on the quotient $M$ takes the form $\bigcap_{x \in r^{-1}(m)} \mathrm{Adm}(x) \neq \varnothing$ for every $m \in M$, and **a unique smallest memory quotient does not exist in general**: on $X = \{x_0, x_1, x_2\}$ with $\mathrm{Adm}(x_0) = \{a_0\}$, $\mathrm{Adm}(x_1) = \{a_0, a_1\}$, $\mathrm{Adm}(x_2) = \{a_1\}$, the minimal cardinality of an admissible memory is `2`, but it is realized by two incomparable partitions $\{\{x_0, x_1\}, \{x_2\}\}$ and $\{\{x_0\}, \{x_1, x_2\}\}$ whose common coarsening $\{X\}$ is inadmissible ($\mathrm{Adm}(x_0) \cap \mathrm{Adm}(x_1) \cap \mathrm{Adm}(x_2) = \varnothing$).

*Proof.* In item (1), the inclusion $R \cap r^{-1}(m) \subseteq D_{\lambda(m)}$ is precisely what rules out failure $\bot_{\mathcal{A}}$ on every $x \in R$; when it holds, a partial function $\delta$ satisfying $\delta(m, o(T_a x)) = r(T_a x)$ on all realizable pairs $(m, o(T_a x))$ ($x \in R \cap r^{-1}(m)$) exists if and only if whenever $o(T_a x) = o(T_a y) = u$ the target classes $r(T_a x) = r(T_a y) = m'$ also coincide, and a specific given $\delta$ is valid if and only if on every such pair it is defined and returns $r(T_a x)$. In item (2), the intersection of all three sets is empty, whereas on both 2-block partitions the intersections within blocks are non-empty (`{a_0}` and `{a_1}`). $\square$

## §I.4.2. Exact Memory Bounds of a Closed-Loop Controller `11 / 19 / 22` on a Finite Two-Dimensional Lag Testbed

Let us demonstrate on a finite two-dimensional lag testbed how, within a single strictly fixed operational contract, one computes the **absolute set-theoretic minimum number of memory states of a closed-loop executor** and how tightening the time budget and the stopping interface raises this minimum along the exact staircase **`11 → 19 → 22`**. Let us stress the methodological boundary right away: the numbers `11`, `19`, and `22` are proved **strictly for the contract declared below on the 49-point grid $D^2$** and are never transferred without a new proof to arbitrary computational systems or everyday instruments.

> **Theorem I.4.2 (Exact lower and upper memory bounds of a stationary controller `11 / 19 / 22`).**  
> *Classical foundation.* Pigeonhole–Moore state-count lower bounds, synthesis of finite feedback automata, and orthogonal cyclic Latin squares over $\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z}$. *Contribution of this book:* Exact memory bounds in a restricted library on the grid $D_M^2$: $C_{\min}(\mathrm{A}) = 11$ ($M=7$), general formula $C_{\min}(D_M) = 2M + 5$ ($19$ at $M=7$, class `B`), and closed-form constructive realizations $Q_{11}, Q_{19}$ (via squares $A_{(u+w)\bmod 3}, B_{(-u+w)\bmod 3}$), and $Q_{22}$ (`139` commands).
> Let the controlled plant have carrier $S = D^2$, where $D = \{-3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3\}$ ($|S| = 49$), and controlled step with integer command $a \in [-L, L] \cap \mathbb{Z}$ ($L \ge 3$):
> $$P_a(x, y) \;=\; (x - y + a,\;\; x).$$
> Unlike the signature of Theorem `I.4.1` (where the command was chosen solely from the current memory code $m$ and the reading arrived after the step), here we consider a **Mealy automaton that reads the fresh input at each tick before issuing the command**: a stationary deterministic controller with a finite set of memory codes $Q$ ($|Q| = C$) is specified by a command rule $\lambda : Q \times D \to [-L, L] \cap \mathbb{Z}$, a memory transition $\delta : Q \times D \to Q$, a readiness predicate, and an answer decoder. It starts from a **single initial code** $q_0 \in Q$ on an unknown pair $(x_0, y_0) \in D^2$, sees at each tick **only the fresh reading of the first coordinate $x$** (with no free clock, step counter, external log, or separate uncounted output register), must keep the state inside $D^2$ at all intermediate ticks, and at the moment of stopping must **simultaneously** return the plant itself to the exact initial pair $(x_0, y_0)$ and output the hidden initial lag $r = y_0$. Distinguish two stopping interfaces:
> - **Class `A` (global code readiness):** the set of ready codes $Q_{\mathrm{ready}} \subset Q$ and the answer decoder $r : Q_{\mathrm{ready}} \to D$ depend only on the code $q \in Q$ (upon entering $q \in Q_{\mathrm{ready}}$ the controller halts immediately without issuing a new command);
> - **Class `B` (conditional readiness reading the fresh input):** the stopping predicate $\mathrm{Ready} : Q \times D \to \{0, 1\}$ and the lag decoder $r : \mathrm{Ready}^{-1}(1) \to D$ depend on the pair $(q, x)$.  
> Then three exact bounds hold:
> 1. **Absolute minimum $C_{\min} = 11$ under unbounded finite time (both in Class `B` and in Class `A`, for any $L \ge 3$):** no successful controller exists with $C \le 10$ states, and there exists an explicit controller already in the stricter Class `A` on exactly **`11` states** ($Q_{11} = \{q_0, q_1, q_2, q_3\} \sqcup \{\mathrm{Ready}_v\}_{v \in D}$, `4 + 7 = 11`): on the first three ticks from $q_0 \to q_1 \to q_2 \to q_3$, upon reading the current $x$ it issues the quarter-turn command $a = -x$ ($|a| \le 3$), sending $(x_0, y_0) \mapsto (-y_0, x_0) \mapsto (-x_0, -y_0) \mapsto (y_0, -x_0)$, and on the fourth tick in state $q_3$ the fresh input read is $x_3 = y_0$, whereupon the controller issues the fourth command $a_4 = -x_3 = -y_0$, returning the plant to $(y_0 - (-x_0) - y_0,\, y_0) = (x_0, y_0)$ while simultaneously transitioning to the ready code $\delta(q_3, y_0) = \mathrm{Ready}_{y_0}$ with answer $r(\mathrm{Ready}_{y_0}) = y_0$. *(For a general grid $D_M = \{-M, \dots, M\}$ with any $M \ge 2$ and any $L \ge M$, the same 4-tick scheme and the same pigeonhole bound establish the exact equality $C_{\min}(D_M) = 2M + 5$, since under $C \le 2M + 4$ the total command count satisfies $(6M - 1) + 2M(8M + 2) = 16M^2 + 10M - 1 > 3(2M + 1)^2 \ge N_{\mathrm{nonready}}$.)*
> 2. **Fast budget of $\le 3$ executed commands:** when $L \le 8$, a solution in $\le 3$ commands is impossible (at the corners $(\pm 3, \pm 3)$ the forced triple $(-x_0,\, x_0 + 2y_0,\, x_0 - 2y_0)$ requires $|a| = 9$). When $L = 9$ and the budget is $\le 3$ commands, the exact minimum total number of commands summed over all `49` inputs is **`139`** ($1\cdot 1 + 6\cdot 2 + 42\cdot 3 = 139$: `1` command $a_1 = 0$ for $(0,0)$, `2` commands $a_1 = -x_0,\, a_2 = 2x_0$ for the `6` starts of the form $(x_0, 0)$ with $x_0 \neq 0$, and `3` commands $a_1 = -x_0,\, a_2 = x_0 + 2y_0,\, a_3 = x_0 - 2y_0$ for the `42` starts with $y_0 \neq 0$), and the exact minimum state counts are **`C_min = 19` in Class `B`** and **`C_min = 22` in Class `A`** ($1 + 7 + 7 + 7 = 22$). Both bounds are achieved by the explicit constructive automata $Q_{19}$ (`1 + 7 + 3 + 3 + 5 = 19`, sharing readout and step-3 command execution via two order-`3` cyclic Latin squares) and $Q_{22}$ (`1 + 7 + 7 + 7 = 22`) given below and verified across all `49` starts by the volume's verifier.
> 3. **Separating state count, register bit-width, and table cost:** in a fixed binary register, both `19` and `22` states occupy the exact same number of bits $\lceil \log_2 19 \rceil = \lceil \log_2 22 \rceil = \mathbf{5}$ bits, while the full ROM cost of the transition table and decoder is accounted for separately.

To descend in Class `B` from `22` to the exact lower bound of **`19` states**, set
$$Q_{19} \;=\; \{q_0\} \;\sqcup\; \{F_u\}_{u \in D} \;\sqcup\; \{A_0, A_1, A_2\} \;\sqcup\; \{B_0, B_1, B_2\} \;\sqcup\; \{C_0, C_1, C_2, C_3, C_4\} \qquad (1 + 7 + 3 + 3 + 5 = 19):$$
- **conditional stopping and decoder $\mathrm{Ready}(q, x) = 1 \implies r(q, x)$:** the pair $(F_0, 0)$ is ready immediately after step 1 with $r(F_0, 0) = 0$; code $C_4$ is ready on all $x \neq 0$ with $r(C_4, x) = 0$; codes $C_m$ ($m \in \{1,2,3\}$) are ready on all $x \neq 0$ with $r(C_m, x) = -\operatorname{sgn}(x)\,m$; codes $A_k$ ($k \in \{0,1,2\}$) are ready at $x = 0$ with $r(A_k, 0) = k + 1$ and at $x > 0$ with the unique $w \in \{1,2,3\}$ satisfying $x + w \equiv k \pmod 3$; symmetrically, $B_k$ ($k \in \{0,1,2\}$) are ready at $x = 0$ with $r(B_k, 0) = -(k + 1)$ and at $x < 0$ with $r(B_k, x) = -w$, where $w \in \{1,2,3\}$ is uniquely determined by $-x + w \equiv k \pmod 3$; all remaining pairs are non-ready;
- **commands and transitions on non-ready pairs:** from $(q_0, x_0)$ issue $(-x_0, F_{x_0})$; from $(F_u, 0)$ with $u \neq 0$ issue $(2u, C_4)$; from $(F_0, v)$ with $v \neq 0$ issue $(-2v, C_0)$, and from $(C_0, w)$ issue $(-2w, A_{w-1})$ when $w > 0$ and $(-2w, B_{-w-1})$ when $w < 0$; for $u > 0$, code $F_u$ stays in $(u - 2v, F_u)$ on inputs $v < 0$, while on inputs $x > 0$ (which occur both at tick 3 for $y_0 = x > 0$ and at tick 2 for $y_0 = -x < 0$) it transitions along the Latin square to $(u - 2x, A_{(u+x)\bmod 3})$, whereupon at tick 3 on negative input $x = -w < 0$ code $A_k$ uniquely recovers $u \in \{1,2,3\}$ from $u + w \equiv k \pmod 3$ and issues $(u - 2x, C_w)$; symmetrically, for $u < 0$, code $F_u$ issues $(u - 2v, F_u)$ on $v > 0$ and $(u - 2x, B_{(-u-x)\bmod 3})$ on $x < 0$, whence on $x = w > 0$ code $B_k$ recovers $u \in \{-3,-2,-1\}$ from $-u + w \equiv k \pmod 3$ and issues $(u - 2x, C_w)$.

*Proof.* (1) Stationarity without a clock forbids both self-intersection of non-ready full triples $(q, x, y) \in Q \times D^2$ along a single trajectory (which would cause an infinite loop) and collision of two trajectories from distinct starts $s_0 \neq s_0'$ at the same non-ready triple (which would force both to terminate in the same state pair). Hence the sum of command lengths $\sum_{s_0 \in D^2} \ell(s_0)$ cannot exceed the total number of non-ready triples $N_{\mathrm{nonready}}$. For each $v \in D$, the seven starts $(v, y_0)$ require $\ge 7$ ready codes on input $v$, so under the hypothesis $C \le 10$ at most $\le 3$ non-ready codes remain on each $v \in D$, giving $N_{\mathrm{nonready}} \le 7 \cdot 3 \cdot 7 = \mathbf{147}$. To keep $x_0 - y_0 + a_1 \in [-3, 3]$ for all $y_0 \in [-3, 3]$, the first step is forced to be $a_1 = -x_0$, sending $(x_0, y_0) \mapsto (-y_0, x_0)$ with code $f(x_0) = \delta(q_0, x_0) \neq q_0$. For any $v \neq 0$, the state $(v, x_0) \neq (x_0, -v)$ has not yet returned to the initial point, so $q_0$ and all values $f(x_0)$ are non-ready on input $v$, whence $|\mathrm{im}\,f| \le 3 - 1 = 2$ (the 7 inputs $x_0$ are partitioned into at most `2` blocks). On the row $y_0 = 0$, returning on the second step requires $a_2 = 2x_0$, which costs $\ge 1 + 2 + 5\cdot 3 = 18$ commands. In each of the `6` rows with fixed $y_0 \neq 0$, returning in $\le 2$ steps is impossible (the second coordinate after step 2 is $-y_0 \neq y_0$), and returning in exactly `3` steps $(x_0, y_0) \mapsto (-y_0, x_0) \mapsto (y_0, -y_0) \mapsto (x_0, y_0)$ requires issuing $a_2 = x_0 + 2y_0$ at tick 2. At tick 2, however, the fresh input $v = -y_0$ is **identical for all `7` starts $(x_0, y_0)$ in row $y_0$**, so the command $\lambda(f(x_0), -y_0)$ issued by the controller depends on $x_0$ solely through the two-valued code $f(x_0)$ ($|\mathrm{im}\,f| \le 2$) and can take **at most `2` distinct values**, whereas the required quantity $a_2 = x_0 + 2y_0$ takes `7` pairwise distinct values as $x_0$ ranges over $D$. By the pigeonhole principle, the equality $\lambda(f(x_0), -y_0) = x_0 + 2y_0$ holds for at most `2` of the `7` starts in row $y_0$, forcing $\ge 4$ steps on the remaining `5` starts: totaling $\ge 18 + 6 \cdot (2\cdot 3 + 5\cdot 4) = 18 + 156 = \mathbf{174 > 147}$ for any $L \ge 3$ (and at $L = 8$ also independently $18 + 38\cdot 3 + 4\cdot 4 = 148 > 147$; on the general grid $D_M$ with $M \ge 2$ and $C \le 2M + 4$, the same count gives $(1 + 2\cdot 2 + (2M - 2)\cdot 3) + 2M(2\cdot 3 + (2M - 1)\cdot 4) = 16M^2 + 10M - 1 > 3(2M + 1)^2$). This contradiction yields $C_{\min} \ge 11$, and the 11-state automaton in the table above explicitly realizes $C = 11$ in Class `A`.  
(2) Under a budget of $\le 3$ commands and $L = 9$, the seven distinct commands $a_2 = x_0 - 2v$ on each $v = -y_0 \neq 0$ force all seven first codes $F_x$ ($x \in D$) to be pairwise distinct and force the eight-element set $\{q_0, F_{-3},\dots,F_3\}$ to be non-ready at every $v \neq 0$. In Class `B` with $C = 15 + k$, on an input $y \neq 0$ outside that eight-element set and the $\ge 7$ ready codes there remain $\le k$ additional non-ready slots, so at least $7 - k$ second codes $G(x, y)$ (issuing $x - 2y$) must coincide with the uniquely compatible first code $F_x$. Yet simultaneous equality $G(x, y) = F_x = G(x, -y)$ for $y \neq 0$ sends the trajectories from $(x, y)$ and $(x, -y)$ after step 3 to the exact same pair $(F_x, x)$, destroying the decoding of $y \neq -y$; hence $2(7 - k) \le 7 \implies k \ge 4 \implies C \ge \mathbf{19}$, and this bound is explicitly achieved by $Q_{19}$ above (whose Latin triples $A_{0,1,2}$ and $B_{0,1,2}$ share positive/negative readout with opposite-sign step-3 execution). In Class `A`, the equality $G(x, y) = F_x$ for $y \neq 0$ is impossible altogether (the third transition from $F_x$ must land in $Q_{\mathrm{ready}}$, yet it is also the second non-ready step from $(x, -y)$), separating 4 disjoint families of sizes $1 + 7 + 7 + 7 = \mathbf{22}$, explicitly realized by $Q_{22}$ in the table above. Direct substitution of the command triples across all `49` inputs verifies return to $(x_0, y_0)$ inside $D^2$ at $L = 9$ and yields the exact sum $1 + 6\cdot 2 + 42\cdot 3 = 139$. $\square$

## §I.4.3. The Three-Layer `[6, 3, 3]` State-and-Order Recovery Scheme, General Compatibility Bound, and Approximate Continuation

Later in Chapter `I.6` we will meet the classical length-`7` Hamming syndrome map on the Fano plane. Here let us unfold its sibling—the **complete three-layer engineering scheme for recovering the named state and polling order of four binary slots**, strictly separating the number of initial realizations **`384`** from the number of verified corrupted packets **`752 640`**.

> **Theorem I.4.3 (Pairwise-comparison code `[6, 3, 3]` and exact model of three-layer error correction).**  
> *Classical foundation.* The code of pairwise comparisons is the classical binary linear `[6, 3, 3]_2` cut-space code of the complete graph $K_4$, equivalent to the shortened `[7, 4, 3]_2` Hamming code (weight enumerator $1 + 4z^3 + 3z^4$). *Contribution of this book:* Exact model of three-layer equivariant single-error correction linking four site values, polling permutations in $\mathfrak{S}_4$ (`384` signed-permutation symmetries), and the comparison syndrome.
> Suppose four named binary slots store a value vector $x = (x_1, x_2, x_3, x_4) \in \mathbb{F}_2^4$ (`16` states), polled in the order of a name permutation $\pi \in \mathfrak{S}_4$ (`24` orders; in total $16 \times 24 = \mathbf{384}$ **initial named realizations** $(x, \pi)$). Set $y_i = x_{\pi(i)}$ ($i = 1, 2, 3, 4$), call $a = y_1 \in \mathbb{F}_2$ the reference bit of the first slot, and record the 6-bit word of all pairwise comparisons on the edges of the tetrahedron $K_4$:
> $$w(y) \;=\; (d_{12}, d_{13}, d_{14}, d_{23}, d_{24}, d_{34}) \;\in\; \mathbb{F}_2^6, \qquad d_{ij} = y_i \oplus y_j.$$
> 1. **The cut space (coboundary image $\operatorname{im}\delta_0$) of $K_4$ as a `[6, 3, 3]` linear code and necessity of all three layers:** the coboundary map $\delta_0 : \mathbb{F}_2^4 \to \mathbb{F}_2^6$, $(y_1, y_2, y_3, y_4) \mapsto w(y)$, has as its kernel the diagonal of constant functions $\ker\delta_0 = \{0000, 1111\}$ and carves out in $\mathbb{F}_2^6$ the three-dimensional **cut space of the complete graph $K_4$** ($C_6 = \operatorname{im}\delta_0$, `8` codewords; this construction anticipates the exact sequence of coboundaries and cycles on graphs in `Chapter I.6` and `Chapter I.9`). If two records $y, y'$ have distinct relative states ($y' \notin \{y, \bar{y}\}$), then after aligning the reference they differ in $r \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ of the four slots, and their comparison words $w(y)$ and $w(y')$ differ precisely on the edges of the cut of $K_4$ between the $r$ flipped and $4 - r$ unflipped vertices—that is, in exactly $r(4 - r) \in \{3, 4\}$ positions. Consequently, the minimum Hamming distance equals the size of the minimum non-trivial cut in $K_4$, $\min_{r=1,2,3} r(4 - r) = \mathbf{3}$, and $C_6$ is a **`[6, 3, 3]`** linear code. *(From the `6` comparisons alone without the reference $a$, the states $y$ and $\bar{y}$ remain indistinguishable, while without recording the order $\pi$ the assignment of values to slot names is lost.)*
> 2. **Exact Cartesian-product model of single-fault corruptions across the three layers:** transmit a packet consisting of three explicitly paid-for layers: (I) the 6-bit comparison word $w \in \mathbb{F}_2^6$, (II) the threefold repetition of the reference bit $(a, a, a) \in \mathbb{F}_2^3$, and (III) three full copies of the order permutation $(\pi, \pi, \pi) \in \mathfrak{S}_4^3$. Declare the admissible fault region to be the **Cartesian product of three tolerances** (this is a set-theoretic product of tolerances, not a hypothesis of probabilistic noise independence):
>    - at most `1` inverted bit out of `6` in the comparison layer (`1 + 6 = 7` patterns);
>    - at most `1` inverted bit out of `3` in the reference repetition (`1 + 3 = 4` patterns);
>    - at most `1` corrupted copy out of `3` in the order record (if the corrupted copy takes the form of one of the `23` other permutations in $\mathfrak{S}_4 \setminus \{\pi\}$, there arise $1 + 3 \times 23 = 70$ order patterns and $7 \times 4 \times 70 = \mathbf{1960}$ corruption patterns per initial realization, and paired with all `384` initial realizations, exactly $384 \times 1960 = \mathbf{752\,640}$ verified combinations; under two-out-of-three majority matching, the same order recovery remains valid even when the single corrupted copy is replaced by an arbitrary malformed string).  
>    Then nearest-neighbor decoding in $C_6$ (unique within the Hamming ball of radius `1`, since $d_{\min} = 3 > 2\cdot 1$), `2-out-of-3` majority voting for $a$, and selecting the matching pair of copies for $\pi$ with inverse assembly $y_1 = \hat{a}$, $y_j = \hat{a} \oplus \hat{d}_{1j}$ ($j=2,3,4$), $x_{\hat{\pi}(i)} = y_i$ **recover the original pair $(x, \pi)$ without error in `100%` of admissible cases** (`752 640 / 752 640`).
> 3. **Boundary of the protection model:** this scheme does NOT protect against two faults in a single layer or against correlated corruption of a common source prior to copying (which alters all three copies of the reference or order simultaneously), nor does it claim global information-theoretic minimality of the combined length of all three layers.

*Proof.* The number of edges of the complete graph $K_4$ between a subset of $r$ flipped vertices and $4 - r$ unflipped vertices is $r(4 - r)$, which for $r \in \{1, 2, 3\}$ takes the values $3, 4, 3 \ge 3$. By the triangle inequality, the Hamming balls of radius `1` around the `8` codewords in $\mathbb{F}_2^6$ are pairwise disjoint ($8 \times (1 + 6) = 56 \le 64$), ensuring unique correction of $\le 1$ bit flip in $w$; two uncorrupted copies out of three uniquely determine both $a$ and $\pi$, after which $y_j = a \oplus (y_1 \oplus y_j)$ and $x_{\pi(i)} = y_i$ recover $(x, \pi)$. $\square$

Let us now examine two criteria for gluing a candidate with its environment and for approximate stochastic continuation under closed-loop control.

> **Lemma I.4.1 (Criterion for a single shared boundary answer and linear kernel equality $\ker A = \ker Q$).**
> *Classical foundation.* Factorization of a map $u_* : R \to U$ through a projection $q : X \to Y$ if and only if $\mathrm{Eq}(q)|_R \subseteq \mathrm{Eq}(u_*)$—standard descent lemma for maps. *Contribution of this book:* Interpreting violation of kernel inclusion $\mathrm{Eq}(q)|_R \nsubseteq \mathrm{Eq}(u_*)$ as the exact operational threshold forcing an internal controller memory register $Q$.
> 1. Let $\Gamma_{\mathrm{comp}} \subseteq X \times E$ be a compatibility relation between a candidate $x \in X$ and an environment $e \in E$ with non-empty fibers of admissible candidates $F_e = \{x \in X \mid (x, e) \in \Gamma_{\mathrm{comp}}\} \neq \varnothing$ on active projections. Compatibility reduces to testing equality of a single shared boundary answer
>    $$(x, e) \in \Gamma_{\mathrm{comp}} \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \alpha(x) = \beta(e)$$
>    **if and only if** any two fibers $F_e, F_{e'}$ either coincide or are disjoint ($F_e = F_{e'}$ or $F_e \cap F_{e'} = \varnothing$). *(On three candidates $\{a, b, c\}$ with $F_0 = \{a, b\}$ and $F_1 = \{b, c\}$, the non-empty intersection $\{b\}$ forces $\alpha(a) = \alpha(b) = \alpha(c)$, destroying the separation of admissible $a$ from inadmissible $c$ in environment $0$.)*
> 2. In a linear problem over $\mathbb{F}_2$ where admissible candidates form an affine coset $v_0 + \ker A$ for $A v = d$ ($d \in \mathrm{im}\,A$), a linear boundary reading $Q$ simultaneously (a) yields the same answer on all admissible completions ($\ker A \subseteq \ker Q$) and (b) allows any inadmissible candidate to be rejected without error from that single answer alone ($\ker Q \subseteq \ker A$) **if and only if $\ker A = \ker Q$**.

*Proof.* (1) If $(x,e)\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{comp}} \iff \alpha(x)=\beta(e)$, then $F_e = \alpha^{-1}(\{\beta(e)\})$ are point preimages and are therefore either equal or disjoint; conversely, partitioning the active union $\bigcup_e F_e$ into disjoint blocks $B_i$ allows one to set $\alpha(x) = i$ for $x \in B_i$ and $\beta(e) = i$ when $F_e = B_i$ (retaining distinct failure markers for inactive elements). (2) Constancy of linear $Q$ on $v_0 + \ker A$ is equivalent to $Q(\ker A) = 0 \iff \ker A \subseteq \ker Q$, while the condition $Q v = Q v_0 \implies A v = d$ is equivalent to $v - v_0 \in \ker Q \implies v - v_0 \in \ker A$, i.e., $\ker Q \subseteq \ker A$. $\square$

> **Lemma I.4.2 (Bound on approximate continuation under visible-history control and non-uniqueness of the coarsest $\varepsilon$-partition).**
> *Classical foundation.* Non-transitivity of threshold proximity relations (Poincaré–Zeeman–Bell chains) and telescoping total-variation bounds for Markov kernels. *Contribution of this book:* Counterexample `{0, 3/5, 6/5}` refuting quotient partitioning by approximate tolerance, paired with an exact rational bound on trajectory divergence over horizon $H$.
> 1. Suppose on a finite carrier $X$ with surjection $\pi : X \twoheadrightarrow Z$ the admissibility domains $D_a = \pi^{-1}(\bar{D}_a)$ and protected instantaneous answers are **strictly constant on fibers of $\pi$**, for each class $z \in Z$ a representative $x_z \in \pi^{-1}(z)$ is fixed in advance with reduced rational kernel $\bar{K}_a(z, z') = \sum_{\pi(u)=z'} K_a(x_z, u)$, and the within-fiber diameter of projected transition rows is uniformly bounded by the rational number
>    $$\delta \;=\; \max_{a \in \mathcal{A},\, z \in \bar{D}_a}\; \max_{x, y \in \pi^{-1}(z)} \frac{1}{2} \sum_{z' \in Z} \left| \sum_{\pi(u)=z'} K_a(x, u) - \sum_{\pi(u)=z'} K_a(y, u) \right| \;\in\; \mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}.$$
>    If $\bar{\mu}_0 = \pi_* \mu_0$ and both sides are driven by **one and the same causal command-selection law depending only on the visible history** $(z_0, a_0, \dots, z_t)$ (without peeking at the hidden $x_t$), then on any finite horizon $H \ge 1$ the full distributions of visible histories $\mathbb{P}_H^{\mathrm{hist}}$ and $\bar{\mathbb{P}}_H^{\mathrm{hist}}$ on the space of records $(z_0, a_0, \dots, z_H)$ satisfy the exact bound
>    $$\mathrm{TV}\bigl(\mathbb{P}_H^{\mathrm{hist}},\; \bar{\mathbb{P}}_H^{\mathrm{hist}}\bigr) \;\le\; \min(1,\; H \delta),$$
>    and if $\mu_H$ and $\bar{\mu}_H$ denote the state distributions **at the terminal instant $H$**, then a fortiori the terminal visible state distribution satisfies $\mathrm{TV}(\pi_* \mu_H,\; \bar{\mu}_H) \le \min(1,\; H \delta)$.
> 2. **Non-uniqueness of the coarsest admissible approximate partition:** on the three-point carrier $X = \{x_0, x_1, x_2\}$ with rational scale $f(x_0) = 0,\, f(x_1) = \frac{3}{5},\, f(x_2) = \frac{6}{5}$ and within-class diameter threshold $\varepsilon = \frac{7}{10}$, both two-class partitions $P = \{\{x_0, x_1\}, \{x_2\}\}$ and $Q = \{\{x_0\}, \{x_1, x_2\}\}$ are admissible (diameter $\frac{3}{5} \le \frac{7}{10}$) and minimal in number of classes, yet their sole common coarsening $\{X\}$ has diameter $\frac{6}{5} > \frac{7}{10}$ and is inadmissible.

*Proof.* (1) Given a shared visible history $(z_0, a_0, \dots, z_t)$, both sides select the same admissible command $a_t$ (any policy randomness is coupled by a shared seed), and the conditional distribution of the hidden $x_t$ is supported on $\pi^{-1}(z_t)$; by convexity of total variation, the mixture of projected rows $\sum_{x \in \pi^{-1}(z_t)} w(x) \pi_* K_{a_t}(x, \cdot)$ lies within $\delta$ of the representative row $\bar{K}_{a_t}(z_t, \cdot)$, so step-by-step maximal coupling glues the next visible symbol $z_{t+1}$ with mismatch probability $\le \delta$. By the union bound over step mismatches at $t = 0, \dots, H-1$, the probability of any discrepancy between the full visible histories $(z_0, a_0, \dots, z_H)$ is at most $\min(1, H\delta)$, and projecting onto the final coordinate $z_H$ cannot increase total variation distance, giving $\mathrm{TV}(\pi_* \mu_H, \bar{\mu}_H) \le \mathrm{TV}(\mathbb{P}_H^{\mathrm{hist}}, \bar{\mathbb{P}}_H^{\mathrm{hist}}) \le \min(1, H\delta)$ (violating exact constancy of admissibility domains or letting the policy read the hidden bit immediately destroys this guarantee). (2) Direct inspection of the rational differences $\frac{3}{5} - 0 = \frac{6}{5} - \frac{3}{5} = \frac{6}{10} \le \frac{7}{10} < \frac{12}{10} = \frac{6}{5} - 0$. $\square$

## §I.4.4. The Five-Dimensional Passport of Memory and Cost, Non-Recoverability of Additive Path Cost, and Left Zero of a Monoid

Let us now bring together the strict **five-dimensional passport of memory and computational cost**, which forbids replacing the full cost of an algorithm by a single number "address bit-width":
1. **Set-theoretic number of memory classes $|P_*|$:** the cardinality of the minimal quotient $X / {\sim_\Gamma}$ or the number of controller codes $|Q|$ (such as `11`, `19`, `22` in Theorem `I.4.2`).
2. **Linear rank $\dim E_\infty$:** the dimension of the space of linear observables wherever a linear span is introduced (not automatically equal to $|P_*|$ or $\log_2 |P_*|$).
3. **Fixed binary address width $\lceil \log_2 |P_*| \rceil$ — strictly for finite sets $1 \le |P_*| < \infty$:** for instance, the `15` non-empty ensembles $\mathcal{P}^+(S_4)$ require at least $\lceil \log_2 15 \rceil = \mathbf{4}$ bits of fixed binary code (`2³ = 8 < 15 ≤ 16 = 2⁴`), while controllers with `19` and `22` states both occupy `5` register bits. For infinite carriers the formula $\lceil \log_2 |P_*| \rceil$ does not apply.
4. **Total cold-start byte budget $B_{\mathrm{total}}$:** the separate sum of the sizes of (a) the executable program code, (b) any external dictionary / transition lookup table (if a table is precomputed in advance, its full volume enters $B_{\mathrm{total}}$), (c) the address itself, (d) working buffers and branch logs, and (e) the explicit unfolding decoder (inverting the embedding $d_j \circ e_j = \mathrm{id}$, as in the cut-address tree of Chapter `I.8` (`§I.8.3`) and on the $5 \times 5 = 25$ pair grid of Chapter `I.7` (`§I.7.2`)).
5. **Operational step cost, operand bit-length, and budget frame:** the count of elementary operations must be accompanied by a declaration of the tariff model and operand bit-length growth. In particular:
   - in the arithmetic bottom-up assembly over prime-divisor subsets (Chapter `I.8`, `§I.8.3`), the canonical step uses exactly **`7` operations in the declared algebraic tariff** (`2` $\gcd$ evaluations, `3` exact integer divisions, and `2` multiplications), which records the number of high-level tariff primitives and **is not identified** with wall-clock time on variable-bit-length integers;
   - when passing a budget recursively to a nested subproblem, the residual frame conservation law is written, relative to an explicitly fixed unit of work, as
     $$\mathrm{local.max\_work} \;=\; \mathrm{local.spent} + \mathrm{global.remaining};$$
   - finally, a **conservation invariant** $I(T(x)) = I(x)$ (guaranteeing non-escape from an admissible fiber) must never be passed off as a **measure of progress toward a goal** $V(T(x)) < V(x)$ (an invariant confines the orbit, but by itself allows endless running in place without ever reaching the target set $\gamma_{\mathrm{goal}}$).

Let us close the chapter with two exact algebraic results on trajectory cost and non-commutative memory reset.

> **Lemma I.4.3 (Non-recoverability of additive path cost from endpoints and length, vertex-potential gauge, and left zero of a reset monoid).**  
> *Classical foundation.* Shifting arc costs by vertex-potential differences $c'(u,v) = c(u,v) + \phi(u) - \phi(v)$ is classical potential reweighting (Gallai, 1958; Johnson, 1977); constant maps are left zeros in $\mathrm{End}(X)$. *Contribution of this book:* Proof of gauge non-recoverability of step costs from closed-cycle sums, connection of irreversible reset with the monoid left zero (`19 683` triples), and pairwise irreducibility of the five coordinates of the cost vector $\mathcal{C}$.
> 1. **Additive path cost and vertex-potential gauge shift:** suppose on the directed graph with two vertices `{1, 2}` and all four directed edges the step-cost matrix is $r = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 0 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 \end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, and the cost of a path $\gamma = (v_0 \to v_1 \to \dots \to v_H)$ is $C(\gamma) = \sum_{t=0}^{H-1} r(v_t, v_{t+1})$. Then two paths of the same length $H = 2$ with the same start `1` and the same end `1` have **different total costs**: $C(1 \to 1 \to 1) = 0 + 0 = \mathbf{0} \neq \mathbf{2} = 2 + 0 = C(1 \to 2 \to 1)$ (so the triple `(start, end, length)` does not recover the cost of the history). For any integer vertex potential $\Phi : V \to \mathbb{Z}$, the gauged edge cost $r_\Phi(i, j) := r(i, j) + \Phi(j) - \Phi(i)$ telescopically changes the cost of any path $v_0 \leadsto v_H$ only by the boundary difference $\Phi(v_H) - \Phi(v_0)$ and **strictly preserves the total cost of every closed cycle** ($v_H = v_0 \implies C_\Phi(\gamma) = C(\gamma)$).
> 2. **Memory reset is a left zero in forward composition order, NOT a two-sided zero:** in the transformation monoid $\mathrm{End}(X)$ under ordinary functional composition $(f \circ g)(x) = f(g(x))$ ("first $g$, then $f$"), the constant reset $K_0(x) \equiv 0$ on $X = \{0, 1\}$ satisfies $K_0 \circ f = K_0$ for every $f$, but **is not a right zero**: for negation $\neg(x) = 1 - x$ we have
>    $$(K_0 \circ \neg)(x) = 0 = K_0(x), \qquad\text{whereas}\qquad (\neg \circ K_0)(x) = \neg(0) = 1 = K_1(x) \neq K_0(x).$$
>    In the exact same way, in the associative semidirect affine-sign monoid $T_{u, s}(m, e) = (s m + u,\; s e)$ on $\mathbb{Z}^d \times \{-1, 0, +1\}$ with composition law $T_{u_2, s_2} \circ T_{u_1, s_1} = T_{u_2 + s_2 u_1,\, s_2 s_1}$ and identity $T_{0, +1}$, the erasure element $T_{0, 0}(m, e) = (0, 0)$ is a **left zero** ($T_{0, 0} \circ T_{u, s} = T_{0, 0}$), but for every $u \neq 0$ multiplication on the right yields $T_{u, +1} \circ T_{0, 0} = T_{u, 0} \neq T_{0, 0}$.

*Proof.* (1) Summing $\sum_{t=0}^{H-1} (r(v_t, v_{t+1}) + \Phi(v_{t+1}) - \Phi(v_t)) = C(\gamma) + \Phi(v_H) - \Phi(v_0)$ telescopically cancels all internal vertices. (2) Direct substitution: $(T_{u_2, s_2} \circ T_{u_1, s_1})(m, e) = (s_2(s_1 m + u_1) + u_2,\, s_2 s_1 e) = ((s_2 s_1)m + (u_2 + s_2 u_1),\, (s_2 s_1)e)$; setting $(u_2, s_2) = (0, 0)$ gives $(0, 0)$, whereas setting $(u_1, s_1) = (0, 0)$ and $(u_2, s_2) = (u, +1)$ gives $T_{u, 0}(m, e) = (u, 0) \neq (0, 0)$ whenever $u \neq 0$. $\square$

- **Category:** the category of partial feedback transducers (Mealy/Moore automata with typed command outputs) $\mathbf{Sys}_{\mathcal{A}, O_\bot}$ and the endomorphism monoid $\bigl(\mathrm{End}(X), \circ, \mathrm{id}_X\bigr)$.

  $$(m, u) \in \mathrm{Dom}(\delta_1) \;\Longrightarrow\; (h(m), u) \in \mathrm{Dom}(\delta_2) \;\;\land\;\; h\bigl(\delta_1(m, u)\bigr) = \delta_2\bigl(h(m), u\bigr).$$

- **What is preserved:** admissibility of commands on the considered fiber representatives ($R \cap r^{-1}(m) \subseteq D_{\lambda(m)}$), deterministic command trajectories on a closed domain $R$, and left absorption for constant reset ($K_0 \circ f = K_0$, $T_{0,0} \circ T_{u,s} = T_{0,0}$).

### Historical-Philosophical Note: Specific Function of the Triad (`SK 29`), the Composite Instrument (*karaṇam*, `SK 31`), and Separating Kinematics from Goal

1. **Motif from the classical source.** In Īśvarakṛṣṇa's *Sāṃkhya-kārikā*, kārikā `SK 29` (*sva-lakṣaṇyaṃ vṛttiṃ trayasya śeṣāḥ...*) isolates the specific operational function (*sva-lakṣaṇyaṃ vṛttiṃ*) of each of the three internal faculties, while kārikā `SK 31` (*svāṃ svāṃ pratipadyante parasparākūta-hetukāṃ vṛttim; puruṣārtha eva hetur na kenacit kāryate karaṇam*) describes how the faculties enter into their mutually coordinated work: the grammatical subject in the second half-stanza of `SK 31` is the **composite instrument of action (*karaṇam*)**, for which *puruṣārtha* sets the sole teleological orientation, and no external pusher forcibly drives the instrument from outside.
2. **Strict realization in TNR.** In the present chapter this motif highlights the strict separation of two independent levels: the operational contract $\Gamma$ and the permitted library $\Omega$ specify only the *admissible transition kinematics and conservation invariants* $I(T(x)) = I(x)$, whereas the **target specification** $\gamma_{\mathrm{goal}}$ and its associated **progress measure** $V(T(x)) < V(x)$ must be declared and paid for separately—without invoking a free external homunculus and without mistaking a conservation invariant for progress.
3. **Boundary of the comparison.** Neither the exact controller state bounds `11 / 19 / 22`, nor the parameters of the `[6, 3, 3]` pairwise-comparison code, nor the formulas of the five-dimensional cost passport are derived from or rely upon the text of `SK 29` and `SK 31`.
