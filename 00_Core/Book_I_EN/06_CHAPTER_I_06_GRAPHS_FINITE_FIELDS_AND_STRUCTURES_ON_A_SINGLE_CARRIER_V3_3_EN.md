# Chapter I.6. Graphs, Finite Fields, and Different Structures on a Single Carrier

In the preceding five chapters we saw how a finite carrier gives rise to question partitions (`Chapter I.1`), contravariant predicate transfer (`Chapter I.2`), minimal memory of future continuations (`Chapter I.3`), a closed-loop feedback executor (`Chapter I.4`), and signed integer sums with contribution cancellation (`Chapter I.5`). Let us now take the next step: fix **one and the same finite set of labels** and ask how changing the connectivity question itself on that carrier gives birth to non-isomorphic graphs, finite fields, the projective Fano plane, the Petersen graph, and linear codes.

---

## §I.6.1. A Tangible Scene: Six Fixed Terminals on the Bench and Four Different Connectivity Graphs

Place on the bench a terminal block with **six fixed terminals**. So as not to invent ad hoc names for them, let us link by one explicitly chosen linear bijection three coordinate representations of an object already familiar from Chapter `I.1`. Order the four states of the polar cell $S_4 = \mathbb{F}_2^2$ as $((0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1)) \leftrightarrow (1,2,3,4)$ and define an $\mathbb{F}_2$-linear isomorphism $\Phi_{\mathrm{aff}} : \mathbb{F}_2^3 \xrightarrow{\sim} \mathrm{Aff}(S_4, \mathbb{F}_2)$ from three-bit binary words onto the `8` affine Boolean questions by the rule $(u_1, u_2, u_3) \mapsto u_1 (1 \oplus \beta) \oplus u_2 q_c \oplus u_3 q_x$:
$$000 \mapsto 0,\quad 111 \mapsto 1,\qquad 001 \mapsto q_x,\quad 110 \mapsto 1 \oplus q_x,\qquad 010 \mapsto q_c,\quad 101 \mapsto 1 \oplus q_c,\qquad 011 \mapsto \beta,\quad 100 \mapsto 1 \oplus \beta.$$
The chosen chart $\Phi_{\mathrm{aff}}$ (we fix this specific consistent coordinate choice without claiming it is the only possible isomorphism) is $\mathbb{F}_2$-linear across all $8 \times 8 = 64$ word pairs, sends bitwise complementation $u \mapsto u \oplus 111$ to answer negation $q \mapsto 1 \oplus q$, and restricts on the non-constant words $A_6 = \mathbb{F}_2^3 \setminus \{000, 111\}$ to an exact bijection with the `6` balanced questions, and—via the unit fiber $S_u = \{s \in S_4 \mid \Phi_{\mathrm{aff}}(u)(s) = 1\}$—with the `6` two-element subsets $\binom{\{1,2,3,4\}}{2}$:
$$A_6 = \{001,\; 010,\; 100,\; 110,\; 101,\; 011\} \;\xrightarrow[\;\sim\;]{\;u \mapsto S_u\;}\; \binom{\{1, 2, 3, 4\}}{2}, \qquad |A_6| = 6.$$

Removing the poles `000` and `111` from the cube $\{0,1\}^3$ leaves by itself only **six labels**, while the resulting graph still depends entirely on which edge question we ask in terms of Hamming distance $d_{\mathrm{H}}(u, v) = \mathrm{wt}(u \oplus v)$:
- **Hamming distance `1` (the single binary-step question):** joins terminals differing in exactly one bit ($d_{\mathrm{H}}(u, v) = 1$, traversing the equator of the cube $Q_3$ bypassing the poles `000` and `111`: $001 - 011 - 010 - 110 - 100 - 101 - 001$). This yields the **simple cycle** $C_6$: **`6` edges**, degree `2`, and the dihedral group $D_6$ of order **`12`**. It is this cycle $C_6$ that forms the *induced subgraph* of the original cube $Q_3$ upon deleting the poles;
- **Hamming distance `2` (the double-flip question):** joins words of equal weight parity ($d_{\mathrm{H}}(u, v) = 2$). This yields **two disconnected triangles** $2C_3 = \{001, 010, 100\} \sqcup \{110, 101, 011\}$: **`6` edges**, degree `2`, and an automorphism group of order $2 \cdot (3!)^2 = \mathbf{72}$;
- **Hamming distance `1` or `2` (the pairwise-independence question on $S_4$):** joins two words whenever $u \neq v$ and $u \oplus v \neq 111$, i.e., $|\{i,j\} \cap \{k,\ell\}| = 1$. Only **after this explicit expansion of the adjacency relation** (uniting the `6` edges of the equator $C_6$ and the `6` edges of $2C_3$) do the same six terminals assemble into the **connected octahedron** $O_6 \cong K_{2,2,2} \cong L(K_4)$: **`12` edges**, degree `4`, group of order **`48`**, and **`8` triangles** splitting into `4` common-state stars and `4` zero-sum triples in $\mathbb{F}_2^3$;
- **Hamming distance `3` (the mutual-complement question):** joins pairs of words with $u \oplus v = 111$ (disjoint pairs in $\{1,2,3,4\}$). This yields **three disconnected opposite pairs** $3K_2$: **`3` edges**, degree `1`, `3` connected components, and an automorphism group of order $2^3 \cdot 3! = \mathbf{48}$.

**Reader's caption to `Fig. I.6.1`:** On one and the same six-element carrier $A_6 = \{001, 010, 100, 110, 101, 011\}$ (linked via the linear chart $\Phi_{\mathrm{aff}}$ to the six balanced questions on $S_4$ and the six two-element fibers in $\binom{S_4}{2}$), three different predicates define the simple undirected graphs $3K_2$ (`3` edges, $|\mathrm{Aut}| = 48$), $O_6$ (`12` edges, $|\mathrm{Aut}| = 48$, $\mathrm{Aut}(O_6) \cong \mathfrak{S}_4 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$), and $C_6$ (`6` edges, $|\mathrm{Aut}| = 12$—the stabilizer of the polar pair $\{000,111\}$ in $\mathrm{Aut}(Q_3)$). The identity map on vertices $\mathrm{id}_{A_6}$ defines an injective graph homomorphism $(A_6, E_{C_6}) \hookrightarrow (A_6, E_{O_6})$, erasing the distinction between a single ($\mathrm{wt}=1$, `6` edges) and a double ($\mathrm{wt}=2$, `6` edges of $2C_3$) bit flip, while simultaneously identifying $3K_2$ with the graph complement of $O_6$ ($E_{3K_2} \sqcup E_{O_6} = \binom{A_6}{2}$, `3 + 12 = 15`). By contrast, neither the identity map $\mathrm{id}_{A_6}$ nor any vertex map whatsoever defines a graph homomorphism $O_6 \to 3K_2$, because $O_6$ contains the triangle $\{001, 010, 100\}$ and has chromatic number $\chi(O_6) = 3 > 2 = \chi(3K_2)$, even though the automorphism groups of the two graphs have the exact same order (`48 = 48`).

## §I.6.2. Methodological Rule of the Chapter: Coincidence of Cardinalities Does Not Imply Coincidence of Structures

In finite mathematics, different questions regularly produce the same small cardinalities (`4, 6, 7, 8, 9, 12, 15, 16, 24, 27, 35, 48, 64, 120`), so throughout this chapter morphism verification is carried out separately from numerical comparison:
- behind the number **`7`** stand the `7` points of the Fano plane $\mathrm{PG}(2,2)$ and separately its `7` lines, whereas their simple bipartite incidence graph (the Levi–Heawood graph) has **`14` vertices** and **`21` edges**;
- behind a pair of Fano planes inside the three-dimensional projective space $\mathrm{PG}(3,2)$ of `15` points stands a union of cardinality $7 + 7 - 3 = \mathbf{11}$ (since two planes in $\mathrm{PG}(3,2)$ intersect in a line of `3` points), not a one-point wedge of cardinality $7 + 7 - 1 = 13$;
- behind the Klein quadric $Q^+(5,2)$ stand **`35` points** (corresponding to the `35` lines of $\mathrm{PG}(3,2)$) and separately **`105` lines** lying wholly on $Q^+(5,2)$ (versus **`315`** isotropic lines of the ambient symplectic geometry $W(5,2)$);
- behind the order **`48`** stand the pairwise non-isomorphic groups $W(B_3)$, $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_3)$, and $2O$ having **`19`**, **`13`**, and **`1`** involutions respectively;
- behind the `128` words of $\mathbb{F}_2^7$ stand **`20` orbits** of the cyclic group $C_7$ on all of $\mathbb{F}_2^7$ (**`18`** on the `126` non-constant words), **`12` orbits** of the Frobenius normalizer $\mathrm{Frob}_{21} = C_7 \rtimes C_3$ (**`10`** on non-constant words), and **`10` orbits** of the full Fano collineation group $\mathrm{GL}_3(\mathbb{F}_2)$ (**`8`** on non-constant words).

Let us now trace a sequence of problems in which each successive finite object is introduced precisely when the preceding carrier encounters an exact obstruction. The entire chapter remains strictly within the scope of Book I: without the real field $\mathbb{R}$, continuous angles, or Lie groups.

## §I.6.3. Five Steps of Equipment Expansion: From Flows on $K_4$ and Finite Fields to the Fano Plane, the Petersen Graph, and Codes

### (a) Flows on $K_4$, Bicycles of the Octahedron, the Distinction $L(Q_3) \not\cong L(O_6)$, and Integer Symmetries of the Cube

**What question motivates this step.** In `§I.6.1` the six questions $A_6$ formed the line graph of the tetrahedron $O_6 = L(K_4)$. Let us now ask: how do closed binary flows on $K_4$ itself transfer to the edges of the octahedron $O_6$, and what graphs arise when passing to the faces and edges of the dual cube–octahedron pair?

First let us separate the structure types on a finite set $V$: a bare carrier $V$ has no edges; a simple graph $(V, E \subseteq \binom{V}{2})$ on four points makes the two-dimensional cube $Q_2$ isomorphic to the cycle $C_4$; the Cartesian product $K_2 \square K_2$ adds a two-coloring of axes; cellularly filling a 2-face kills the boundary cycle; and a $k$-uniform hypergraph $(V, \mathcal{H} \subseteq \binom{V}{k})$ for $k \ge 3$ does not reduce to its pairwise edges.

Around the dual pair $(O_6, Q_3)$, four exact geometric facts must be kept in mind. First, the cube $Q_3$ dual to the octahedron (`8` vertices, `12` edges) is built **on the `8` triangular faces of $O_6$** adjacent across shared edges, not by any vertex map `6 → 8`; in turn, passing from the `6` elements of $A_6$ to the `7` points of the Fano plane is accomplished by adjoining the constant $\mathbf{1} = 111$ ($6 + 1 = 7$), not by gluing vertices of $O_6$ (any gluing reduces the number of vertices to $\le 5$).

Second, **the cuboctahedron is the line graph of the cube $L(Q_3)$ (`12` vertices and `24` edges)** and is **not isomorphic** to the full line graph of the octahedron $L(O_6)$, which has **`12` vertices and `36` edges**. Indeed, at each vertex of the cube `3` edges meet, yielding a triangle $K_3$ in $L(Q_3)$, whereas at each vertex of the octahedron `4` edges meet, generating in $L(O_6)$ a complete tetrahedron $K_4$ ($6 \times 6 = 36$ edges); the cuboctahedron is obtained from the octahedron as its **medial graph** $M(O_6)$, which joins only edges that are consecutive around a shared triangular face (a cycle $C_4$ around each vertex instead of $K_4$).

Third, on the centered integer cube $\{-1, +1\}^3 \subset \mathbb{Z}^3$, the group of all $3 \times 3$ signed permutation matrices $W(B_3) = \mathrm{O}_3(\mathbb{Z})$ of order $2^3 \cdot 3! = \mathbf{48}$ acts linearly via $z \mapsto P z$, and this action transfers to the binary cube $\{0, 1\}^3$ by the **affine** formula $x \mapsto \frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{1}_3 + P(2x - \mathbf{1}_3))$. The inscribed even tetrahedron $\{(x_1,x_2,x_3)\in\{0,1\}^3 \mid x_1\oplus x_2\oplus x_3 = 0\}$ has a stabilizer of order **`24`** in $W(B_3)$ and of order **`12`** ($A_4$) in the subgroup $\mathrm{SO}_3(\mathbb{Z})$ of matrices with $\det P = +1$; meanwhile, the triangles of weight-`1` and weight-`2` vertices in $\{0,1\}^3$ are congruent under the integer inversion $x \mapsto (1,1,1) - x$ with equal squared side lengths $\sum_m (x_m - y_m)^2 = \mathbf{2}$, without invoking the irrational square root $\sqrt{2}$.

Fourth, the Coxeter group $W(B_3)$ is generated by the three simple reflections $(s_0, s_1, s_2)$ of a flag "vertex $\subset$ edge $\subset$ face," and the indices of its **seven proper standard parabolic subgroups** (generated by subsets of unmarked reflections of orders `8, 6, 4, 2, 2, 2, 1`) form the exact Wythoff sequence **`6, 8, 12, 24, 24, 24, 48`**. The number `4` of space diagonals of the cube does not belong to this parabolic series, as it corresponds to a non-parabolic stabilizer of order `12` containing the central inversion $-I_3$.

> **Theorem I.6.1 (Two quadruples of octahedron triangles, the identity $M M^\top = N^\top N$, and isomorphism of the wire space onto the bicycle code).**  
> *Classical foundation.* Cycle and cut spaces of a graph are Kirchhoff's classic (1847); $O_6$ is the line graph $L(K_4)$, and the identity $M M^\top = N^\top N$ over $\mathbb{F}_2$ relates the incidence matrix to the line-graph adjacency matrix. *Contribution of this book:* Pairwise non-isomorphism of the graphs `3K_2, O_6, C_6, 2C_3` on the shared carrier $A_6$ of balanced questions of $S_4$, non-isomorphism $L(Q_3) \not\cong L(O_6)$, face split `8 = 4 + 4` of $O_6$, and binary bicycle code `[12, 3, 6]_2`.
> Let $N \in \mathbb{F}_2^{4 \times 6}$ be the vertex–edge incidence matrix of the complete graph $K_4$, and let $M \in \mathbb{F}_2^{6 \times 12}$ be the vertex–edge incidence matrix of the octahedron $O_6 = L(K_4)$.
> 1. **Splitting `8 = 4 + 4` of the triangular cliques of $O_6$ and symmetry reduction `48 → 24`:** the eight triangles ($K_3$) in $O_6$ split into `4` vertex stars of $H_R$-type (triples of edges of $K_4$ sharing a common endpoint; in $A_6$ their sum is $\mathbf{1}=111$) and `4` cyclic triangles of $H_F$-type (triples of edges forming a 3-cycle in $K_4$; in $A_6$ their sum is $000$). The complement involution $\kappa(q) = q \oplus 111$ swaps these two quadruples; the full group $|\mathrm{Aut}(O_6)| = 48$ acts transitively on the `8` faces, while the subgroup of automorphisms preserving each of the two face classes individually has order **`24`** ($\cong \mathfrak{S}_4$).
> 2. **Wire space and bicycle code:** let $W_w = \ker N \subset \mathbb{F}_2^6$ ($\dim_{\mathbb{F}_2} W_w = 3$, $|W_w| = 8$), and let $\mathcal{B} = \ker M \cap \mathrm{im}\, M^\top \subset \mathbb{F}_2^{12}$ be the bicycle space of the octahedron. Over $\mathbb{F}_2$ the identity
> $$M M^\top = N^\top N$$
> holds, and the map $\Theta : W_w \to \mathcal{B}$, $\Theta(u) = M^\top u$, is a linear isomorphism ($|\mathcal{B}| = 8$) onto a self-orthogonal code in $\mathbb{F}_2^{12}$ with Hamming weight enumerator **`0^{(1)}, 6^{(4)}, 8^{(3)}`** and linear half-weight functional $h_{\mathcal{B}}(B) = \frac{1}{2}\mathrm{wt}(B) \bmod 2$.

*Proof.* On the diagonal, $(M M^\top)_{e,e} = 4 \equiv 0 \pmod 2$ and $(N^\top N)_{e,e} = 2 \equiv 0 \pmod 2$; for $e \neq f$, edges $e, f$ are adjacent in $O_6$ if and only if they share exactly one vertex in $K_4$, whence $(M M^\top)_{e,f} = (N^\top N)_{e,f}$ over $\mathbb{F}_2$. For $u \in \ker N$, we have $M(M^\top u) = N^\top(N u) = 0$, so $\Theta(u) \in \mathcal{B}$. By connectivity of $O_6$, $\ker M^\top = \{0, \mathbf{1}_6\}$, while $N \mathbf{1}_6 = \mathbf{1}_4 \neq 0$, whence $\ker N \cap \ker M^\top = \{0\}$ and $\Theta$ is injective. Conversely, if $M^\top v \in \ker M$, then $N^\top N v = 0$; connectivity of $K_4$ implies $N v = \tau \mathbf{1}_4 = N(\tau \mathbf{1}_6)$ ($\tau \in \mathbb{F}_2$), and the vector $u = v \oplus \tau \mathbf{1}_6 \in \ker N$ gives $\Theta(u) = M^\top v$. The weights `0^{(1)}, 6^{(4)}, 8^{(3)}` and linearity of the half-weight follow from the orthogonality of cycles and cuts over $\mathbb{F}_2$. $\square$

> **Cross-volume spine (`Chapters I.1 → I.2 → I.6 → I.9`): the `8 = 4 + 4` face splitting of the octahedron $O_6$ as slice holonomy and the nerve of the cover of $S_4$.**  
> The six vertices of the octahedron $O_6$ are the six two-element slices $X_0, X_1, C_0, C_1, B_0, B_1$ of the questions $q_x, q_c, \beta$ on $S_4$, and the `12` edges of $O_6$ join pairs of slices having a one-point intersection $|\Omega_i \cap \Omega_j| = 1$. Thus $O_6$ is the **1-skeleton of the nerve $\mathcal{N}$ of the cover of $S_4$ by the six two-element slices** (`Chapter I.9, §I.9.4`). Under this reading, the `4` vertex stars of $H_R$-type from Theorem `I.6.1(1)` are precisely the `4` triples of slices sharing a common state point $(x_0, c_0) \in S_4$ and having trivial holonomy $\kappa = x_0 \oplus c_0 \oplus b_0 = 0$ ($H_3 = \mathrm{id}_{\Omega_3}$ in `Theorem I.2.4`), which form the **filled 2-simplices** of the nerve $\mathcal{N}$; whereas the `4` cyclic triangles of $H_F$-type are precisely the `4` triples of slices with empty triple intersection and non-trivial holonomy $\kappa = 1$ ($H_3 = \mathrm{swap}_{\Omega_3}$), which form the **four hollow triangular faces** of the nerve $\mathcal{N}$ and generate in `Theorem I.9.3` the cohomological gluing obstruction $\dim_{\mathbb{F}_2} H^1(\mathcal{N}; \mathbb{F}_2) = 3$.

Let us note alongside three instructive distinctions on small finite carriers. On the cell $S_4 = \mathbb{F}_2^2$, the Zhegalkin polynomials $k \oplus a x \oplus d c \oplus e x c$ with $e = 0$ single out the `8` affine questions $W_{\mathrm{aff}}$, preserved under pullback by exactly the **`64` affine maps** $\mathrm{Aff}(\mathbb{F}_2^2)$ (and by all `24` bijections on $A_6$), where $\mathrm{Aff}(\mathbb{F}_2^2)$ does not coincide with the `64` maps $D_2 = \mathcal{D}(\{O_0, O_1\})$ of Theorem `I.3.3` ($|\mathrm{Aff}(\mathbb{F}_2^2) \cap D_2| = 32$). On the eight divisors $D(30)$ of $30 = 2\cdot 3\cdot 5$, the undirected covering graph is isomorphic to the cube $Q_3$ with an automorphism group of order **`48`**, the directed divisibility poset has automorphism group $\mathfrak{S}_3$ of order **`6`**, and adjoining the complement involution $d \mapsto 30/d$ extends it to $\mathfrak{S}_3 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ of order **`12`**. Finally, on the Gaussian integer lattice $\mathbb{Z}[i]$, the orbits of the Klein four-group of reflections $V_4 = \{z, -z, \bar{z}, -\bar{z}\}$ on the points $(2+i,\, 1+i,\, i,\, 0)$ have cardinalities **`4, 4, 2, 1`**, whereas the orbits of the full dihedral group of the square $D_4$ (including the rotations $\pm i z$) have cardinalities **`8, 4, 4, 1`**.

### (b) Finite Fields $\mathbb{F}_3$ and $\mathbb{F}_4$: Directed Ray, Multiplication Without Zero Divisors, and Three Non-Isomorphic Groups of Order `48`

**What question motivates this step.** Over $\mathbb{F}_2$ one cannot distinguish the forward direction of a ray from its opposite ($-x = x$), while on four states the familiar residue ring $\mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ has the zero divisor $2 \cdot 2 \equiv 0 \pmod 4$. To separate opposite signs $+1 \neq -1$ over a minimal field and to construct invertible division on four elements, we pass to the finite fields $\mathbb{F}_3$ and $\mathbb{F}_4$.

In the **four-element field** $\mathbb{F}_4 = \mathbb{F}_2[\omega] / (\omega^2 + \omega + 1) = \{0, 1, \omega, \omega^2\}$, the additive group is isomorphic to $\mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ ($x + x = 0$, so $\mathbb{F}_4 \not\cong \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z}$ already additively), while the non-zero elements form the cyclic group $\mathbb{F}_4^\times = \{1, \omega, \omega^2\}$ of order `3` with multiplication $\omega \cdot \omega = \omega + 1$, $\omega \cdot \omega^2 = 1$ and no zero divisors.

Over the field $\mathbb{F}_3 = \{0, +1, -1\}$, the affine plane $\mathrm{AG}(2,3) = \mathbb{F}_3^2$ contains **`9` points** and **`12` lines** of `3` points each (`4` parallel pencils of `3` lines each; note that the open grid $P_3 \square P_3$ on the same `9` nodes has `12` adjacency edges, the two-dimensional torus $K_3 \square K_3$ has `18` edges, and the `12` affine lines of $\mathrm{AG}(2,3)$ are 3-element blocks of an incidence geometry). The punctured plane $\mathbb{F}_3^2 \setminus \{(0,0)\}$ consists of **`8` non-zero vectors** on which the quaternion subgroup $Q_8 \triangleleft \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{F}_3)$ ($|\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{F}_3)| = 24$) acts freely and transitively, fibering two-to-one ($v \sim -v$) over the `4` points of the projective line $\mathrm{PG}(1,3)$, while the projective closure $\mathrm{PG}(2,3)$ contains **`13` one-dimensional points and `13` projective lines** with `4` points on each. In the three-dimensional ternary cube $\{-1,0,+1\}^3$, the **`27` vectors** correspond to the `27` non-empty faces of the binary cube $Q_3$ (**`28`** elements when the empty face $\varnothing$ is included), the Manhattan ball $|x_1|+|x_2|+|x_3| \le 2$ contains **`19` points**, and quotienting the `26` non-zero vectors by the sign $\pm 1$ once again recovers the `13` points of $\mathrm{PG}(2,3)$.

> **Lemma I.6.1 (Triple anti-collision of finite groups of order `48`: `19 ≠ 13 ≠ 1` involutions).**  
> *Classical foundation.* The hyperoctahedral group $W(B_3)$ of order $48$, the general linear group $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_3)$ of order $48$, and the binary octahedral group $2O \subset \mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{F}_9)$—classical finite group theory. *Contribution of this book:* Separating three non-isomorphic groups of the same cardinality `48` purely by the operational invariant of order-`2` element counts (`19`, `13`, and the unique central involution `1` in $2O$).
> The following three finite groups have the same order `48` but are pairwise non-isomorphic:
> 1. The cube automorphism group $W(B_3) = \mathrm{O}_3(\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathfrak{S}_4 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ contains exactly **`19` non-identity involutions** ($g^2 = e,\, g \neq e$).
> 2. The general linear group $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_3)$ ($|\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_3)| = (9-1)(9-3) = 48$) contains exactly **`13` non-identity involutions** (`1` central matrix $-I_2$ and `12` reflections with $\det = -1$ and $\mathrm{tr} = 0$).
> 3. The binary octahedral group $2O$ of order `48` (realized inside $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{F}_9)$ where $\mathbb{F}_9 = \mathbb{F}_3[\iota]/(\iota^2 + 1)$) contains **exactly `1` non-identity involution** ($-I_2$).

*Proof.* In $\mathfrak{S}_4$ the equation $\pi^2 = \mathrm{id}$ has $1 + 6 + 3 = 10$ solutions, whence in $\mathfrak{S}_4 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ there are $10 \times 2 = 20$ solutions, i.e., `19` non-identity involutions. In $\mathrm{GL}_2(\mathbb{F}_3)$, $A^2 = I_2$ implies that the minimal polynomial divides $t^2 - 1 = (t-1)(t+1)$, giving either $A = -I_2$ (`1`) or a matrix with eigenvalues $(+1, -1)$ determined by an ordered pair of distinct lines among the `4` points of $\mathrm{PG}(1,3)$ ($4 \cdot 3 = 12$, totaling $1 + 12 = 13$). In $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{F}_9)$, the added condition $\det A = +1$ rules out eigenvalues $(+1, -1)$ (since $-1 \neq +1$ in characteristic `3`), leaving $-I_2$ as the sole non-identity involution. $\square$

### (c) Closing $A_6$ into the Fano Plane $\mathrm{PG}(2,2)$, Symplectic Carving of the Petersen Graph $KG(5,2)$, and the Klein Quadric $Q^+(5,2)$

**What question motivates this step.** The six active questions $A_6 = \mathbb{F}_2^3 \setminus \{000, 111\}$ are not closed under adding disjoint pairs ($u \oplus \bar{u} = 111 \notin A_6$). Adjoining the seventh non-zero point $p_7 = 111$ closes the carrier into the projective Fano plane $\mathrm{PG}(2,2)$, and passing to the four-dimensional space $\mathbb{F}_2^4 \setminus \{0\}$ equipped with a non-degenerate symplectic form $\omega$ carves out the Petersen graph $KG(5,2)$ from its `15` points and classifies the `35` projective lines of $\mathrm{PG}(3,2)$, which under the Plücker embedding become the `35` points of the Klein quadric $Q^+(5,2)$ (on which lie `105` totally singular lines).

**1. Coordinated labeling of the Fano plane $\mathrm{PG}(2,2)$, the Levi–Heawood bipartite graph, and the action of the quarter-turn $\tau R$.** Fix a single numbering of the seven points $P_F = \{p_1, \dots, p_7\} = \mathbb{F}_2^3 \setminus \{000\}$ and seven lines $\mathcal{L}_F = \{L_1, \dots, L_7\}$, which below in subsection `(d)` defines the incidence matrix $\delta_7$:
$$p_1 = (1,0,0),\quad p_2 = (0,1,0),\quad p_3 = (1,1,0),\quad p_4 = (0,0,1),\quad p_5 = (1,0,1),\quad p_6 = (0,1,1),\quad p_7 = (1,1,1),$$
and each line $L_r = \{p_k \in P_F \mid \langle \ell_r, p_k \rangle = 0\}$ with normal $\ell_r = p_r$ ($r = 1,\dots,7$) consists of three distinct points summing to $000$:
$$L_1 = \{p_2, p_4, p_6\},\quad L_2 = \{p_1, p_4, p_5\},\quad L_3 = \{p_3, p_4, p_7\},\quad L_4 = \{p_1, p_2, p_3\},$$
$$L_5 = \{p_2, p_5, p_7\},\quad L_6 = \{p_1, p_6, p_7\},\quad L_7 = \{p_3, p_5, p_6\}.$$

> **Theorem I.6.4 (Square roots of the complement involution on `6` and `8` labels, and coloring orbits on $\mathbb{F}_2^7$).**
