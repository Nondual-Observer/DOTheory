## §I.2.1. A Tangible Scene: A Two-Door Airlock, a Locked Lever, and the Exact Witness of Lost Descent `01 / 10`

Imagine an airlock with two doors in series: a left door $x \in \{0, 1\}$ and a right door $y \in \{0, 1\}$ (`0` — closed, `1` — open). On the cover of the airlock only a single mismatch (parity) indicator is lit:
$$q(x, y) \;=\; x \oplus y \;\in\; \{0, 1\},$$
partitioning the four states $S_4 = \{00, 01, 10, 11\}$ into two fibers of two points each:
$$O_0 = \{00, 11\} \quad (\text{doors in the same position, } q = 0), \qquad O_1 = \{01, 10\} \quad (\text{doors in different positions, } q = 1).$$
Outside, two levers are brought out:
1. **The bypass lever `T` ("open the right door if the left door is open"):** the everywhere-defined rule
   $$T(x, y) \;=\; (x,\; x \lor y).$$
2. **The interlocked purge lever `T_lock`:** maps `00 ↦ 00`, `01 ↦ 01`, `10 ↦ 10`, but when both doors are open (`11`) a safety catch engages—the lever mechanically refuses to turn, returning the **action inadmissibility marker** $\bot_{\mathcal{A}} \notin S_4$ ($\mathrm{Dom}(T_{\mathrm{lock}}) = \{00, 01, 10\}$).

Let us try to predict the future reading of the indicator $q$ knowing only its current answer.
- Look first at the fiber of matched doors $O_0 = \{00, 11\}$ under the bypass lever $T(x,y) = (x, x \lor y)$. In state `00` both doors are closed: $T(0,0) = (0,0)$, and the indicator shows $q(0,0) = 0$. In state `11` both doors are already open: $T(1,1) = (1,1)$, and the indicator **again shows $0$**. Contrary to hasty intuition, **the pair `00 / 11` remains indistinguishable after the step $T$** ($q(T(00)) = 0 = q(T(11))$).
- Where does predictability from the single bit $q$ actually break down? On the odd fiber **$O_1 = \{01, 10\}$**. Before pulling the lever, both states yield the same answer $q(0,1) = q(1,0) = 1$. Now pull $T$:
  $$T(0, 1) = (0, 0 \lor 1) = (0, 1) \implies q(T(0,1)) = 1, \qquad T(1, 0) = (1, 1 \lor 0) = (1, 1) \implies q(T(1,0)) = 0!$$
  The pair **`01 / 10`** (rather than `00 / 11`) provides the exact witness that the pullback question $(T^*q)(x,y) = q(T(x,y)) = x \oplus (x \lor y) = \bar{x} \land y$ ceases to be constant on the fiber $O_1$ and requires going beyond the single bit $q$.
- Under the interlocked lever $T_{\mathrm{lock}}$, by contrast, it is the even fiber $O_0 = \{00, 11\}$ that splits—and it does so without the slightest change of states on its domain of definition: at `00` the action is admissible, whereas at `11` in the same fiber it is blocked ($\bot_{\mathcal{A}}$). Action failure $\bot_{\mathcal{A}}$ and reading failure $\bot_q$ are distinct typed outcomes outside $\{0, 1\}$ that must never be recorded as the number `0`.

```text
   (a) Bypass lever T(x,y) = (x, x ∨ y)                  (b) Partial lever T_lock and hidden group V_4
       under parity window q(x,y) = x ⊕ y:                   on the same fibers O_0 = {00,11}, O_1 = {01,10}:

       Fiber O_0 (q=0):  00 ↺          11 ↺                 00 ↺               11 ──X──> ⊥_A (interlocked!)
       [NOT a witness!]  q(T(00))=0 == q(T(11))=0           Dom(T_lock)={00,01,10} NOT saturated by Eq(q)!

       Fiber O_1 (q=1):  01 ↺          10 ───T───> 11       Four steps V_4 preserving partition {O_0,O_1} as a fiber pair:
       [WITNESS!]        q(T(01))=1 != q(T(10))=0           id,  σ_π(x,c)=(1⊕x,1⊕c) [preserve each fiber],
                                                            K_x(x,c)=(1⊕x,c), K_c(x,c)=(x,1⊕c) [swap O_0↔O_1]
```

**Reader's caption to `Fig. I.2.1`:** behavior of the two levers on the four-element two-door square $S_4 = \{00, 01, 10, 11\}$ under the parity window $q(x,y) = x \oplus y$. In panel (a), the functional graph of the everywhere-defined step $T(x,y) = (x, x \lor y)$ has three self-loops (`00↺, 01↺, 11↺`) and one transition `10 → 11`, so the sole witness of future-answer splitting is the odd pair `(01, 10)` ($q(T(01)) = 1 \neq 0 = q(T(10))$). In panel (b), the partial lever $T_{\mathrm{lock}}$ sends the point `11` to the interlock marker $\bot_{\mathcal{A}}$, making the even pair `(00, 11)` the witness of unsaturated admissibility, whereas the bijections of the square that preserve the partition $\{O_0, O_1\}$ as a pair of fibers form the dihedral group $D_4$ of order `8` with its commutative bitwise-shift subgroup $V_4 = \{\mathrm{id}, \sigma_\pi, K_x, K_c\}$ of order `4`.

Let us record three methodological safeguards around this scene right away:
1. **Mandatory declaration of the polarity of $\beta$ before computing $T^*\beta$.** For the reversible mixing step $T_{\mathrm{mix}}(x, c) = (x \oplus c, c)$ under the canonical mismatch polarity $\beta(x,c) = x \oplus c$, the pullback gives $(T_{\mathrm{mix}}^*\beta)(x,c) = (x \oplus c) \oplus c = x$; if one locally replaces the question by the opposite match polarity $\widetilde{\beta}(x,c) = 1 \oplus x \oplus c$, then $(T_{\mathrm{mix}}^*\widetilde{\beta})(x,c) = 1 \oplus x$. One cannot transfer a formula without declaring the chosen polarity.
2. **Asymmetry or non-equivariance of a step does NOT by itself break descent.** The constant reset $T_{\mathrm{const}}(x,y) = (1,0)$ does not commute with the door swap $\sigma(x,y) = (y,x)$ ($\sigma T_{\mathrm{const}} \equiv (0,1) \neq (1,0) \equiv T_{\mathrm{const}}\sigma$), yet it descends to the constant parity answer $\widehat{T}_{\mathrm{const}}(b) = 1$ (unlike reset to $(0,0)$, which is $\sigma$-invariant); and the flip $K_c(x,c) = (x, 1 \oplus c)$ descends to $b \mapsto 1 \oplus b$. To lose descent, one must exhibit a pair of equivalent states with inequivalent images (such as `01 / 10` for $T(x,y)=(x,x\lor y)$).
3. **The order of a symmetry group is not the period of an individual action, and the graph of elementary steps is not deduced from composition alone.** The group $D_4$ of all bijections of $S_4$ preserving the partition $\{O_0, O_1\}$ has order $|D_4| = 8$, yet the period of every individual transformation $g \in D_4$ divides `4` and equals `1`, `2`, or `4` (no single element traverses an `8`-step cycle). When a graph of admissible elementary steps and their inverses is fixed on a finite carrier, the choice of generating edges belongs to the operational contract rather than following from the group multiplication table alone.

## §I.2.2. Partial Actions ($\mathbf{Par}$), the Category of Relations ($\mathbf{Rel}$), Galois Connections, and Composable Histories

Before turning to multi-step compositions, let us fix an explicit typing convention for the operational contract from Chapter `I.1` that rules out the ambiguity of "bare button names."

**Typing of named actions in the six-tuple contract $\Gamma$.** In the six-component notation for an operational contract $\Gamma = (X, \mathcal{Q}, \mathcal{A}, \{\mathrm{Dom}(T_a)\}_{a \in \mathcal{A}}, \Pi, \Phi)$, every element $a \in \mathcal{A}$ is a **typed named action** $a = (\mathrm{name}_a,\, T_a : \mathrm{Dom}(T_a) \to X)$, i.e., it unambiguously carries its partial transition map $T_a$ (while the fourth entry explicitly highlights the compatible family of domains of admissibility $\{\mathrm{Dom}(T_a)\}_{a \in \mathcal{A}}$). Equality of two contracts includes equality of the partial maps $T_a$ themselves. *(If $\mathcal{A}$ were understood merely as a set of bare button labels without the maps $T_a$, then on $X = \{0, 1\}$ with a single button $\mathrm{name}_a$, full admissibility $\mathrm{Dom}(T_a) = X$, and question $q(x) = x$, two distinct dynamics $T_a = \mathrm{id}_X$ and $T_a(x) = 1 - x$ would share identical bare fields while producing opposite future answers $q(T_a(0)) = 0 \neq 1$.)*

Let us now formalize the transfer of partial questions and multi-valued transitions by strict definitions and theorems.

> **Definition I.2.1 (Partial maps, composition of admissibility domains, and pullback of a partial question).**  
> 1. A **partial map** $T : X \rightharpoonup Y$ is a pair $(D_T, T_0)$, where $D_T = \mathrm{Dom}(T) \subseteq X$ is the domain of admissibility and $T_0 : D_T \to Y$ is an ordinary map of sets (in totalized notation $T_\bot : X \to Y \sqcup \{\bot_{\mathcal{A}}\}$ we set $T_\bot(x) = \bot_{\mathcal{A}} \notin Y$ for $x \notin D_T$).
> 2. For $T : X \rightharpoonup Y$ and $U : Y \rightharpoonup Z$, the **composition of partial maps** $U \circ T : X \rightharpoonup Z$ has as its domain of admissibility the preimage
>    $$D_{U \circ T} \;=\; \{x \in D_T \mid T(x) \in D_U\} \;=\; T_0^{-1}(D_U) \subseteq D_T,$$
>    and acts by the rule $(U \circ T)(x) = U(T(x))$ for $x \in D_{U \circ T}$.
> 3. For a partial question $q : D_q \to V_q$ ($D_q = \mathrm{Dom}(q) \subseteq Y$), its **contravariant transfer (pullback)** along $T : X \rightharpoonup Y$ is the partial question $T^* q = q \circ T : D_{T^* q} \to V_q$ with domain $D_{T^* q} = \{x \in D_T \mid T(x) \in D_q\} \subseteq X$ and value $(T^* q)(x) = q(T(x))$; for a subset (event) $B \subseteq Y$ we set $T^*(B) = \{x \in D_T \mid T(x) \in B\}$.

Pay special attention to domains of definition when comparing "before and after a step": for distinct carriers $Y \neq X$, the original question $q$ lives on $Y$ whereas the pulled-back question $T^* q$ lives on $X$; a direct pointwise comparison of substantive answers $q(x)$ and $(q \circ T)(x)$ makes sense for a **partial endomorphism of one and the same carrier ($Y = X$)** and is legitimate strictly on their common domain of definition $D_q \cap D_{T^* q}$. One must never replace failure $\bot_q$ by an identity answer or declare a discrepancy outside the common domain to be a dynamical shift: for instance, on $X = \{0, 1, 2\}$ with $D_q = \{0, 1\}$, $D_T = \{0, 1, 2\}$, and $T(0) = T(1) = 2,\, T(2) = 0$, we have $D_{q \circ T} = \{2\}$ and $D_q \cap D_{q \circ T} = \varnothing$, so the discrepancy between the totalized tables is generated entirely by the disjointness of their domains.

> **Theorem I.2.1 (Composition laws in $\mathbf{Par}$, relative complement, and criterion for joint partial descent).**  
> *Classical foundation.* Laws of composition of partial maps are properties of the classical category $\mathbf{Par}$ (equivalent to pointed sets); relative complement of domains of definition is Boolean algebra of subsets. *Contribution of this book:* Contravariant transfer of readings $T^\sharp q$ along a partial step separating blocking $\bot_A \notin X$ from the false answer $0$, plus exact criterion of joint partial descent.
> 1. **The category $\mathbf{Par}$ and contravariance of transfer:** composition of partial maps is associative ($W \circ (U \circ T) = (W \circ U) \circ T$ with common domain $\{x \in D_T \mid T(x) \in D_U \land U(T(x)) \in D_W\}$) with identities $\mathrm{id}_X$ ($D_{\mathrm{id}_X} = X$), and pullback is contravariant:
>    $$(U \circ T)^* q \;=\; T^*(U^* q), \qquad\qquad \mathrm{id}_X^* q \;=\; q.$$
> 2. **Law of relative complementation inside the admissibility domain:** for any $T : X \rightharpoonup Y$ and any $B \subseteq Y$, the preimage operator $T^* : \mathcal{P}(Y) \to \mathcal{P}(X)$ preserves intersections and unions (defining a Boolean algebra homomorphism onto the restricted algebra $\mathcal{P}(D_T)$ with unit $D_T$), but when $D_T \subsetneq X$ it fails to preserve the unit and complement in all of $\mathcal{P}(X)$, mapping $Y \setminus B$ to the **relative complement inside $D_T$**:
>    $$T^*(Y \setminus B) \;=\; D_T \setminus T^*(B) \;\subsetneq\; X \setminus T^*(B) \quad (\text{whenever } D_T \subsetneq X).$$
> 3. **Criterion for joint partial descent:** let $\pi : X \twoheadrightarrow M$ be a surjective projection. A pair consisting of a partial question $q : D_q \to V_q$ on $X$ and a partial action $T : D_T \to X$ descends uniquely to a partial question $\widehat{q} : \widehat{D}_q \to V_q$ and a partial step $\widehat{T} : \widehat{D}_T \to M$ on $M$ satisfying $D_q = \pi^{-1}(\widehat{D}_q)$, $q = \widehat{q} \circ \pi|_{D_q}$, $D_T = \pi^{-1}(\widehat{D}_T)$, and $\pi \circ T = \widehat{T} \circ \pi|_{D_T}$ **if and only if** both domains $D_q, D_T$ are saturated with respect to $\mathrm{Eq}(\pi)$ ($x \sim_\pi x' \implies [x \in D_q \iff x' \in D_q] \land [x \in D_T \iff x' \in D_T]$), the question $q$ is constant on fibers of $\pi$ inside $D_q$, and for all $x, x' \in D_T$ the equality $\pi(x) = \pi(x')$ implies $\pi(T(x)) = \pi(T(x'))$. In this case descent commutes with composition: $\widehat{U \circ T} = \widehat{U} \circ \widehat{T}$ with saturated domain $D_{U\circ T} = \pi^{-1}(D_{\widehat{U}\circ\widehat{T}})$.

*Proof.* (1) The condition $x \in D_{(W\circ U)\circ T}$ means $x \in D_T$ and $T(x) \in D_{W\circ U} = \{y \in D_U \mid U(y) \in D_W\}$, which coincides with $x \in D_{U\circ T}$ and $(U\circ T)(x) \in D_W$, i.e., $x \in D_{W\circ(U\circ T)}$; on this common domain the values equal $W(U(T(x)))$. Substituting $W = q$ yields $(U\circ T)^* q = T^*(U^* q)$. (2) By definition, $x \in T^*(Y \setminus B) \iff (x \in D_T \land T(x) \notin B) \iff x \in D_T \setminus T^*(B)$; if there exists $x_0 \in X \setminus D_T$, then $x_0 \notin T^*(B)$, hence $x_0 \in X \setminus T^*(B)$ whereas $x_0 \notin D_T \setminus T^*(B)$. (3) Necessity of domain saturation and fiberwise compatibility follows from $D_q = \pi^{-1}(\widehat{D}_q)$, $D_T = \pi^{-1}(\widehat{D}_T)$, $q = \widehat{q}\circ\pi|_{D_q}$, and $\pi\circ T = \widehat{T}\circ\pi|_{D_T}$; conversely, under saturation we set $\widehat{D}_q = \pi(D_q)$, $\widehat{D}_T = \pi(D_T)$, $\widehat{q}(\pi(x)) = q(x)$, and $\widehat{T}(\pi(x)) = \pi(T(x))$. For $U, T$ with these properties, $x \in D_{U\circ T} \iff x \in \pi^{-1}(\widehat{D}_T) \land \pi(T(x)) \in \widehat{D}_U \iff \pi(x) \in D_{\widehat{U}\circ\widehat{T}}$. $\square$

Let us emphasize the strict separation between four different operations on a system: a bijective change of coordinates $X \xrightarrow{\sim} X'$ alters neither cardinality nor dynamics; a quotient $\pi : X \twoheadrightarrow M$ glues indistinguishable states; a carrier extension $X \hookrightarrow \widetilde{X}$ adds hidden memory; and realizability in a generator library requires expressibility of a step through allowed elementary actions—thus on two points, out of $(2+1)^2 = 9$ partial and $2^2 = 4$ total maps, the involution $\neg(x)=1\oplus x$ does not belong to the closure $\langle \{\mathrm{id}\} \rangle = \{\mathrm{id}\}$, while in the six-bit three-pair testbed (which we will analyze completely in Lemma `I.3.5` of the next chapter) adding each new named pair-flip $F_j$ expands precisely the family of admissible actions $\mathcal{A}$, lifting the required memory step by step along the ladder `4, 6, 8, 8`.

When a single step can send a state to several admissible successors, or when we invert a non-injective action, partial maps embed into the **category of binary relations $\mathbf{Rel}$**.

> **Theorem I.2.2 (Galois adjunction $\exists_R \dashv \forall_R$ in the category of relations $\mathbf{Rel}$, vacuous truth, and counterexamples to adjunction and intersection preservation).**  
> *Classical foundation.* The calculus of binary relations goes back to Peirce–Schröder–Tarski relation algebra (category $\mathbf{Rel}$), and the adjunction $\exists_R \dashv \forall_R$ is a classical Galois connection (Lawvere, 1969). *Contribution of this book:* Strict typing of modal preimages $\exists_R, \forall_R$ by reading direction and minimal counterexamples on dead-end states showing where replacing $\forall_R$ by $R^{-1}$ breaks the adjunction.
> For any binary relation $R \subseteq X \times Y$, define the directionally typed operators:
> - **direct existential image** $\exists_R : \mathcal{P}(X) \to \mathcal{P}(Y)$, $\exists_R(A) = \{y \in Y \mid \exists x \in A,\; (x, y) \in R\}$;
> - **pullback universal preimage** $\forall_R : \mathcal{P}(Y) \to \mathcal{P}(X)$, $\forall_R(B) = \{x \in X \mid \forall y \in Y,\; (x, y) \in R \implies y \in B\}$;
> - **converse (transposed) relation** $R^\dagger = \{(y, x) \in Y \times X \mid (x, y) \in R\} \subseteq Y \times X$.  
> Then the following four statements hold:
> 1. **Exact Galois adjunction:** for all subsets $A \subseteq X$ and $B \subseteq Y$,
>    $$\exists_R(A) \subseteq B \;\Longleftrightarrow\; A \subseteq \forall_R(B),$$
>    where $\exists_R$ preserves arbitrary unions ($\exists_R(\varnothing) = \varnothing$) and $\forall_R$ preserves arbitrary intersections ($\forall_R(Y) = X$).
> 2. **Vacuous truth on dead-end states and failure of adjunction for $\forall_R^{\neq\varnothing}$:** if the successor slice $R(x) = \{y \in Y \mid (x,y) \in R\}$ is empty ($R(x) = \varnothing$), then $x \in \forall_R(B)$ for **every** $B \subseteq Y$ ($\forall_R(\varnothing) = \{x \in X \mid R(x) = \varnothing\}$). If one adds an existential exit requirement and considers $\forall_R^{\neq\varnothing}(B) := \{x \in X \mid R(x) \neq \varnothing \land R(x) \subseteq B\}$, then on two-point carriers $X = Y = \{0, 1\}$ the operator changes on **`28` out of `64`** pairs $(R, B)$ and **breaks the Galois equivalence $\exists_R(A) \subseteq B \iff A \subseteq \forall_R^{\neq\varnothing}(B)$ on exactly `46` out of `256` triples $(R, A, B)$** (for instance, when $R = \varnothing$, $A = \{0\}$, $B = \varnothing$, the left-hand side $\exists_\varnothing(\{0\}) = \varnothing \subseteq \varnothing$ is true, whereas the right-hand side $\{0\} \subseteq \forall_\varnothing^{\neq\varnothing}(\varnothing) = \varnothing$ is false).
> 3. **De Morgan duality between $\exists_{R^\dagger}$ and $\forall_R$:** the relational preimage $\exists_{R^\dagger}(B) = \{x \in X \mid \exists y \in B,\; (x, y) \in R\}$ is linked to $\forall_R$ by the exact identity $\forall_R(B) = X \setminus \exists_{R^\dagger}(Y \setminus B)$.
> 4. **Two mandatory failures of intersection preservation:** first, for the merging relation $R_{\mathrm{merge}} = \{(x_1, y), (x_2, y)\} \subseteq \{x_1, x_2\} \times \{y\}$, the direct existential image fails to preserve intersections: $\exists_{R_{\mathrm{merge}}}(\{x_1\} \cap \{x_2\}) = \varnothing \neq \{y\} = \exists_{R_{\mathrm{merge}}}(\{x_1\}) \cap \exists_{R_{\mathrm{merge}}}(\{x_2\})$; second, the converse $R^\dagger$ need not be an inverse: for an arbitrary relation even the equality $R^\dagger \circ R = \Delta_X$ does not guarantee single-valuedness (on $X=\{x\}, Y=\{0,1\}$ the multi-valued $R=\{(x,0),(x,1)\}$ gives $R^\dagger \circ R = \Delta_X$; two-sided invertibility in $\mathbf{Rel}$ requires both $R^\dagger \circ R = \Delta_X$ and $R \circ R^\dagger = \Delta_Y$, i.e., the graph of a bijection), and for the branching relation $R_{\mathrm{branch}} = \{(1, 1), (1, 2)\}$ on $\{1, 2\}$ the preimage $\exists_{R_{\mathrm{branch}}^\dagger}$ loses intersections:
>    $$\exists_{R_{\mathrm{branch}}^\dagger}(\{1\}) \cap \exists_{R_{\mathrm{branch}}^\dagger}(\{2\}) \;=\; \{1\} \cap \{1\} \;=\; \{1\} \;\neq\; \varnothing \;=\; \exists_{R_{\mathrm{branch}}^\dagger}(\{1\} \cap \{2\}).$$

*Proof.* (1) The condition $\exists_R(A) \subseteq B$ is equivalent to $\forall x \in X\,\forall y \in Y,\; (x \in A \land (x,y) \in R) \implies y \in B$, which by permuting premises yields $A \subseteq \forall_R(B)$. (2) When $R(x) = \varnothing$, the implication $(x,y) \in R \implies y \in B$ holds vacuously for all $y \in Y$, whereas in $\forall_R^{\neq\varnothing}(B)$ all points with an empty slice are forcibly removed; the exact counts `28 / 64` and `46 / 256` on $|X|=|Y|=2$ are verified by exhaustive enumeration of all pairs and triples. (3) The negation of $\forall y\, ((x,y) \in R \implies y \in B)$ is $\exists y \in Y \setminus B,\; (x,y) \in R$. (4) Direct substitution of $R_{\mathrm{merge}}$ and $R_{\mathrm{branch}}$ gives the stated set inequalities. $\square$

Let us also show here how, in a model of composable histories, three relative parity bits on the segments of a history satisfy an additive compatibility law, yet once the step range is $\ge 2$ they cease to exhaust the history itself.

> **Lemma I.2.1 (Composable three-point histories and the reconstruction defect from three parity bits).**  
> *Classical foundation.* Composition of binary relations over the Boolean semiring vs. integer multiplication of path matrices—standard distinction between categories $\mathbf{Rel}$ and $\mathbf{Mat}_{\mathbb{Z}}$. *Contribution of this book:* Explicit finite witness of path-multiplicity erasure (`2 → 1`) under relational composition that forgets an intermediate seam.
> Let finite histories across three consecutive ticks $m < n < p$ be given by triples $(c, i, j)$, where $c \in C = \{0, 1\}$ is the reference label at the initial instant $m$, and $i \in \{0, \dots, d_1\}$, $j \in \{0, \dots, d_2\}$ are non-negative increments on the segments $m \to n$ and $n \to p$ (total increment $k = i + j$ on $m \to p$). Assign to each history the triple of relative parity bits on the faces $(n,p)$, $(m,p)$, and $(m,n)$:
> $$q_{\triangle}(c, i, j) \;=\; \bigl(j \bmod 2,\;\; (i + j) \bmod 2,\;\; i \bmod 2\bigr) \;\in\; \{0, 1\}^3.$$
> Then:
> 1. For any $d_1, d_2 \ge 1$, the image of $q_\triangle$ coincides with the 4-element even shell $R_0 = \{(x, y, z) \in \{0,1\}^3 \mid x \oplus y \oplus z = 0\}$ ($|R_0| = 4$), independent of $c$.
> 2. For unit steps $d_1 = d_2 = 1$ ($2 \times 2 \times 2 = 8$ histories), the pair $(c, q_\triangle(c,i,j))$ uniquely reconstructs the history (`8` histories bijectively decompose into `4` fibers of size `2`), while forgetting the reference $c$ yields the compression `8 → 4`.
> 3. Already for $d_1 = d_2 = 2$, there are $2 \times 3 \times 3 = \mathbf{18}$ histories and $3 \times 3 = \mathbf{9}$ increment pairs $(i, j)$, whereas $q_\triangle$ still distinguishes only **`4`** even classes in $R_0$ with non-uniform fiber sizes **`8, 4, 4, 2`** ($8 + 4 + 4 + 2 = 18$; for fixed $c$, fibers of size `4, 2, 2, 1` for even, mixed, and odd increments). Meanwhile, the integer carry bit between the least significant residues $\mathrm{carry}(i \bmod 2,\, j \bmod 2) = \frac{(i \bmod 2) + (j \bmod 2) - ((i+j) \bmod 2)}{2} \in \{0, 1\}$ **is computable from the retained parity triple $q_\triangle$ itself** (taking the value `1` precisely on the class $(1, 0, 1)$ of odd steps $i \equiv j \equiv 1 \pmod 2$), whereas inside the multi-point fibers of $q_\triangle$ what is irreversibly lost is the pair of **higher binary quotients of the increments** $\bigl(\lfloor i/2 \rfloor,\, \lfloor j/2 \rfloor\bigr)$: thus the histories with $(i, j) = (0, 0)$ and $(i, j) = (2, 0)$ share the same parity triple $(0, 0, 0)$ and the same low-bit carry `0`, but have different integer increments.

*Proof.* The congruence $(j \bmod 2) \oplus ((i+j) \bmod 2) \oplus (i \bmod 2) = 0$ holds identically in $\mathbb{F}_2$, and already for $i, j \in \{0, 1\}$ all `4` solutions $(0,0,0), (1,1,0), (0,1,1), (1,0,1) \in R_0$ are attained. When $d_1 = d_2 = 2$, the set $\{0, 1, 2\}$ contains `2` even numbers (`0, 2`) and `1` odd number (`1`), so the number of pairs $(i, j)$ with parities `(0,0), (0,1), (1,0), (1,1)` equals $2\cdot 2 = 4$, $2\cdot 1 = 2$, $1\cdot 2 = 2$, and $1\cdot 1 = 1$ (sum `9`), and multiplying by $|C| = 2$ gives fiber sizes `8, 4, 4, 2` (sum `18`). $\square$

## §I.2.3. Lawvere's Boolean Hyperdoctrine $\exists_\pi \dashv \pi^* \dashv \forall_\pi$, the Shadow Boundary $\partial_\pi(S)$, and Failure of Beck–Chevalley Outside Pullback Squares

Let us now join a total quotient window $\pi : E \to B$ with the logic of fiberwise predicates: Lawvere's categorical logic provides the mathematical language for those three boundary reading statuses ("guaranteed," "boundary shadow," "excluded") that we met in the drawing of the cylinder, sphere, cone, and cube in the Introduction.

> **Theorem I.2.3 (Lawvere's adjoint triad $\exists_\pi \dashv \pi^* \dashv \forall_\pi$, the shadow boundary $\partial_\pi(S)$, and a counterexample to Beck–Chevalley outside a pullback square).**  
> *Classical foundation.* The adjoint triad $\exists_\pi \dashv \pi^* \dashv \forall_\pi$ and the Beck–Chevalley base-change condition along a pullback square—classical categorical logic (Lawvere, 1969; Bell, 1988). *Contribution of this book:* Operational definition of the shadow boundary $\partial_\pi(S) = (\exists_\pi S \cap \mathrm{im}\,\pi) \setminus \forall_\pi S$, saturation criterion $S = \pi^{-1}(\pi(S))$ (`4/16` on $S_4$), and counterexamples when surjectivity of $\pi$ or the pullback condition is dropped.
> Let $\pi : E \to B$ be an arbitrary everywhere-defined map of sets, $\pi^*(U) = \pi^{-1}(U) \subseteq E$ the inverse-image Boolean algebra homomorphism, $\exists_\pi(S) = \{\pi(e) \mid e \in S\} \subseteq B$ the direct image, and $\forall_\pi(S) = \{b \in B \mid \pi^{-1}(\{b\}) \subseteq S\} \subseteq B$ the universal image along fibers of $\pi$. Define the **shadow boundary** of a predicate $S \subseteq E$ along the window $\pi$ by
> $$\partial_\pi(S) \;:=\; \exists_\pi(S) \setminus \forall_\pi(S) \;=\; \bigl\{b \in B \;\big|\; \pi^{-1}(\{b\}) \cap S \neq \varnothing \;\land\; \pi^{-1}(\{b\}) \setminus S \neq \varnothing\bigr\}.$$
> Then:
> 1. **Triad of adjunctions:** for all $S \subseteq E$ and $U \subseteq B$,
>    $$\exists_\pi(S) \subseteq U \;\Longleftrightarrow\; S \subseteq \pi^*(U), \qquad\qquad \pi^*(U) \subseteq S \;\Longleftrightarrow\; U \subseteq \forall_\pi(S),$$
>    where for a fixed $S \subseteq E$ the inclusion $\forall_\pi(S) \subseteq \exists_\pi(S)$ is equivalent to $\forall_\pi(S) \subseteq \mathrm{im}(\pi)$, while validity of $\forall_\pi(S) \subseteq \exists_\pi(S)$ **for all** $S \subseteq E$ is equivalent to surjectivity of $\pi$ onto all of $B$ (already witnessed at $S = \varnothing$, where $\forall_\pi(\varnothing) = B \setminus \mathrm{im}(\pi)$ and $\exists_\pi(\varnothing) = \varnothing$; likewise for $\pi : \{0\} \to \{0,1\}$ with $\pi(0)=0$ and $S=\{0\}$, the empty fiber $\pi^{-1}(\{1\}) = \varnothing \subseteq S$ yields $\forall_\pi(S) = \{0,1\} \nsubseteq \{0\} = \exists_\pi(S)$).
> 2. **Exact criterion for descent of a predicate across a window:** when $\pi : E \twoheadrightarrow B$ is surjective, the shadow boundary $\partial_\pi(S)$ is empty **if and only if** the predicate $S$ descends losslessly to the base $B$, i.e., there exists (and is then unique) a subset $U \subseteq B$ such that $S = \pi^*(U)$ (in which case $U = \exists_\pi(S) = \forall_\pi(S)$). On the parity window $\pi : S_4 \twoheadrightarrow \{0,1\}$, out of the `16` subsets $S \subseteq S_4$ exactly **`4`** have $\partial_\pi(S) = \varnothing$ ($\varnothing, O_0, O_1, S_4$), while the remaining **`12`** have a non-empty shadow boundary.
> 3. **Beck–Chevalley law for pullback squares and its failure outside them:** if a commutative square of maps $f \circ g' = g \circ f'$ ($A' \xrightarrow{g'} A \xrightarrow{f} B$ and $A' \xrightarrow{f'} B' \xrightarrow{g} B$) is a **pullback (Cartesian) square** in $\mathbf{Set}$, then for every $S \subseteq A$ the Beck–Chevalley equality holds:
>    $$g^*\bigl(\exists_f(S)\bigr) \;=\; \exists_{f'}\bigl(g'^*(S)\bigr).$$
>    Without the pullback condition, commutativity of the square alone guarantees only the inclusion $\exists_{f'}(g'^*(S)) \subseteq g^*(\exists_f(S))$ and **does not guarantee equality** (of course, a single counterexample refutes the sufficiency of commutativity without claiming that *every* non-Cartesian square must violate Beck–Chevalley): for $A' = \varnothing$, $A = \{a\}$, $B' = \{b'\}$, $B = \{b\}$ with the unique maps $f(a) = b$, $g(b') = b$ and the empty maps $f' : \varnothing \to \{b'\}$, $g' : \varnothing \to \{a\}$, the square commutes ($f \circ g' = \varnothing_{A'\to B} = g \circ f'$), yet $A \times_B B' = \{(a, b')\} \neq \varnothing = A'$, and for $S = \{a\} \subseteq A$ we obtain
>    $$g^*\bigl(\exists_f(\{a\})\bigr) \;=\; g^*(\{b\}) \;=\; \{b'\} \;\neq\; \varnothing \;=\; \exists_{f'}(\varnothing) \;=\; \exists_{f'}\bigl(g'^*(\{a\})\bigr).$$

*Proof.* (1) $\exists_\pi(S) \subseteq U \iff (\forall e \in S,\; \pi(e) \in U) \iff S \subseteq \pi^{-1}(U) = \pi^*(U)$; similarly, $\pi^{-1}(U) \subseteq S \iff (\forall b \in U,\; \pi^{-1}(\{b\}) \subseteq S) \iff U \subseteq \forall_\pi(S)$. When $\pi$ is surjective, every fiber $\pi^{-1}(\{b\})$ is non-empty, so $\pi^{-1}(\{b\}) \subseteq S \implies \pi^{-1}(\{b\}) \cap S \neq \varnothing \implies b \in \exists_\pi(S)$. (2) When $\pi$ is surjective, $\partial_\pi(S) = \varnothing$ means that no fiber $\pi^{-1}(\{b\})$ is cut in two by $S$: each fiber either lies wholly in $S$ or is disjoint from $S$, which is equivalent to $S = \pi^{-1}(\exists_\pi(S)) = \pi^*(U)$. (3) If $A' \cong A \times_B B'$, then $b' \in g^*(\exists_f(S)) \iff \exists a \in S,\; f(a) = g(b') \iff \exists z \in A',\; g'(z) \in S \land f'(z) = b' \iff b' \in \exists_{f'}(g'^*(S))$. In the counterexample with $A' = \varnothing$, the left-hand side equals $g^{-1}(\{b\}) = \{b'\}$ while the right-hand side is the image of the empty set $\varnothing$. $\square$

## §I.2.4. The Complete Lattice of `16` Predicates on $S_4$, Three Distinct "NOT" Operations, and the Stratification of Distinction Logic `L.0 / L.1`

Let us now unfold the full Boolean algebra of all $2^4 = 16$ predicates $\mathcal{P}(S_4) \cong \mathbb{F}_2^{S_4}$ and the three-level architecture of the logic of observable distinction `L.0 / L.1 / L.2`, keeping in mind that the `16` general predicates, the `8` affine tables, the `7` projective points of the Fano plane (which will appear in Chapter `I.6`), and the `6` oriented balanced questions are **distinct explicitly defined mathematical objects**, not interchangeable numbers.

The polynomial calculus of Boolean functions was developed by I. I. Zhegalkin; his 1927 paper is cited in the appendix. Below we use the two-variable case.

Every predicate $q : S_4 \to \mathbb{F}_2$ on the Boolean square $S_4 = \mathbb{F}_2^2$ is uniquely written as a Zhegalkin polynomial of degree $\le 2$:
$$q(x, c) \;=\; k \oplus a x \oplus d c \oplus e\, x c \qquad (k, a, d, e \in \mathbb{F}_2,\;\; 2^4 = \mathbf{16}\text{ functions}),$$
forming a strict filtration of embeddings:
- **all `16` Boolean tables** $\mathcal{B}_{16}$: `1` empty ($|q^{-1}(1)|=0$), `4` point indicators of atoms ($|q^{-1}(1)|=1$, $e=1$), `6` balanced `2+2` partitions ($|q^{-1}(1)|=2$, $e=0, (a,d)\neq(0,0)$), `4` co-atoms `3+1` ($|q^{-1}(1)|=3$, $e=1$), and `1` total unit ($|q^{-1}(1)|=4$);
- **`8` affine tables** $W_8 = \{e = 0\}$ (`2` constants $0, 1$ plus `6` balanced questions);
- **`7` non-zero affine vectors** $L_7 = W_8 \setminus \{0\} \cong \mathrm{PG}(2,2)$ (not to be confused with the **four-element quotient $W_8 / \{0,1\}$ by the subspace of constants** of dimension `2`, which consists of the zero class and **`3`** non-zero linear directions, each yielding `2` oppositely oriented balanced questions, `6 = 3 × 2`);
- **`6` active balanced questions** $A_6 = \{e = 0,\; (a, d) \neq (0, 0)\} = \{q_x, 1\oplus q_x,\; q_c, 1\oplus q_c,\; \beta, 1\oplus\beta\}$.

Geometrically on the three-dimensional state simplex $\Delta_3$ with four vertices $S_4$, the eight non-affine tables with $e = 1$ (four point indicators $\delta_s$ of weight `1` and their complements of weight `3`) correspond to uncalibrated isolation of a single vertex against three, whereas the six balanced questions $A_6$ correspond bijectively to the **six oriented edges of $\Delta_3$** (the six two-element slices): in the Boolean hypercube of truth tables $Q_4 = \mathbb{F}_2^{S_4}$, every balanced question $q \in A_6$ lies at odd Hamming distance $d_{\mathrm{H}}(q, \delta_s) \in \{1, 3\} \neq 0$ from every atom indicator $\delta_s$, so a single binary answer never isolates a state of $S_4$—a state is carved out by intersecting two crossing questions.

Right here let us separate **three completely different meanings of the word "NOT"** on a finite scene and distinguish them from the subset preimage $T^{-1}(B)$ (defined for *any*, possibly irreversible map $T : X \to Y$, without requiring an inverse action $T^{-1} : Y \to X$), relational converse $R^\dagger$, and conjugation $C T C^{-1}$ (admissible **only** when invertibility of $C$ has been explicitly proved):
1. **Relative complement of a subset inside a fixed regime $\Omega \subseteq S_4$:** $\neg_\Omega A = \Omega \setminus A$ (in particular, $\neg_{S_4} A = S_4 \setminus A$).
2. **Permutation (involution) of the state carrier itself** $\sigma : S_4 \to S_4$, acting on predicates by pullback $A \mapsto \sigma^{-1}(A)$ (for example, the joint flip $\sigma_\pi(x,c) = (1\oplus x, 1\oplus c)$ sends the atom $\{(0,0)\}$ to the atom $\{(1,1)\}$ of cardinality `1`, whereas the complement $\neg_{S_4}\{(0,0)\}$ has cardinality `3`).
3. **Answer-scale inversion** $\kappa(q) = 1 \oplus q$ (which for a partial question $D_q \subsetneq S_4$ flips labels only inside $D_q$ without touching $\bot_q$, and in Chapter `I.5` is further separated from additive coefficient negation $v \mapsto -v$ in $\mathbb{Z}[X]$).

> **Lemma I.2.2 (Levels `L.0` and `L.1`: balanced disjoint detector versus point detector, and divergence of contraposition under the non-emptiness proviso).**  
> *Classical foundation.* Zhegalkin algebraic normal form (1927) of Boolean functions on $\mathbb{F}_2^2$ and laws of contraposition in classical propositional calculus. *Contribution of this book:* Filtration `16 → 8 → 6` of predicates on $S_4$ (all functions / affine / balanced non-constant) and exact boundary between classical reasoning inside a fixed slice `L.1` and `6/16` failures of naive contraposition under a partial step.
> 1. **Level `L.0` (pre-Boolean selection of admissible questions on $S_4$):** if an instrument is required to split $S_4$ into two equinumerous non-empty fibers `2 + 2` ($|q^{-1}(1)| = |q^{-1}(0)| = 2$), the admissible vocabulary carves out exactly **`6`** balanced questions $D_6 = A_6$; in it, disjoint addition $A \oplus_{\mathrm{disj}} B = A \cup B$ for $A \cap B = \varnothing$ sends every antipodal pair $\{A, S_4 \setminus A\} \subset D_6$ to the full scene $S_4 \notin D_6$, and the triple of canonical questions under an arbitrary choice of the `2³ = 8` scale orientations $q'_x = q_x \oplus \varepsilon_x$, $q'_c = q_c \oplus \varepsilon_c$, $q'_\beta = \beta \oplus \varepsilon_\beta$ satisfies the exact linear coupling law $q'_x \oplus q'_c \oplus q'_\beta = \eta$ with constant $\eta = \varepsilon_x \oplus \varepsilon_c \oplus \varepsilon_\beta \in \mathbb{F}_2$ (`4` even choices yield $\eta = 0$, `4` odd choices yield $\eta = 1$). If instead the instrument is equipped with a **point detector** of individual states, a non-affine vocabulary of **`10`** non-trivial sets of cardinalities `1` and `2` ($4 + 6 = 10$) arises, closed under complementation to `4 + 6 + 4 = 14` proper non-empty events (and to the full Boolean algebra $\mathcal{B}_{16}$ upon adjoining $\varnothing$ and $S_4$).
> 2. **Level `L.1` (inside a single fixed two-element slice $\Omega \subset S_4$, $|\Omega| = 2$):** the set $\mathcal{P}(\Omega)$ of `4` subsets forms a standard Boolean algebra where a single Sheffer stroke $\mathrm{NAND}_\Omega(A, B) = \Omega \setminus (A \cap B)$ generates all **`16`** binary Boolean operations at term nesting depth $\le 3$ (the number of functions generated at composition depths $0, 1, 2, 3$ from the coordinate projections is $2, 5, 10, 16$). Meanwhile, two natural notions of entailment inside $\Omega$ behave in opposite ways with respect to contraposition:
>    - **set-theoretic inclusion** $A \le_\Omega B :\iff A \subseteq B$ imposes no non-emptiness condition and yields **`0` contraposition divergences** out of `16` pairs $(A, B) \in \mathcal{P}(\Omega)^2$: always $(A \subseteq B) \iff (\neg_\Omega B \subseteq \neg_\Omega A)$;
>    - **entailment with the existential proviso of observable realizability of the premise**
>      $$A \vdash_\Omega^{\neq\varnothing} B \;\;:\Longleftrightarrow\;\; (A \neq \varnothing) \;\land\; (A \subseteq B)$$
>      diverges from its formal contraposition $\neg_\Omega B \vdash_\Omega^{\neq\varnothing} \neg_\Omega A$ on exactly **`6` pairs out of `16`**: on the `3` pairs of the form $(\varnothing, B)$ with $B \subsetneq \Omega$ (where $A = \varnothing$ makes the direct statement false, while $\neg_\Omega B \neq \varnothing$ and $\neg_\Omega B \subseteq \Omega = \neg_\Omega \varnothing$ make the contrapositive true) and on the `3` dual pairs $(A, \Omega)$ with $\varnothing \subsetneq A$ (where the direct statement is true, while $\neg_\Omega \Omega = \varnothing$ falsifies the contrapositive).

*Proof.* All counts in item (1) follow from $\binom{4}{0}+\binom{4}{1}+\binom{4}{2}+\binom{4}{3}+\binom{4}{4} = 1+4+6+4+1 = 16$ and the identity $(q_x \oplus \varepsilon_x) \oplus (q_c \oplus \varepsilon_c) \oplus (q_x \oplus q_c \oplus \varepsilon_\beta) = \varepsilon_x \oplus \varepsilon_c \oplus \varepsilon_\beta$. In item (2), for $|\Omega| = 2$ the Boolean equivalence $(A \subseteq B) \iff (\Omega \setminus B \subseteq \Omega \setminus A)$ is universal; when the conjunct $A \neq \varnothing$ versus $\Omega \setminus B \neq \varnothing$ is added while retaining $A \subseteq B$, the two statements agree when $(A \neq \varnothing \land B \neq \Omega)$ and when $(A = \varnothing \land B = \Omega)$, and diverge if and only if either $(A = \varnothing \land B \subsetneq \Omega)$ (`3` choices of $B$ out of `4` minus `1` = `3` pairs) or $(\varnothing \subsetneq A \land B = \Omega)$ (`3` non-empty choices of $A$ out of `4` minus `1` = `3` pairs), totaling $3 + 3 = \mathbf{6}$ pairs out of `16`. Full generation of the `16` operations via $\mathrm{NAND}_\Omega$ is verified by finite closure of compositions of depth $\le 3$. $\square$

## §I.2.5. Cross-Regime Transfer `L.2`: Canonical Uniqueness of Slice Gluing, Holonomy $H_3$, and Three Target Predicates

Let us now turn to the subtlest point in the logic of observable distinction—**level `L.2`**, where the observer assembles a chain of inferences across three different local regimes (two-element slices) on one and the same square $S_4$:
- the reference-fixing regime $\Omega_1(c_0) = \{(x, c_0) \mid x \in \mathbb{F}_2\}$ ($|\Omega_1| = 2$, fiber of the question $q_c = c_0$);
- the left-input-fixing regime $\Omega_2(x_0) = \{(x_0, c) \mid c \in \mathbb{F}_2\}$ ($|\Omega_2| = 2$, fiber of the question $q_x = x_0$);
- the relative-reading-fixing regime $\Omega_3(b_0) = \{(x, c) \in S_4 \mid x \oplus c = b_0\}$ ($|\Omega_3| = 2$, fiber of the question $\beta = b_0$),
indexed by parameters $(c_0, x_0, b_0) \in \mathbb{F}_2^3$ (`8` configurations of regime triples).

Why is the transition between any two slices from different families ($\Omega_i$ and $\Omega_j$ for $i \neq j$) determined **canonically and uniquely**, without any arbitrariness on the observer's part? Two facts suffice:
1. Any two slices from different families are defined by a pair of linearly independent equations over $\mathbb{F}_2$ and therefore intersect in **exactly one common state** $p_{ij} \in S_4$ ($|\Omega_i \cap \Omega_j| = 1$):
   $$\Omega_1(c_0) \cap \Omega_2(x_0) = \{p_{12}\} = \{(x_0, c_0)\}, \qquad \Omega_1(c_0) \cap \Omega_3(b_0) = \{p_{13}\} = \{(c_0 \oplus b_0, c_0)\}, \qquad \Omega_2(x_0) \cap \Omega_3(b_0) = \{p_{23}\} = \{(x_0, x_0 \oplus b_0)\}.$$
2. Because both slices $\Omega_i$ and $\Omega_j$ are two-element sets ($\Omega_i = \{p_{ij}, q_i\}$, $\Omega_j = \{p_{ij}, q_j\}$), there exist only two bijections between them, and **the requirement that the common gluing point be fixed, $\varphi_{ij}(p_{ij}) = p_{ij}$, uniquely singles out the canonical bijection $\varphi_{ij} : \Omega_i \xrightarrow{\sim} \Omega_j$** (which maps the common point $p_{ij}$ to itself and the unique remaining point $\Omega_i \setminus \{p_{ij}\}$ to the unique remaining point $\Omega_j \setminus \{p_{ij}\}$):
$$\varphi_{12} : \Omega_1(c_0) \xrightarrow{\sim} \Omega_2(x_0),\quad \varphi_{12}(x, c_0) = (x_0,\; c_0 \oplus x_0 \oplus x) \quad \bigl(\text{unique bijection with }\varphi_{12}(p_{12}) = p_{12}\bigr),$$
$$\varphi_{23} : \Omega_2(x_0) \xrightarrow{\sim} \Omega_3(b_0),\quad \varphi_{23}(x_0, c) = (c \oplus b_0,\; c) \quad \bigl(\text{unique bijection with }\varphi_{23}(p_{23}) = p_{23}\bigr),$$
$$\varphi_{13} : \Omega_1(c_0) \xrightarrow{\sim} \Omega_3(b_0),\quad \varphi_{13}(x, c_0) = (x,\; x \oplus b_0) \quad \bigl(\text{unique bijection with }\varphi_{13}(p_{13}) = p_{13}\bigr).$$
*(Equivalently, if each slice is parametrized by the offset $\delta \in \mathbb{F}_2$ from the common point $p_{ij}$ along the single coordinate that varies on that slice, then $\varphi_{ij}$ identifies the points having the same offset $\delta \in \{0, 1\}$ from the intersection node $p_{ij}$.)*

Now traverse the closed triangle of regimes $\Omega_3 \xrightarrow{\varphi_{13}^{-1}} \Omega_1 \xrightarrow{\varphi_{12}} \Omega_2 \xrightarrow{\varphi_{23}} \Omega_3$. Because each of the three maps $\varphi_{ij}$ is uniquely determined by the intersection $\Omega_i \cap \Omega_j$, their composite—the **canonical holonomy of the regime triangle** $H_3 := \varphi_{23} \circ \varphi_{12} \circ \varphi_{13}^{-1} : \Omega_3(b_0) \to \Omega_3(b_0)$—is an intrinsic invariant of the slice triple itself: for $(x, c) \in \Omega_3(b_0)$ ($c = x \oplus b_0$), substitution gives
$$H_3(x, c) \;=\; (x \oplus \kappa,\;\; c \oplus \kappa), \qquad \text{where }\; \kappa \;:=\; c_0 \oplus x_0 \oplus b_0 \;\in\; \mathbb{F}_2.$$

When the compatibility defect of the three slices is even ($\kappa = 0$, `4` configurations out of `8`), all three pairwise gluing points coincide ($p_{12} = p_{13} = p_{23} = (x_0, c_0) \in \Omega_1 \cap \Omega_2 \cap \Omega_3$), and the holonomy is trivial: $H_3 = \mathrm{id}_{\Omega_3}$. When the defect is odd ($\kappa = 1$, the remaining `4` configurations), the triple intersection is empty ($\Omega_1 \cap \Omega_2 \cap \Omega_3 = \varnothing$), the three pairwise gluing nodes $p_{12}, p_{13}, p_{23}$ are pairwise distinct, and traversing the cycle $H_3 = \mathrm{swap}_{\Omega_3}$ **swaps the two points of the two-element slice $\Omega_3$**.

## §I.2.6. Looking Ahead: State and Question in Dual Transfer

Before consolidating the material of this chapter categorically, let us record one structural lesson that links the discrete picture of `I.2` to the linear and probabilistic-quantum models of Book II: **why a question (measurement effect) must never be confused with a state distribution**.

Let us verify this boundary in exact rational arithmetic on the two-element carrier $\{1, 2\}$ without any limits or floating-point approximations. For any rational leakage parameter $\alpha \in \mathbb{Q} \cap [0, 1]$, consider the stochastic transfer matrix
$$F_\alpha \;=\; \begin{pmatrix} 1 & \alpha \\ 0 & 1 - \alpha \end{pmatrix}.$$
Let the column vector $m = (m_1, m_2)^\top \in \mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}^2$ give the state weights ($m_1 + m_2 = 1$), and let the column vector $q = (q_1, q_2)^\top \in \mathbb{Q}^2$ ($0 \le q_1, q_2 \le 1$) give an evaluation question about the outcome with expectation pairing $\langle q, m \rangle = q_1 m_1 + q_2 m_2$. Then:
1. **Adjointness of the forward step of states and the backward step of questions:** the state is pushed forward by the rule $m' = F_\alpha m$, whereas a question about the future outcome is pulled back by the transposed operator $q' = F_\alpha^\top q$:
   $$\langle q,\, F_\alpha m \rangle \;=\; \langle F_\alpha^\top q,\, m \rangle.$$
   This identity requires **one and the same operator** $F_\alpha$ on both sides: substituting a different parameter $\beta \neq \alpha$ on one side destroys the equality already on $m = (0, 1)^\top$, $q = (1, 0)^\top$, where $\langle q, F_\alpha m \rangle = \alpha \neq \beta = \langle F_\beta^\top q, m \rangle$.
2. **Different conserved invariants for states and for questions:** the forward operator $F_\alpha$ preserves the **total sum of state weights** ($m'_1 + m'_2 = (m_1 + \alpha m_2) + (1-\alpha)m_2 = m_1 + m_2$), but does not preserve the constant vector $(1, 1)^\top$ when $\alpha > 0$ ($F_\alpha(1,1)^\top = (1+\alpha, 1-\alpha)^\top$). Conversely, the pullback operator $F_\alpha^\top = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ \alpha & 1-\alpha \end{pmatrix}$ preserves the **unit question** $F_\alpha^\top(1,1)^\top = (1,1)^\top$ and the admissibility corridor $[0, 1]^2$, but **does not preserve the sum of coordinates of an arbitrary question**:
   $$q'_1 + q'_2 \;=\; (1 + \alpha)\,q_1 + (1 - \alpha)\,q_2 \;\neq\; q_1 + q_2 \qquad (\text{when } \alpha > 0 \text{ and } q_1 \neq q_2).$$
   A question differs from a state distribution: it lives in the dual space and is pulled back against the step.

## §I.2.7. Categorical Consolidation of the Chapter

### Block K-I.2.1. The Categories of Partial Maps $\mathbf{Par}$ and Binary Relations $\mathbf{Rel}$

- **Category:** the category of partial maps $\mathbf{Par}$ (equivalent to the category of pointed sets $\mathbf{Set}_*$ under standard adjoining of a failure point) and its ambient dagger category of binary relations $\mathbf{Rel} \simeq \mathbf{Kl}(\mathcal{P})$.
- **Objects:** arbitrary state sets $X, Y, Z$ (in the finite core, objects of $\mathbf{FinPar}$ and $\mathbf{FinRel}$).
- **Morphisms:** in $\mathbf{Par}$, partial maps $(D_T, T_0 : D_T \to Y)$ with composition domain $D_{U\circ T} = T_0^{-1}(D_U)$; in $\mathbf{Rel}$, subsets $R \subseteq X \times Y$ with relational composition $S \circ R = \{(x,z) \mid \exists y,\; (x,y)\in R \land (y,z)\in S\}$ and converse involution $R^\dagger = \{(y,x) \mid (x,y) \in R\}$.
- **Construction / universal property:** the faithful functorial graph embedding $\Gamma : \mathbf{Par} \hookrightarrow \mathbf{Rel}$, $T \mapsto \{(x, T(x)) \mid x \in D_T\}$, characterizing morphisms of $\mathbf{Par}$ inside $\mathbf{Rel}$ by the single-valuedness inequality $\Gamma(T) \circ \Gamma(T)^\dagger \subseteq \Delta_Y$, together with the Galois connection $\exists_R \dashv \forall_R$ between the complete Boolean lattices $\mathcal{P}(X)$ and $\mathcal{P}(Y)$ for each fixed relation $R$.
- **What is preserved:** associativity taking into account admissibility domains $D_{U\circ T}$, contravariant pullback of partial questions $(U\circ T)^* q = T^*(U^* q)$, union preservation by $\exists_R$, and intersection preservation by $\forall_R$.
- **Where it breaks / what the construction does NOT claim:** (a) when $D_T \subsetneq X$, the pullback $T^*$ does NOT preserve the global unit $Y \mapsto D_T \neq X$ or complementation in all of $X$ ($T^*(Y \setminus B) = D_T \setminus T^*(B)$, Theorem `I.2.1(2)`); (b) the converse $R^\dagger$ need NOT be an inverse: the single equality $R^\dagger \circ R = \Delta_X$ does not rule out multi-valuedness ($R=\{(x,0),(x,1)\}$), two-sided invertibility in $\mathbf{Rel}$ requires both $R^\dagger \circ R = \Delta_X$ and $R \circ R^\dagger = \Delta_Y$ (the graph of a bijection), and $\exists_{R^\dagger}$ does NOT preserve $\cap$ under branching (`Theorem I.2.2(4)`).

### Block K-I.2.2. Lawvere's Boolean Hyperdoctrine $\mathcal{P} : \mathbf{Set}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathbf{BoolAlg}$

- **Category:** the indexed category (Boolean hyperdoctrine) of fiberwise predicate algebras $\mathcal{P}(-) : \mathbf{Set}^{\mathrm{op}} \to \mathbf{BoolAlg}$.
- **Objects:** over each set $E$ hangs the complete atomic Boolean algebra of subsets $\mathcal{P}(E)$.
- **Morphisms:** to every total window $\pi : E \to B$ is assigned the inverse-image Boolean algebra homomorphism $\pi^* = \pi^{-1} : \mathcal{P}(B) \to \mathcal{P}(E)$ together with its left and right monotone adjoints $\exists_\pi \dashv \pi^* \dashv \forall_\pi$.
- **Construction / universal property:** the shadow boundary operator $\partial_\pi(S) = \exists_\pi(S) \setminus \forall_\pi(S) \subseteq B$, measuring the obstruction to descending a predicate $S \subseteq E$ along the window $\pi$, and the Beck–Chevalley base-change law $g^* \circ \exists_f = \exists_{f'} \circ g'^*$ for pullback squares.
- **What is preserved:** for surjective $\pi$, the equality $\partial_\pi(S) = \varnothing$ characterizes the subalgebra of descendable predicates $\mathrm{im}(\pi^*) \cong \mathcal{P}(B)$, and pullback squares preserve commutation of quantification with context change.
- **Where it breaks / what the construction does NOT claim:** (a) the quantifiers $\exists_\pi$ and $\forall_\pi$ themselves are NOT Boolean algebra homomorphisms ($\exists_\pi$ fails to preserve $\cap$, $\forall_\pi$ fails to preserve $\cup$); (b) without the pullback condition on the square, Beck–Chevalley equality is NOT guaranteed: the exhibited square with $A' = \varnothing$ provides an explicit counterexample (`Theorem I.2.3(3)`, while there also exist non-Cartesian squares where equality happens to hold).

### Historical-Philosophical Note: Holding a Declared Locus (*dhāraṇā*, `YS 3.1`)

1. **Motif from the classical source.** In Patañjali's sūtra `YS 3.1` (*deśa-bandhaś cittasya dhāraṇā*), the initial limb of collected contemplation is defined as **binding directed attention (*citta*) to a single chosen locus (*deśa-bandha*)**. In contemplative practice, a failure of *dhāraṇā* occurs not because the rules of inference inside a single focus break down, but because attention imperceptibly slips from one reference locus to another, carrying an old evaluation into a foreign regime without recalculating the shift.
2. **Strict realization in TNR.** In the present chapter this motif helps visualize the difference between classical Boolean logic *inside a single fixed slice* $\Omega$ (`L.1`, where ordinary inclusion laws hold) and *cross-regime transport* across three distinct fixations $\Omega_1(c_0), \Omega_2(x_0), \Omega_3(b_0)$ (`L.2`), where the forgotten holonomy of the regime triangle $H_3$ with odd defect $\kappa = x_0 \oplus c_0 \oplus b_0 = 1$ swaps the two points of the target slice.
