## 1. Purpose and Architecture of Volume Two

Volume Two of the monograph continues the development of Volume One ("Book I. The Observer, Distinction, and the Finite Core") and answers the question posed in the concluding `Table I.9.3` of Book I: **what happens to the observer's operational contract $\Gamma$ when we successively lift the four finite set-theoretic restrictions of Volume One?**

The four enrichments of Book II form a unified structural ladder, at each rung of which the backbone constructions of Book I return—the four-point scene $S_4 = \mathbb{F}_2^2$, the checkerboard octahedron of six slices $O_6$, the torsor presheaf $\mathcal{S}_w$, and the triple parity charge $\kappa = x_0 \oplus c_0 \oplus b_0$:

1. **Enrichment I (Chapters `II.1–II.2`): Linear Memory and Continuous Continuation.**
   - In `Chapter II.1`, the finite Moore–Nerode memory automaton from `Chapters I.3` and `I.8` is endowed with a linear structure over a field $K$: the observer's hidden memory appears as a minimal linear realization $(A, B, C)$ by the Ho–Kalman theorem, whose dimension is exactly equal to the rank of the infinite block Hankel matrix $\mathcal{H}_f$.
   - In `Chapter II.2`, the discrete observation step is connected to continuous time and smooth jets without a metaphysical "leap into the continuum": the dyadic map $\mathbb{Z}_2 \to \mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}$ exhibits the first topological gluing of indistinguishable binary tails ($0.0111\ldots_2 = 0.1000\ldots_2$) while failing to be a group homomorphism owing to opposite carry directions, the Cayley transform maps an infinitesimal generator $J_a$ into a reversible step $(I + \frac{h}{2}J_a)(I - \frac{h}{2}J_a)^{-1}$ with quadratic accuracy $O(h^3)$, and the ring of dual numbers $K[\varepsilon]/(\varepsilon^2)$ algebraizes the first-order derivative.
2. **Enrichment II (Chapters `II.3–II.4`): Simplicial Gluing, Homology, Coverings, and Connection.**
   - In `Chapter II.3`, sign and orientation from `Chapter I.5` are joined with the nerve of a covering from `Chapter I.9` via the chain boundary operator $\partial$ and coboundary $\delta$: the identity $\partial_1 \partial_2 = 0$ expresses exact sign cancellation along a twice-traversed seam. Two types of obstructions to global assembly are separated—violation of the sheaf axiom (the parity presheaf $\mathcal{G}$) and a non-trivial cohomology class $[w] \ne 0$ in $H^1(\mathcal{N}; \mathbb{F}_2) \cong \mathbb{F}_2^3$ of the genuine torsor sheaf $\mathcal{S}_w$ on the checkerboard octahedron $O_6$.
   - In `Chapter II.4`, a one-dimensional cycle is endowed with a monodromy group, a discrete connection on a graph, and the structure of a principal $G$-torsor. The central geometric bridge between Books I and II is proved: the Weyl group $W(B_3) \cong \mathfrak{S}_4 \times C_2$ of order $48$ is the full automorphism group $\mathrm{Aut}(O_6)$ of the checkerboard octahedron of six slices, where the tetrahedral subgroup $W(D_3) = T_d \cong \mathfrak{S}_4$ preserves the charge $\kappa$ and permutes the four filled faces $T_+$ (the four points of the scene $S_4$), the subgroup of proper rotations $W(B_3)^+ \cong \mathfrak{S}_4$ permutes the four main diagonals of the cube (reversing $\kappa$ on its $12$ odd rotations), and the central inversion $C_2 = \{\pm I_3\}$ swaps filled faces and hollow holes, reversing the parity charge $\kappa \mapsto \kappa \oplus 1$.
3. **Enrichment III (Chapters `II.5–II.7`): The Nonassociative Fano Step, Quantum Effects, and Contextuality.**
   - In `Chapter II.5`, the seven non-zero vectors of the three-bit charge space $\mathbb{F}_2^3 \setminus \{0\}$ form the projective Fano plane $\mathrm{PG}(2, \mathbb{F}_2)$. It is proved that the unsigned multiplication table along the Fano lines is commutative and associative ($\cong \mathbb{F}_2[\mathbb{F}_2^3]$), meaning that a sign-blind observer cannot distinguish the orientation of lines; a chosen signed $2$-cochain defines the nonassociative alternative algebra of octonions. Separately, in the split algebra over $\mathbb{F}_2$, the simple Moufang loop—the Paige loop of order $120$ (with trace split $64 + 56 = 120$ and $q_0$-split $36 + 84 = 120$)—is examined, while the next doubling step ($8 \to 16$, sedenions) destroys alternativity and produces $84$ elementary zero-divisor quartets.
   - In `Chapter II.6`, the observer's operational contract $\Gamma$ is transferred into a complex Hilbert space $\mathcal{H} = \mathbb{C}^d$: the Walsh–Bloch transform maps the $16$ Boolean tables of the four-point scene $S_4$ into Hermitian qubit operators with a three-level positivity threshold ($16 \to 8 \to 2$ for $|\lambda| \le 1/\sqrt{3}$, $1/\sqrt{3} < |\lambda| \le 1$, $|\lambda| > 1$), and the branch closure $W_\infty$ yields the quantum analogue of the Nerode theorem.
   - In `Chapter II.7`, the four single-point questions of the scene $S_4$ at the threshold $\lambda = 1/\sqrt{3}$ form the regular `SIC-POVM` tetrahedron of a qubit; the Hesse ($d=3$) and Hoggar ($d=8$, $64$ rays with frame matrix $G_{\mathrm{frame}}^2 = 8 G_{\mathrm{frame}}$ of rank $8$, integer matrix $S^2 = 63 I_{64} + 18 S$ over $\mathbb{Q}(i)$, and positive frame potential gap $\Phi_3 - \Phi_3^{\mathrm{Welch}} = \frac{7}{810} > 0$) packings are investigated, and the odd cohomological obstruction of the Peres–Mermin square is compared with the charge $\kappa$ of Book I via one-dimensional cokernels over $\mathbb{F}_2$ (without identifying the classes $[\beta_{\mathrm{PM}}]$ and $[\kappa]$ themselves in the absence of explicit chain maps between the complexes).
4. **Enrichment IV (Chapters `II.8–II.9`): Cellular Limits, Rational Certificates, and Lawvere's Diagonal Limit.**
   - In `Chapter II.8`, five levels of transition from a finite cellular mesh to a continuous variational limit are strictly separated (Whitney forms, the semi-discrete Cahn–Hilliard gradient flow on a graph, and exact rational `SOS` non-negativity certificates on the $21$-dimensional flag space of the Fano plane with invariant decomposition $84 + 1687 = 1771$).
   - In `Chapter II.9`, Lawvere's diagonal fixed-point theorem in Cartesian closed categories is proved as a strict internal limit of complete self-description of the observer, the nine canonical regimes of minimal memory across Books I–II are synthesized, and the boundary conditions for transitioning to the domain bridges of Book III are formulated.
