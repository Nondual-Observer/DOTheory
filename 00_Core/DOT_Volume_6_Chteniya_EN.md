# Distinction Observable Theory

# Volume 6. External readings as projections

The subject of this volume is the external readings of the scene, assembled into an atlas. The corpus has accumulated separate bridges: logical reading, color, spectral, semantic, arithmetic. Here a rigorous notion of reading as projection is introduced, the readings are assembled into a system with transition maps between them, and the functorial reading is given as a projection of the vertical.

The exposition completes the corpus. The status of each reading is marked: where it is worked out rigorously, where there is an applied projection onto a concrete domain. External domains are not generated by the theory — they are the domains into which the scene is projected.

---

# Section 0. Starting point

A rank-$n$ scene is a finite structure: carrier $U_n$, complement $\kappa$, three directions, relations $R_d$, boundary $\partial$, rotation $T$, observer-center. This structure admits readings in external domains — logic, color, spectrum, the language of meanings, arithmetic. In the corpus the readings existed separately. Their organization into an atlas with transition maps is the subject of the present volume.

---

# Section 1. What a reading is

## §1.1. Reading as projection

A **reading** of the scene into a domain $D$ is a structure-preserving map

$$
\rho : \text{scene} \longrightarrow D,
$$

carrying the invariants of the scene into the invariants of the domain. A reading is a projection: it realizes the structure of the scene in $D$, adding nothing to the scene and not generating $D$ from it. The domain $D$ has its own nature; the reading merely lays into it the already-built finite structure.

## §1.2. Criterion for a reading

A map $\rho$ is a reading if it carries the load-bearing invariants of the scene into their counterparts in $D$:

$$
\boxed{
\rho(\kappa) = \text{involution of } D, \quad \rho(\text{directions}) = \text{axes of } D, \quad \rho(\partial) = \text{boundary of } D, \quad \rho(T) = \text{rotation of } D.
}
$$

A reading that does not preserve these invariants is not a projection of the scene but an extraneous structure. The criterion fixes what counts as a reading, and it fixes the condition for agreement of two readings (Section 3): the transition between them must commute with the invariants.

## §1.3. Realizations and projections

There are two kinds of reading. A realization is an isomorphism: the scene is laid into the domain without loss, and there is an inverse map recovering the state exactly. A lossy projection carries several states into one: recovery yields a fiber, not a point. The logical reading (Boolean algebra), the color reading (the cube $[0,1]^3$) and the arithmetic reading (the divisor lattice of a squarefree number) are realizations — isomorphic copies of the carrier. The spectral and semantic readings are lossy projections. The exact criterion for the kind of reading is the recovery discipline (Volume 9): a realization carries an inverse map, a projection carries a fiber.

## §1.4. The observer in a reading

The observer-center passes to the neutral point of the domain — the point invariant under the involution of $D$ and equidistant from the poles of the reading. The center $c = \tfrac12(0^n + 1^n)$ is read as the neutral element: in logic, the undefined value; in color, the gray center of the cube. The observer is that which every reading carries into its neutral point.

# Section 2. Readings of the scene

## §2.1. Logical reading

In the logical reading, states are the values of binary features, the carrier $Q_n$ is the Boolean algebra $\mathcal P(J)$, the complement $\kappa$ is negation $\operatorname{NOT}$. The operator tower (repeated application of the power-set functor on the carrier) yields the Boolean operations; the three directions are the three truth coordinates. The observer-center is read as the undefined value — the point between true and false, fixed under negation.

Status: a worked-out reading. The complement as $\operatorname{NOT}$ and the carrier as Boolean algebra are a rigorous realization; functional completeness of the Boolean operations requires the operator tower.

## §2.2. Color reading

In the color reading, the three directions are the three color channels, the carrier is realized in the color cube $[0,1]^3$. The six points of the active scene are the six vertices of the color layer: three channels $RGB$ and three complements $CMY$. The complement $\kappa$, that is $x \mapsto 1^n - x$, carries $RGB$ into $CMY$. The octahedron $R_1 \cup R_2$ is the six-color layer; the three complement pairs are the three pairs of complementary colors. The observer-center $(\tfrac12, \tfrac12, \tfrac12)$ is the gray center of the cube — the achromatic point, equidistant from all colors, fixed under color complementation.

## §2.3. Spectral reading

In the spectral reading, the relations $R_d$ are the classes of an association scheme — the Hamming scheme — and the scene is read through the spectrum of its graphs. The cycle $R_1 = C_6$ has a known spectrum; the union of relations gives a scheme with eigenvalues. The observer corresponds to the trivial eigen-direction — the all-ones vector, invariant under all relations.

Status: rigorous as algebraic combinatorics (the Hamming scheme and graph spectra are defined exactly); a projection of the scene onto its spectral invariants. The cryptographic application of the spectral block is an applied outcome of this reading, with the status of an application.

## §2.4. Semantic reading

In the semantic reading, the rank-3 scene is read as a structure of meanings: axis and polarity, the mixed admissibility of the layers $S_1, S_2$. The directions are the axes of meaning, the complement is the change of polarity, the layers are levels of admissibility.

Status: an applied projection. The semantic reading lays the structure of the scene into a language of meanings; it does not derive semantics from the theory and does not assert that meaning is generated by the scene. A projection onto a concrete domain with applied status.

## §2.5. Arithmetic reading

In the arithmetic reading, the divisor lattice serves as carrier. The divisor branch (AMR-DC) is a rigorous realization: for a squarefree number, $D(p_1\cdots p_n) \cong Q_n$ verbatim, the number 30 yields the rank-3 scene with its four relations, the complement being $d \mapsto N/d$. This is not a preliminary projection but an isomorphism of carriers (Volume 8). The scale-residue branch (AMR-SR) gives only partial typed bridges to the relations of the scene and remains preliminary.

The arithmetic of compositeness agrees with the break: the first composite number $4 = 2\times 2$ answers to the birth of the interior (Volume 2), while multiplicities give the second axis of growth (Volume 8). The divisor realization and its multiplicative-recovery-residue structure are unfolded in Volumes 8 and 9; here the arithmetic reading is indicated as one of the realizations of the scene.

# Section 3. Transition maps

## §3.1. The common invariant of readings

All readings are projections of one scene and therefore carry one and the same invariant skeleton: the complement $\kappa$, the three directions, the observer-center, the relations $R_d$. The transition between two readings is a map of their domains commuting with this skeleton. The atlas of readings is glued by these transitions.

## §3.2. Logical and color

The transition between the logical and color readings identifies their invariants. Negation $\operatorname{NOT}$ of logic and the color complement $RGB \mapsto CMY$ are one complement $\kappa$. The undefined value of logic and the gray center of color are one observer-center. The three truth coordinates and the three color channels are the three directions. The transition commutes with $\kappa$, because both readings project one complement.

$$
\boxed{
\operatorname{NOT} \;\leftrightarrow\; (RGB \mapsto CMY), \qquad \text{undefined} \;\leftrightarrow\; \text{gray}, \qquad \text{both are } \kappa \text{ and center}.
}
$$

## §3.3. Discrete and continuous

The transition between the discrete and continuous readings is a geometric realization: the simplicial scene $\mathcal F(\partial\Delta^{n-1})$ is laid into a continuous body (the color cube, the torus of rotation). The discrete states are the vertices, the continuous body their geometric shell. The transition commutes with the boundary $\partial$: the simplicial boundary passes to the boundary of the continuous body.

## §3.4. Cycle and analytics

The transition between the cyclic and analytic readings identifies the rotation $T$ with continuous rotation — the flow on the torus with rotation number $\rho$ (Volume 4). The discrete cycle $C_6$ is a section of the continuous winding; its smoothing is an analytic avatar. The transition commutes with $T$: the discrete rotation is the return map of the continuous one.

## §3.5. The atlas of readings

The readings form an atlas: each is a projection of the scene into its domain, and the transitions between them commute with the load-bearing invariants. The atlas is consistent because all projections issue from one scene, and the transition from any reading to any other passes through the common invariant skeleton. No reading is primary; the scene is primary, the readings are its projections.

# Section 4. Functorial reading: projection of the vertical

The readings of the preceding sections project the scene of a fixed rank. The functorial reading projects the movement between ranks.

In the functorial reading (Volume 3) the ranks are objects, the lift is a functor between them, the growth vector $Q_n^* \cong U_{n+1}/\kappa$ is a natural correspondence, the adjoint pair $\Lambda \dashv \pi$ is growth and reduction. This is a projection of the scene onto its categorical structure; it expresses the vertical — that which the logical, color, spectral, semantic and arithmetic readings, projecting a single rank, do not express.

$$
\boxed{
\text{the functorial reading is a projection of the vertical — the movement between ranks.}
}
$$

The readings are distributed by what they project: logical, color, spectral, semantic, arithmetic — the horizontal, the structure of one rank in different domains; the functorial — the vertical, the movement of the ladder; the cyclic-analytic (Volume 4) — the screw, folding together the horizontal and the vertical.

# Section 5. Consistency of the atlas

## §5.1. One scene, many projections

The readings are projections of one scene, not different theories. The scene — the finite structure $U_n$ with complement, directions, relations, boundary, rotation and observer — is primary; each reading realizes it in its domain. The difference of domains is not a difference of theories: logic, color, spectrum, meaning, arithmetic are five domains of one projection.

## §5.2. What is invariant across all readings

The load-bearing skeleton of the scene is visible in every projection: the complement $\kappa$ (negation, color complement, passage to the complementary), the three directions (truth coordinates, color channels, axes of meaning), the observer-center (undefined, gray, trivial eigen-direction), the relations $R_d$, the screw movement. These invariants do not depend on the reading — they are the proper structure of the scene, showing through in every domain.

## §5.3. What depends on the reading

What depends on the reading is the filling of the domain: colors, truth values, divisors, spectral numbers are the content of a concrete projection. This filling is not the structure of the scene — it is that with which the domain answers to the projection. The scene fixes the skeleton; the domain gives the filling; the reading is their joining.

# §6. Summary of Volume 6 and of the corpus

A reading is a structure-preserving projection of the scene into an external domain, carrying the invariants of the scene — complement, directions, boundary, rotation, observer — into the invariants of the domain. The external domain is not generated by the theory; the reading lays the finite structure into it.

The scene admits readings: logical (complement as $\operatorname{NOT}$, carrier as Boolean algebra — worked out), color (directions as channels, center as gray, $RGB \mapsto CMY$ as complement — an exact realization), spectral (relations as the Hamming scheme, spectra — rigorous as combinatorics), semantic (axes and polarity — applied), arithmetic (divisor lattice — a rigorous realization for the divisor branch, Volumes 8–9; the scale-residue branch is preliminary).

The readings form an atlas, glued by transition maps: $\operatorname{NOT}$ and the color complement are one $\kappa$, undefined and gray are one center, discrete and continuous are linked by geometric realization, cycle and analytics by the return map. The transitions commute with the load-bearing skeleton; the atlas is consistent because all projections issue from one scene.

The functorial reading is a projection of the vertical — the movement between ranks, which the readings of a single rank do not express.

Across all readings the skeleton of the scene is invariant — complement, three directions, observer, relations, screw; only the filling of the domain depends on the reading. The scene is primary; the readings are its projections.

$$
\boxed{
\text{one scene, many projections; the load-bearing skeleton is invariant, the filling of the domain depends on the reading.}
}
$$

The readings complete the projections of the scene: from the absolute invariant through the carrier, threeness, the break, the vertical, the screw and the observer — to projections of one scene into external domains. There remains to turn the gaze: to see that the scene itself unfolds from the structure of the observer. This is the subject of the closing volume.
